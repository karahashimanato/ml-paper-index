## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Setting: GCN layers are formulated as f_l(X) = sigma(P X W_l) with ReLU sigma and P = I - Delta~, where Delta~ is the augmented normalized Laplacian (citing Wu et al., 2019a) built from A~ = A + I and D~ = D + I; following Kipf & Welling one-layer MLPs are used, but the authors state the result holds for the multi-layer case. The GCN is considered without readout operations and the interest is the output as the number of layers L -> infinity.([Section 4, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.10947v5#page=5 "GCNs iteratively update signals on V using the connection information and weights."))
- **c2** Theorem 1 and its assumptions: for a symmetric P and an M-dimensional subspace U with (Assumption 1) an orthonormal basis of non-negative vectors and (Assumption 2) invariance under P, d_M(f_l(X)) <= s_l lambda d_M(X), where s_l is the product of maximum singular values of the layer's weights and lambda is the operator norm of P restricted to the orthogonal complement of U; the proof hinges on ReLU not increasing the distance to M, using the non-negativity of the basis vectors.([Section 3.3, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.10947v5#page=4 "We use the non-negativity of em to prove this claim."))
- **c3** Theorem 2 (GCN case): d_M(X^(l)) <= (s lambda)^l d_M(X^(0)), so it converges exponentially to 0 when s lambda < 1; within M, nodes in the same connected component with identical degrees have identical representations, so the authors interpret this as exponential information loss with depth; even with s >= 1 the loss can occur when s < lambda^-1, and the rate depends on the spectrum of the augmented normalized Laplacian.([Section 4, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.10947v5#page=5 "In this sense, M only has information about connected components and node degrees and we can interpret this theorem as the exponential information loss of GCNs in terms of the layer size."))
- **c4** The non-negativity assumption matters: in a 2-node synthetic one-step-transition experiment, choosing an eigenvector whose entries have different signs (violating Assumption 1) makes M not invariant and the transition does not uniformly decrease the distance to M.([Section 6.1, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.10947v5#page=7 "Therefore, we cannot remove the non-negativity assumption from Theorem 1."))
- **c5** Theorem 3 (Erdős-Rényi graphs G_{N,p} with log N/(Np) = o(1)): for any eps > 0, if the supremum s of maximum singular values is below a threshold s0 that grows with N and p, then for sufficiently large N the GCN satisfies the condition of Theorem 2 with probability at least 1 - eps; the authors stress the graph must not be extremely sparse, and conclude that for sufficiently large and not extremely sparse graphs most GCNs suffer the information loss.([Section 5, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.10947v5#page=6 "Theorem 3 requires that an underlying graph is not extremely sparse."))
- **c6** Applicability limit: the authors acknowledge that real-world graphs are often not dense, so Theorem 2 applies to very limited GCNs; citing Coja-Oghlan (2007), with bounded expected average degree the smallest positive normalized-Laplacian eigenvalue of G_{N,p} is o(1) with high probability, and the behavior on sparse graphs is left for future research.([Section 7, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.10947v5#page=8 "However, real-world graphs are not often dense, which means that Theorem 2 is applicable to very limited GCNs."))
- **c7** Real-data experiment design: the citation networks are too sparse for the theory to say anything non-trivial (for Cora only when 1 <= s < lambda^-1 ~ 1 + 3.62e-3), so the authors make 'noisy' versions by randomly adding edges, raising lambda^-1 to 1.11; they report that GCNs with s outside the region s < lambda^-1 outperform those inside it in almost all configurations, and conclude the results are consistent with the theorems.([Section 6.3, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.10947v5#page=8 "To circumvent this, we make noisy versions of citation networks by randomly adding edges to graphs."))
- **c8** Scope limitations: the analysis covers only ReLU activations (it uses ReLU as a projection onto the non-negative cone), so extending to sigmoid or Leaky ReLU is 'far from trivial'; it considers update operations only, not readout.([Section 7, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.10947v5#page=9 "In particular, we cannot directly apply our theory to graph classiﬁcation tasks in which each sample is a graph."))
- **c9** Residual connections: by the Markov-process analogy (residual links correspond to a lazy process) the authors imagine residual links might not help against over-smoothing, but citing Li et al. (2019), who reported graph NNs with as many as 56 layers performing well with residual connections, they say the situation could be more complicated than their intuitions and leave it for future research.([Section 7, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.10947v5#page=9 "However, Li et al. (2019) reported that graph NNs with as many as 56 layers performed well if they added residual connections."))
