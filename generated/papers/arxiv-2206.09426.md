## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Datasets: besides more than 40 existing benchmark datasets (kept only if the anomaly ratio is below 40%), 10 CV and NLP datasets are added as embeddings from pretrained models (BERT [CLS] token for NLP, ImageNet-pretrained ResNet18 after the last average pooling for CV). For these, one class is set as normal, the remaining classes are downsampled to 5% of the total instances as anomalies, and results are averaged over all classes, following previous works.([Section 3.2, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.09426v2#page=4 "Following previous works [151, 152], we set one of the multi-classes as normal, downsample the remaining classes to 5% of the total instances as anomalies, and report the average results over all the respective classes."))
- **c2** Protocol: although unsupervised detectors are primarily designed for the transductive setting, all algorithms are adapted to the inductive setting (as is common in PyOD, TODS and PyGOD): 70% of the data for training and 30% for testing with stratified sampling to keep the anomaly ratio, each experiment repeated 3 times and averaged.([Section 4.1, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.09426v2#page=6 "We repeat each experiment 3 times and report the average."))
- **c3** Hyperparameters: all algorithms use their default hyperparameter settings from the original paper (specific values in Appendix B.1 and the codebase). The authors acknowledge that a small hold-out set could be used to tune semi- and fully-supervised methods, but this setting is not considered.([Appendix C (Details on Experiment Setting), p.28](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.09426v2#page=28 "It is also acknowledged that it is possible to use a small hold-out data for hyperparameter tuning for semi- and fully-supervised methods [164], while we do not consider this setting in this work."))
- **c4** Unsupervised setting (57 datasets, AUCROC): none of the 14 unsupervised methods is statistically better than the others in the critical difference diagram, where most algorithms are connected without statistical significance.([Section 4.2, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.09426v2#page=7 "None of the unsupervised methods is statistically better than the others, as shown in the critical difference diagram of Fig. 4a"))
- **c5** Some deep unsupervised methods such as DeepSVDD and DAGMM are surprisingly worse than shallow methods; the authors attribute this to deep unsupervised methods being harder to train (more hyperparameters) and harder to tune without label guidance.([Section 4.2, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.09426v2#page=7 "We also note that some DL-based unsupervised methods like DeepSVDD and DAGMM are surprisingly worse than shallow methods."))
- **c6** Few labels: for most semi-supervised methods, 1% labeled anomalies suffice to surpass the best unsupervised method, while most supervised methods need 10%; with few labels (5% or less) semi-supervised methods are generally better than fully-supervised ones, and with many labeled anomalies both are comparable.([Section 4.2, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.09426v2#page=7 "For most semi-supervised methods, merely 1% labeled anomalies are sufﬁcient to surpass the best unsupervised method (shown as the dashed line in Fig. 4b), while most supervised methods need 10% labeled anomalies to achieve so."))
- **c7** Anomaly types (synthetic anomalies injected into benchmark datasets): unsupervised performance depends on how well the method's assumptions match the anomaly type; LOF is statistically best for local anomalies and KNN for global anomalies, and no algorithm performs well on all types (LOF does poorly on clustered anomalies). The authors advise selecting algorithms by the characteristics of the task.([Section 4.3, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.09426v2#page=8 "Performance of unsupervised algorithms highly depends on the alignment of its assumptions and the underlying anomaly type."))
- **c8** For local, global and dependency anomalies, most label-informed (semi- and fully-supervised) methods perform worse than the best unsupervised method of each type; only for clustered anomalies do label-informed (especially semi-supervised) methods beat the best unsupervised method. The authors believe partial labels cannot capture all characteristics of a given anomaly type, so knowing the anomaly type may outweigh partial labels.([Section 4.3, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.09426v2#page=8 "The “power” of prior knowledge on anomaly types may overweigh the usage of partial labels."))
- **c9** Conflict of interest stated in the text: most unsupervised algorithms are taken from PyOD, which the authors call their early work; the semi-supervised methods are implemented by the authors and released with ADBench.([Section 3.2, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.09426v2#page=4 "Most unsupervised algorithms are readily available in our early work Python Outlier Detection (PyOD) [198], and some supervised methods are available in scikit-learn [133] and corresponding libraries."))
