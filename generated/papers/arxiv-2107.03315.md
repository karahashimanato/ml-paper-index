## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Proposed signal: DoC is defined as the base set's average confidence minus the target set's average confidence (over classes shared by both); it is used as a feature for a regressor of the accuracy gap or directly as an accuracy estimate, motivated by average confidence (AC) being an overly optimistic accuracy predictor for miscalibrated models.([Predicting performance with difference of confidences, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03315v2#page=4 "We propose difference of conﬁdences DoC as a way to quantify distribution shifts:"))
- **c2** Negative finding: common distributional distances such as Frechet distance and MMD fail to give reliable performance estimates under distribution shift; in Results, when calibrated on synthetic shifts, A-proxy and discriminator AUC did not significantly improve on the naive base-accuracy baseline for natural shifts.([Abstract, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03315v2#page=1 "Our investigation determines that common distributional distances such as Frechet distance or Maximum Mean Discrepancy, fail to induce reliable estimates of performance under distribution shift."))
- **c3** Evaluation protocol: ImageNet is the base distribution; regressors are trained on labeled 'calibration' groupings of shifts and evaluated on held-out 'validation' groupings, with similar shifts placed in the same group to avoid leakage; the main setting calibrates on synthetic shifts (ImageNet-C) and predicts on natural shifts (ImageNet-V2, ImageNet-Vid-Robust, ImageNet-Rendition, ImageNet-Sketch), averaged over many pretrained architectures; metric is MAE of predicted accuracy.([Experiments, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03315v2#page=5 "Our work’s chief concern is the ability to predict natural distribution shifts, and as such focuses on settings with synthetic calibration and natural validation datasets."))
- **c4** Failure mode (synthetic shifts): although DoC improved on the AC baseline for every natural shift, it decreased prediction accuracy on 2 of 8 synthetic shifts (Defocus Blur and Gaussian Blur).([Results, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03315v2#page=7 "While DoC improves upon the AC baseline for each form of natural distribution shift, we note that in 2 of the 8 synthetic shifts explored (Defocus Blur and Gaussian Blur) DoC actually decreases the accuracy of the predictions."))
- **c5** Failure mode (training interventions): the improvement from DoC declines steeply for models trained with synthetic corruptions (AugMix, AugMix-DeepAugment), and DoC did not improve the AugMix-DeepAugment model; the authors think the distances are likely off because AugMix corruptions were not replicated between base and calibration sets.([Results, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03315v2#page=6 "Furthermore, we observe a steep decline in the magnitude of improvement offered from our DoC approach as it is evaluated over models which incorporate synthetic corruptions into the training process (Augmix, AM-DeepAugment)."))
- **c6** Failure mode (adversarially collected shift): ImageNet-A produced the highest accuracy-prediction error of all shifts studied for every approach; when calibrated on synthetic shifts, DoC was the only approach to noticeably improve on AC there; calibrating on natural shifts instead improved all approaches, but only DoE and DoC then beat AC (the others stayed significantly worse than AC), which the authors take as showing the importance of calibrating and evaluating on natural shifts and as highlighting limitations of these approaches.([Results, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03315v2#page=7 "In Figure 7, we observe that this distribution shift produces the highest predicted accuracy error of shifts we have studied across all approaches."))
- **c7** Stated limitation: the authors call their results a promising step for detecting performance drop under shift but say the problem is far from solved; they also echo Ovadia et al. that many methods designed for uncertainty prediction seem to fall short of simpler baselines. No impossibility or identifiability result is stated in the text.([Conclusion, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03315v2#page=8 "While our results present a promising step forward for detecting performance drop under distribution shift, we note that the problem is far from solved."))
