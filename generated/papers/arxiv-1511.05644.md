## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Definition: the AAE is an autoencoder regularized by matching the aggregated posterior q(z) (the encoding distribution averaged over the data) to an arbitrary prior p(z), using an adversarial network attached to the latent code; the encoder plays the role of the GAN generator.([Adversarial Autoencoders, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.05644v2#page=2 "The adversarial autoencoder is an autoencoder that is regularized by matching the aggregated posterior, q(z), to an arbitrary prior, p(z)."))
- **c2** Training: the adversarial network and the autoencoder are trained jointly with SGD in two phases on each mini-batch, a reconstruction phase (encoder and decoder minimize reconstruction error) and a regularization phase (the discriminator is updated, then the encoder is updated to confuse it).([Adversarial Autoencoders, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.05644v2#page=2 "Both, the adversarial network and the autoencoder are trained jointly with SGD in two phases – the reconstruction phase and the regularization phase – executed on each mini-batch."))
- **c3** Encoder choice: deterministic, Gaussian-posterior and universal-approximator encoders were considered; the authors argue the stochastic ones could smooth q(z), but after an extensive hyperparameter search they obtained similar test likelihood with each, and report only the deterministic encoder in the rest of the paper.([Adversarial Autoencoders, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.05644v2#page=3 "Nevertheless, after extensive hyper-parameter search, we obtained similar test-likelihood with each type of q(z/x)."))
- **c4** Relation to the VAE regularizer: the VAE bound is rewritten as reconstruction minus the entropy of q(z|x) plus the cross-entropy between the aggregated posterior and the prior; the authors argue the KL/cross-entropy term encourages q(z) to pick the modes of p(z), and the AAE replaces the two regularization terms with an adversarial procedure that matches the whole distribution of p(z).([Relationship to Variational Autoencoders, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.05644v2#page=4 "In adversarial autoencoders, we replace the second two terms with an adversarial training procedure that encourages q(z) to match to the whole distribution of p(z)."))
- **c5** Qualitative comparison (2-D codes of held-out MNIST, Figure 2, same architecture): with a 2-D Gaussian prior the VAE roughly matches the prior shape but leaves local regions of the code space with no data points, which the authors take to indicate that the VAE may not have captured the data manifold as well as the AAE. Evidence is visual only.([Relationship to Variational Autoencoders, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.05644v2#page=4 "However, no data points map to several local regions of the coding space indicating that the VAE may not have captured the data manifold as well as the adversarial autoencoder."))
- **c6** Practical difference: backpropagating through the VAE's KL term by Monte Carlo sampling requires the exact functional form of the prior, whereas the AAE only needs samples from the prior (demonstrated with a swiss-roll prior).([Relationship to Variational Autoencoders, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.05644v2#page=5 "However, in AAEs, we only need to be able to sample from the prior distribution in order to induce q(z) to match p(z)."))
- **c7** Likelihood evaluation protocol (real-valued MNIST and Toronto Face Dataset): since p(x) cannot be computed directly, a Gaussian Parzen window is fitted to samples generated from the model and the test-data likelihood is computed under it, with the Parzen bandwidth chosen by cross-validation; 10K- and 10M-sample estimates are reported.([Likelihood Analysis of Adversarial Autoencoders, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.05644v2#page=7 "We ﬁt a Gaussian Parzen window (kernel density estimator) to 10, 000 samples generated from the model and compute the likelihood of the test data under this distribution."))
- **c8** Stated caveat on that evaluation: although the AAE achieved superior log-likelihoods in all their comparisons, the authors warn that the currently available likelihood metrics for generative models such as GANs are deeply flawed, citing Theis et al. for problems including the 10K and 10M sample Parzen window estimates.([Likelihood Analysis of Adversarial Autoencoders, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.05644v2#page=7 "However, the reader must be aware that the metrics currently available for evaluating the likelihood of generative models such as GANs are deeply ﬂawed."))
