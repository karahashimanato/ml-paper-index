- **c1** Main observation (framed in the abstract as a counter-intuitive empirical observation): maximization-based decoding methods such as beam search lead to degeneration, i.e. output text that is bland, incoherent, or stuck in repetitive loops.([Abstract, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.09751v2#page=1 "maximization-based decoding methods such as beam search lead to degeneration — output text that is bland, incoherent, or gets stuck in repetitive loops."))
- **c2** Contribution: Nucleus Sampling, proposed as a simple method that draws considerably higher quality text from neural language models than previous decoding strategies.([Abstract, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.09751v2#page=1 "To address this we propose Nucleus Sampling, a simple but effective method to draw considerably higher quality text out of neural language models than previous decoding strategies."))
- **c3** Against a search-error explanation: asking whether beam search merely fails to find higher-probability, higher-quality text, the authors point to Figures 2 and 6, where the per-token probability of natural text is on average much lower than that of beam-search text; they conjecture that human text not being the most probable is an intrinsic property of human language.([Natural Language Does Not Maximize Probability, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.09751v2#page=7 "Yet Figures 2 & 6 show that the per-token probability of natural text is, on average, much lower than text generated by beam search."))
- **c4** Protocol: GPT-2 Large (762M parameters) generates 5,000 passages conditioned on WebText held-out paragraphs, each ending at an end-of-document token or 200 tokens; methods are compared against human text on perplexity, Zipf coefficient, Self-BLEU, repetition and HUSE.([Experimental setup, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.09751v2#page=6 "Our analysis is based on generating 5,000 text passages, which end upon reaching an end-of-document token or a maximum length of 200 tokens."))
- **c5** Repetition metric: a phrase of at least two tokens counts as a repetition when it repeats at least three times at the end of the generation (within the first 200 tokens).([Distributional Statistical Evaluation, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.09751v2#page=9 "A phrase (minimum length 2) is considered a repetition when it repeats at least three times at the end of the generation."))
- **c6** Link to greedy decoding: all stochastic methods show repetition issues when their parameters are set too low, which over-truncates and mimics greedy search.([Distributional Statistical Evaluation, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.09751v2#page=9 "Further, all stochastic methods face repetition issues when their tuning parameters are set too low, which tends to over-truncate, mimicking greedy search."))
- **c7** Caveat on beam width: beam search looks less repetitive on this metric as beam width grows largely because generations get shorter as the beam width increases.([Distributional Statistical Evaluation, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.09751v2#page=9 "Finally, although beam search becomes less repetitive according to this metric as beam width increases, this is largely because average length gets shorter as b increases (see Appendix A)."))
