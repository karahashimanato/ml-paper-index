## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Method: starting from a random initialization, Pathfinder locates normal approximations along a quasi-Newton (L-BFGS) optimization path, with local covariance from the optimizer's inverse Hessian estimates, and returns draws from the approximation with the lowest estimated KL divergence; the Monte Carlo KL estimates are embarrassingly parallel, while L-BFGS itself is the serial bottleneck.([Abstract, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.03782v4#page=1 "Pathﬁnder returns draws from the approximation with the lowest estimated Kullback-Leibler (KL) divergence to the true posterior."))
- **c2** Failure handling: in some cases the optimization path terminates at the initialization point and in others it fails to produce a positive definite inverse Hessian estimate; in both cases Pathfinder essentially fails, and it then returns the last iterate with infinite approximate log density so that the failed fit gets zero importance weight in multi-path Pathfinder.([Section II.1, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.03782v4#page=5 "In both of these settings, Pathﬁnder essentially fails."))
- **c3** Multi-path Pathfinder runs I independent Pathfinder instances and importance-resamples among their draws using Pareto-smoothed importance weights (building on Vehtari et al., 2019), so that paths stuck in minor modes, saddle points or plateaus can be down-weighted; the authors state that, as far as they know, Pareto smoothing had not been applied to importance resampling before.([Section II.7, p.12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.03782v4#page=12 "As far as we know, Pareto smoothing has not been previously applied to importance resampling, but only to importance sampling."))
- **c4** Evaluation protocol: 20 posteriordb models, each with a reference posterior of 10,000 roughly independent draws; 100 repetitions each of single-path Pathfinder (defaults L_max = 1000, relative tolerance 1e-13, history J = 6, K = 5 ELBO draws, M = 100 draws), multi-path Pathfinder (I = 20 runs, R = 100 draws), Stan phase I warmup (NUTS, last of 75 iterations), and dense and mean-field ADVI with Stan default settings, all initialized from uniform(-2, 2); approximations are compared by 1-Wasserstein distance on the unconstrained scale.([Section III.1, p.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.03782v4#page=14 "We evaluate Pathﬁnder using the 20 models and data sets from posteriordb (Magnusson et al., 2021), each of which is supplied with reference posteriors in the form of 10,000 roughly independent draws."))
- **c5** Cost is counted as log density and gradient evaluations (implementation agnostic); since gradients typically take closer to 80% of compute for Stan phase I and ADVI (citing Carpenter et al., 2015), the authors conclude the other methods require around 30 to 50 times more operations than Pathfinder. These counts consider serial execution only; multi-path Pathfinder needs 20 times the evaluations of single-path plus resampling.([Section III.1, p.16](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.03782v4#page=16 "Because gradients are more expensive with automatic differentiation than log density evaluations, the results indicate that other methods require around 30 to 50 times more operations than Pathﬁnder."))
- **c6** Failure mode with multiple modes: on the hidden Markov model bball_drive_event_0 (multiple meaningful posterior modes) mean-field ADVI did much better than single-path Pathfinder, which the authors attribute to SGD noise letting ADVI escape minor modes that trap L-BFGS; multi-path Pathfinder removed this problem for that model.([Section III.1, p.15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.03782v4#page=15 "The noise inherent in the stochastic gradient descent approach used by ADVI allows it to escape minor modes than can trap the L-BFGS optimizer used by Pathﬁnder."))
- **c7** For posteriors far from normal (e.g. the gp_pois_regr model), Pathfinder's draws concentrate in a small part of the high-probability region; the authors say this is expected because selecting the normal approximation by minimizing KL to the target favors more concentrated approximations, so Pathfinder tends to make a conservative guess.([Section III.3, p.24](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.03782v4#page=24 "Therefore, Pathﬁnder tends to make a conservative guess on the high probability region when the posterior cannot be well approximated by a normal distribution."))
- **c8** Limitations shown on challenging posteriors: results are sensitive to the initial distribution (underdispersed draws when started in a high-density region of a funnel); importance resampling cannot recover when all optimization paths are trapped in minor modes; on a 3075-parameter horseshoe logistic regression with many modes, optimization from random inits tends to find the mode at the origin, so in high dimensions Pathfinder can miss other relevant modes; weakly identified posteriors also frustrate it.([Section III.3, p.27](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.03782v4#page=27 "Thus, even though Pathﬁnder is ﬁnding a single region of high probability, in high dimensions it can fail to ﬁnd other relevant high probability modes."))
- **c9** Use for MCMC initialization: the stated motivation is to cheaply generate draws to initialize MCMC; initialization only needs draws inside the high-probability volume, not coverage of the posterior, and the authors say problems with Pathfinder's distributional approximation can be diagnosed with the Pareto-k statistic (citing Yao et al., 2018). In the birthday Gaussian process example, 5 of 20 randomly initialized adaptive HMC chains were trapped in minor modes, and Pathfinder initialization avoided them.([Section IV, p.31](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.03782v4#page=31 "Initialization to remove most of the transient bias of MCMC can succeed by producing draws within the high probability volume of the posterior without covering that posterior—it only requires draws to look reasonable."))
