## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Calibration notions: full multiclass calibration, classwise calibration (each one-vs-rest probability calibrated) and confidence calibration (only the top-class probability, as used by Guo et al.); the authors note that some authors use the weaker confidence calibration and that the multiclass setting has subtleties not always recognised.([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.12656v1#page=2 "some authors use the weaker notion of conﬁdence calibration (our term), which requires only that the classiﬁer’s predicted probability for what it considers the most likely class is calibrated"))
- **c2** Temperature scaling (one parameter fit on a hold-out validation set) made a wide ResNet on CIFAR-10 nearly confidence-calibrated (the Vaicenavicius et al. test did not reject at alpha = 0.01), yet it systematically mis-estimated individual classes; with a single parameter it cannot act differently on different classes, motivating classwise-ECE.([Section 2 (Evaluation of calibration and temperature scaling), p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.12656v1#page=3 "Having only a single tuneable parameter, temperature scaling cannot learn to act differently on different classes."))
- **c3** Fundamental limitation of reliability diagrams and ECE measures: predicting the overall class distribution regardless of the instance gives almost perfect calibration, so other measures (error rate, proper losses such as Brier score or log-loss) should always be considered too.([Section 2 (Evaluation of calibration and temperature scaling), p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.12656v1#page=4 "Namely, it is easy to obtain almost perfectly calibrated probabilities by predicting the overall class distribution, regardless of the given instance."))
- **c4** Relationship to other families: for 2 classes Dirichlet calibration coincides with beta calibration; temperature-scaling maps are contained in the Dirichlet family; matrix scaling uses the same layer but on logits, and because softmax followed by log loses information, matrix scaling has an informational advantage on deep nets.([Section 3 (Dirichlet calibration), p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.12656v1#page=6 "As softmax followed by log-transform is losing information, matrix scaling has an informational advantage over Dirichlet calibration on deep neural networks, which we will turn back to in the experiments section."))
- **c5** Assumption: although derived from Dirichlet-distributed likelihoods, the method does not assume the probability vectors are actually Dirichlet-distributed within each class (analogous to Platt scaling and Gaussian likelihoods).([Section 5 (Conclusion), p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.12656v1#page=9 "While derived from Dirichlet-distributed likelihoods, it does not assume that the probability vectors are actually Dirichlet-distributed within each class"))
- **c6** Overfitting: responding to Guo et al.'s view that calibration models with many parameters overfit a small validation set, the authors agree some overfitting happens, but find L2 regularisation suffices on non-neural models and propose ODIR for deep nets; fitting minimises log-loss on validation instances plus ODIR terms whose weights are tuned by internal cross-validation on the validation data.([Section 3 (Fitting and ODIR regularisation), p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.12656v1#page=6 "We agree that some overﬁtting happens, but in our experiments a simple L2 regularisation sufﬁces on non-neural models"))
- **c7** Non-neural protocol: 21 UCI datasets x 11 classifiers; calibration maps trained with 3-fold internal cross-validation inside 5x5-fold external cross-validation, the 3 maps averaged as an ensemble; calibrator hyperparameters chosen on the classifier's training fold by lowest log-loss; 8 measures (including conf-ECE, cw-ECE and their significance-test variants) compared with Friedman and Bonferroni-Dunn tests. The authors note svc-linear and svc-rbf have an unfair advantage because sklearn applies Platt scaling internally.([Section 4 (Calibration of non-neural models), p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.12656v1#page=7 "We used 3-fold internal cross-validation to train the calibration maps within the 5 times 5-fold external cross-validation."))
- **c8** Deep-net protocol: 11 CNNs on CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100 and SVHN; following Guo et al., 5,000 training instances (6,000 for SVHN) were set aside for calibration, except for 3 pretrained models where 5,000 test instances were set aside; regularisation chosen by 5-fold cross-validation on the validation set and the 5 resulting calibrators averaged. Unregularised matrix scaling was excluded as reported to perform very poorly.([Section 4 (Calibration of deep neural networks), p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.12656v1#page=8 "On other models we followed [9], setting aside 5,000 training instances (6,000 in SVHN) for calibration purposes and training the models as in the original papers."))
- **c9** Deep-net results are hedged: the best general-purpose calibrator depends heavily on model and dataset; Dir-L2 is poor on CIFAR-100 while Dir-ODIR beats temperature scaling on cw-ECE at a minor log-loss cost; by average rank Dir-ODIR is best but without statistical significance. Logit-based MS-ODIR beat Dir-ODIR in most cases but the effect is numerically usually very small (under 1% average relative reduction).([Section 4 (Calibration of deep neural networks), p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.12656v1#page=8 "According to the average rank across all deep net experiments, Dir-ODIR is best, but without statistical signiﬁcance."))
