## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Setting: the only access to the model is a log probability query for a sequence (no access to training data or parameters), mirroring API-based providers and open models with secret training data; contamination detection is framed as a hypothesis test of H0 (model independent of dataset X) vs H1 (dependent) with false-positive rate at most alpha.([Problem Setting, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.17623v2#page=3 "In our setting, the only method we have to study θ is through a log probability query log pθ(s) for a sequence s (i.e. no access to dataset or parameters)."))
- **c2** Key assumption: exchangeability of the dataset (every ordering of the examples equally likely), which holds under the standard i.i.d. assumption; under exchangeability and H0 the log probability of the concatenated canonical ordering has the same distribution as that of a random permutation (Proposition 1), which gives the false-positive guarantee.([Problem Setting (False positives under H0), p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.17623v2#page=3 "Notably, this assumption would hold under the standard assumption that the dataset is a collection of i.i.d examples."))
- **c3** Sharded rank comparison test (Algorithm 1): partition the examples into r shards, for each shard compute the canonical-order log likelihood minus the mean over m random permutations, and run a one-sided t-test on these differences. The authors motivate it by the naive permutation test's O(m|X|) cost and 1/(m+1) minimum p-value, and its requirement that the canonical order beat nearly all permutations, which can fail if the model prefers certain orderings regardless of training.([Methods (Algorithm 1), p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.17623v2#page=5 "Run a one-sided t-test for E[si] > 0, returning the associated p-value of the test as p."))
- **c4** The sharded test's guarantee is weaker than the permutation test's: it gives asymptotic rather than finite-sample valid p-values (as the number of permutations grows, Theorem 2) and assumes i.i.d. X for the proof; the authors say this could be relaxed with Berry-Esseen bounds or a CLT for exchangeable variables but chose the simpler test.([Methods, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.17623v2#page=6 "it provides asymptotic, rather than finite-sample valid p-values and assumes i.i.d X for the proof."))
- **c5** Positive-control setup: a 1.4B-parameter GPT-2 model trained from scratch on Wikitext (from RedPajama) plus 10 test sets subsampled to around 1000 examples and randomly shuffled to make them exchangeable, each duplicated between 1 and 100 times; 20.2B tokens in total, 20M of them benchmark tokens. Test parameters were fixed (50 shards, 51 permutations) to avoid the possibility of p-hacking.([Section 4.1, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.17623v2#page=6 "The overall pretraining dataset has 20.2B tokens, with 20M tokens associated with some benchmark dataset."))
- **c6** Sensitivity to duplication count: the test gives near-zero p-values at duplication rates of 10 or above, cannot detect contamination at a duplication rate of 1, begins to detect some test sets at counts of 2 and 4, with a detection threshold around 4; the authors note prior work found duplication-1 detection extremely difficult and state it is an open question whether any test can reliably detect it.([Section 4.1, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.17623v2#page=7 "We find that we cannot reliably detect duplication rates of 1, but that at counts of 2 and 4 we begin to detect some test sets (gray points below the dashed line) and that the detection threshold is around a duplication rate of 4."))
- **c7** Open models (LLaMA2-7B, Mistral-7B, Pythia-1.4B, GPT-2 XL; BioMedLM as a negative control) on eight benchmarks, 50 shards and 250 permutations, first 5,000 examples: mostly non-significant results, which the authors say are not direct evidence for the null; Mistral on AI2-ARC gave p = 0.001, which they say would be at the boundary of significance after multiple-testing correction and should be interpreted cautiously. They present the results as first steps toward third-party audits, not proof of contamination of specific models.([Evaluating Existing Models for Dataset Contamination, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.17623v2#page=9 "We present these results as showing promising first steps towards third-party audits of test set contamination, rather than a direct proof of contamination of specific models."))
- **c8** Limitations: no multiple-test corrections are applied to the reported p-values; for off-the-shelf benchmarks it is difficult to know whether the dataset is truly exchangeable (it cannot be proven without knowing the data-generating process, negative controls only help heuristically), so the authors strongly encourage dataset creators to randomly shuffle their datasets and publicize it.([Limitations, p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.17623v2#page=10 "We strongly encourage future dataset creators to apply a random shuffle to their datasets (and to publicize this fact), which would allow our tests to be applied."))
- **c9** The test addresses only verbatim contamination (the model ingesting the test set directly); contamination via sources used to build a benchmark (e.g. Wikipedia for SQuAD) or more complex partial contamination cannot be ruled out.([Limitations, p.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.17623v2#page=11 "Verbatim memorization of a test set is not the only form of contamination, and our test cannot rule out the possibility of more complex forms of partial contamination."))
