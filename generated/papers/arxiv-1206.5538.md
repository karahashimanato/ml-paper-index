## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Why plain reconstruction is not enough: since the identity function has zero reconstruction error everywhere, something in the training criterion or the parametrization must prevent the auto-encoder from learning it; the authors call auto-encoders with such means regularized auto-encoders, a low-dimensional code (as in the classical auto-encoder or PCA) being one particular form.([Section 7.1, p.13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.5538v3#page=13 "To capture the structure of the data-generating distribution, it is therefore important that something in the training criterion or the parametrization prevents the auto-encoder from learning the identity function, which has zero reconstruction error everywhere."))
- **c2** A linear auto-encoder with squared reconstruction error learns the same subspace as PCA; citing Bourlard and Kamp (1988), this also holds with a sigmoid encoder, but not when encoder and decoder weights are tied.([Section 7.1, p.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.5538v3#page=14 "In the case of a linear auto-encoder (linear encoder and decoder) with squared reconstruction error, minimizing Eq. 19 learns the same subspace"))
- **c3** Overcomplete codes (more hidden units than inputs), favoured by sparse coding and RBM approaches, can let the auto-encoder simply copy the input into the features and reconstruct perfectly without extracting meaningful features; regularized auto-encoders are presented as ways to constrain the representation even when it is overcomplete.([Section 7.2, p.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.5538v3#page=14 "This can allow the auto-encoder to simply duplicate the input in the features, with perfect reconstruction without having extracted more meaningful features."))
- **c4** Sparse auto-encoders: sparsity has been obtained by penalizing hidden biases (which risks the weights compensating for the bias) or the hidden activations directly (L1, Student-t, or a KL penalty towards a target mean activation); the authors state that a clear comparative analysis of the activation-penalty variants is still lacking.([Section 7.2.1, p.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.5538v3#page=14 "When directly penalizing the hidden unit outputs, several variants can be found in the literature, but a clear comparative analysis is still lacking."))
- **c5** Denoising auto-encoders (citing Vincent et al. 2008, 2010) are trained to reconstruct the clean input from an artificially corrupted one, so learning the identity is no longer enough; the reconstruction is described as essentially a nearby point of higher density than the corrupted input.([Section 7.2.2, p.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.5538v3#page=14 "Learning the identity is no longer enough: the learner must capture the structure of the input distribution in order to optimally undo the effect of the corruption process, with the reconstruction essentially being a nearby but higher density point than the corrupted input."))
- **c6** Contractive auto-encoders (citing Rifai et al. 2011a) add the Frobenius norm of the encoder Jacobian; the authors list three differences from DAEs (feature rather than reconstruction sensitivity is penalized, the penalty is analytic rather than stochastic, and a hyper-parameter controls the trade-off) that may be partly responsible for the better performance CAE features seem to show, and note that the analytic penalty only encourages robustness to infinitesimal input variations.([Section 7.2.3, p.15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.5538v3#page=15 "There are at least three notable differences with DAEs, which may be partly responsible for the better performance that CAE features seem to empirically demonstrate"))
- **c7** Implication of the score interpretation (citing Vincent 2011 and Alain and Bengio 2012): the reconstruction-minus-input vector is aligned with the estimated score, which can be zero at local maxima but also at local minima of the log-density, so low reconstruction error cannot be equated with high estimated probability.([Section 9.2, p.19](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.5538v3#page=19 "It means that we cannot equate low reconstruction error with high estimated probability."))
- **c8** Evaluation caveat: test-set reconstruction error of auto-encoder and sparse coding variants may be misleading, because larger capacity (more features, more training time) tends to lower it even on the test set, so it cannot be used reliably to select most hyper-parameters; denoising reconstruction error is stated to be immune to this, but not for the hyper-parameter controlling the amount of noise or contraction.([Section 9.5, p.20](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.5538v3#page=20 "Hence it cannot be used reliably for selecting most hyper-parameters."))
