## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Setting and guarantee: an online setting where the data-generating distribution may vary over time in an unknown fashion; previous conformal methods rely on exchangeability, while ACI provably achieves the desired coverage frequency over long time intervals regardless of the true data-generating process.([Abstract, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.00170v3#page=1 "our adaptive approach provably achieves the desired coverage frequency over long-time intervals irrespective of the true data generating process."))
- **c2** Method: since under non-stationarity the realized miscoverage M_t(alpha) need not equal alpha, the argument of the fitted quantile function is recalibrated online, lowering (raising) alpha_t when past sets under-covered (over-covered), via alpha_{t+1} = alpha_t + gamma (alpha - err_t); this requires the label Y_t to be observed after each prediction. A weighted-history variant gave almost identical results in their experiments.([Adapting conformal inference to distribution shifts, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.00170v3#page=3 "To perform this calibration we will use a simple online update."))
- **c3** Distribution-free result (Proposition 4.1): with probability one, |(1/T) sum_t err_t - alpha| <= (max{alpha_1, 1 - alpha_1} + gamma) / (T gamma) for all T, so the long-run miscoverage frequency converges to alpha with no assumptions on the data. The authors argue that without assumptions on the initialization this cannot be improved in the worst case right after initialization.([Section 4.1, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.00170v3#page=6 "Proposition 4.1 puts no constraints on the data generating distribution."))
- **c4** Long-run versus per-time coverage: the large-deviation bound on the average miscoverage (Theorem 4.1) says nothing about marginal coverage at a single time step; Theorem 4.2 bounds the expected squared gap between realized marginal miscoverage and alpha by the size of the shift in the optimal alpha*_t, under a hidden Markov model with a stationary chain, a quantile/score function fixed over time and a Lipschitz miscoverage function.([Section 4.2, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.00170v3#page=8 "On the other hand, it provides no information about the marginal coverage frequency at a single time step."))
- **c5** The hidden-Markov-model assumptions behind the per-time results are simplifications: the authors do not expect them to hold exactly in any real-world setting, though they consider the results representative and expect similar results under other models.([Section 4.2, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.00170v3#page=7 "While we do not expect these assumptions to hold exactly in any real-world setting, we do consider our results to be representative of the true behaviour of adaptive conformal inference and we expect similar results to hold under alternative models."))
- **c6** Step size: gamma trades adaptability against stability (large gamma lets alpha_t oscillate between small conservative and large anti-conservative sets); the experiments use gamma = 0.005, chosen because it was found to give relatively stable trajectories while still adapting; too small a gamma behaves like fixed alpha. The data on which this choice was made is not stated (searched for tuned, validation, held out).([Section 2.1, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.00170v3#page=3 "The choice of γ gives a tradeoff between adaptability and stability."))
- **c7** Volatility experiment: GARCH(1,1) point predictions refit on the last 1250 trading days, a normalized absolute-residual score, alpha = 0.1, compared with fixed alpha via local coverage over about two years. The four stocks shown were selected out of 12 examined because they showed a clear failure of the non-adaptive method; ACI was found to perform well in all cases (appendix figure).([Section 2.2, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.00170v3#page=5 "These stocks were selected out of a total of 12 stocks that we examined because they showed a clear failure of the non-adaptive method."))
- **c8** Score choice: coverage of ACI is governed by the size of the shift in alpha*_t, which corresponds to the 1 - alpha quantile of the score, so it depends on how close the score is to stationary; an unnormalized residual score gave much wider swings in local coverage than the normalized one (partially mitigated by a larger gamma). Interval length depends on the fitted model, so the model should be refit on recent data.([Impact of St(·) on the performance, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.00170v3#page=9 "Thus, the coverage properties of adaptive conformal inference depend on how close St(Xt, Yt) is to being stationary."))
- **c9** Limitations stated by the authors: the method assumes Y_t is revealed at every time point (delayed or batched feedback is not covered); the per-time theory covers a single data model with a fixed quantile function; gamma should ideally be chosen adaptively without prior knowledge; experiments cover only two domains.([Discussion, p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.00170v3#page=10 "The methods we develop are speciﬁc to cases where Yt is revealed at each time point."))
