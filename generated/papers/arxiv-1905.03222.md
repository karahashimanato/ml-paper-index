## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Setting and guarantee: samples are assumed exchangeable (e.g. i.i.d.) from an arbitrary joint distribution; the target is marginal coverage P{Y_{n+1} in C(X_{n+1})} >= 1 - alpha with probability over all n + 1 samples. The training data are split into a proper training set and a calibration set, and the conformalized interval satisfies the guarantee regardless of the choice or accuracy of the quantile regression estimator.([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.03222v1#page=2 "Unlike the original interval, the conformalized prediction interval is guaranteed to satisfy the coverage requirement (1) regardless of the choice or accuracy of the quantile regression estimator."))
- **c2** Plain quantile regression is adaptive to heteroscedasticity but is not guaranteed to cover in finite samples (validity only for specific models under regularity and asymptotic conditions); the authors state that the absence of a finite-sample guarantee can sometimes be disastrous and that their experiments show neural-network quantile intervals can substantially undercover.([Section 2, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.03222v1#page=3 "Indeed, the absence of any ﬁnite sample guarantee can sometimes be disastrous."))
- **c3** Limitation of standard split conformal regression: the interval length is fixed (twice the calibrated residual quantile) and independent of X_{n+1}; citing Lei et al., full conformal intervals also vary only slightly with X_{n+1} if the regression algorithm is moderately stable. Split conformal avoids the infinitely many refits of full conformal at the cost of splitting the data.([Section 3, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.03222v1#page=4 "A closer look at the prediction interval (8) reveals a major limitation of this procedure"))
- **c4** Theorem 1: if (X_i, Y_i), i = 1..n+1, are exchangeable, split CQR has coverage >= 1 - alpha; if the conformity scores are almost surely distinct, coverage is also <= 1 - alpha + 1/(|I2| + 1). The result holds even conditionally on the proper training set.([Section 4, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.03222v1#page=6 "The result even holds, and we will prove it, conditionally on the proper training set."))
- **c5** Hyperparameters of the underlying quantile regressor may be tuned by cross validation on the proper training set (minimizing average interval length); because quantile regression forests are often and quantile networks occasionally overly conservative, the nominal quantiles can be tuned as hyperparameters, which does not invalidate the coverage guarantee but may yield shorter intervals.([Section 4, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.03222v1#page=7 "Notably, this tuning does not invalidate the coverage guarantee, but it may yield shorter intervals, as our experiments conﬁrm."))
- **c6** Limitations of locally adaptive (scaled-residual) split conformal: when data are homoskedastic it inflates intervals (noted by Lei et al., presumably due to extra variability from estimating the scale); more fundamentally, the scale model is fit on proper-training residuals, which are biased by the fitting, so it tends to systematically underestimate prediction error, forcing a large correction and less adaptive intervals (severe for neural networks that nearly interpolate the training data).([Section 5, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.03222v1#page=8 "Because it uses the proper training residuals (as it must to ensure valid coverage), the locally adaptive method tends to systematically underestimate the prediction error."))
- **c7** Protocol: eleven regression benchmark datasets; features standardized and response rescaled using statistics from the proper training set only (to preserve exchangeability); 20 random 80/20 training-test splits; proper training and calibration sets of equal size; alpha = 0.1. Random forests use sklearn defaults except 1000 trees; network epochs are tuned by cross validation (upper limit 1000); gamma = 1 for the locally adaptive offset. Quantile forests' coverage parameters are tuned by cross validation.([Section 6, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.03222v1#page=8 "The proper training and calibration sets for split conformal prediction have equal size."))
- **c8** Results summary (Table 1, 2,200 experiments): on average CQR achieves shorter intervals than standard and locally adaptive conformal prediction; the authors call it possibly surprising that CQR also beats non-conformalized quantile regression (which uses more training data) and give possible explanations (signed scores mitigating overcoverage; tuning quantiles by cross validation, where CQR selects quantiles below nominal).([Section 6, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.03222v1#page=9 "On average, our method achieves shorter prediction intervals than both standard and locally adaptive conformal prediction."))
- **c9** All conformal methods reached the nominal 90% coverage; non-conformalized quantile forests were slightly conservative and quantile networks tended to undercover. Citing Tagasovska & Lopez-Paz, quantile-network coverage depends greatly on hyperparameter tuning, which the authors take as showing the importance of the finite-sample guarantee.([Section 6, p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.03222v1#page=10 "Turning to the issue of valid coverage, all methods based on conformal prediction successfully construct prediction bands at the nominal coverage rate of 90%, as the theory suggests they should."))
