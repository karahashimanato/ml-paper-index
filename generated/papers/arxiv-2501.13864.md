## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Thesis: autoencoder anomaly detection assumes normal data is reconstructed more accurately than anomalies; the authors show this assumption does not hold and illustrate that anomalies far from normal data can be perfectly reconstructed in practice.([Abstract, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.13864v1#page=1 "In this work we show that this assumption indeed does not hold, and illustrate that anomalies, lying far away from normal data, can be perfectly reconstructed in practice."))
- **c2** Linear theory (PCA and linear AE): any point in the span of the first d principal components has zero reconstruction loss, and moving along this subspace outward from the convex hull gives points beyond any distance delta (Euclidean) from all training data; for linear autoencoders the authors assume convergence to the global optimum (citing Baldi & Hornik) so the case reduces to PCA, with bias terms covered in Appendix A.1. The proof holds in the semi-supervised setting (training data contain no anomalies) and hence also the unsupervised one; other distance metrics are only posited.([Out-of-bounds reconstruction: PCA, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.13864v1#page=4 "We can prove this even in the semi-supervised setting, where we guarantee that the model was not exposed to anomalous data at training time."))
- **c3** Non-linear (ReLU) case: for a 2D dataset on the diagonal, a ReLU autoencoder can find the same weights as a linear one, and far points on the diagonal are perfectly reconstructed; beyond this simple case the authors only theorize that similar linear behavior can occur outside the convex hull, and show it experimentally on synthetic 2D data (100 points per distribution, [2,5,1,5,2] or deeper autoencoders).([Failure of a non-linear network with ReLU activations, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.13864v1#page=6 "We theorize that even beyond this simple case, similar linear behavior can occur beyond the convex hull that the normal data occupies."))
- **c4** Activation functions: C0 activations such as ReLU suffer most from out-of-bounds reconstruction but are easier to train deep; C-infinity activations such as sigmoid generally have more desirable properties for anomaly detection but suffer from vanishing gradients, and sigmoid autoencoders can still show low-loss regions where the two clusters' sigmoids intersect.([Other activation functions, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.13864v1#page=7 "In summary, activation functions with an order of continuity of C0 suffer most from out-of-bounds reconstruction, but allow for more easily trainable deep networks."))
- **c5** Image experiment: a convolutional autoencoder (2-layer encoder/decoder, ReLU, sigmoid output) with a 2-dimensional latent space (chosen to rule out the 'identical shortcut') is trained on MNIST subsets (digits 4, 5, 7; digits 0, 1); decoding low-loss latent grid points gives adversarial anomalies with lower reconstruction loss than all training data. The authors note there is no consensus on which digits are normal and that only in some of their tried combinations does out-of-bounds reconstruction occur.([Failure on real-world data: MNIST, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.13864v1#page=8 "In our experiments, both shown and non-shown, we have tried several different combinations and observe that in some cases out-of-bounds reconstruction occurs."))
- **c6** Instability of the failure: whether out-of-bounds reconstruction is easily detected depends on the random seed and the digits chosen as normal, and it can disappear and reappear between epochs; the authors argue that in semi-supervised or unsupervised settings one cannot accurately judge whether a network will fail on future data. They also note some out-of-bounds reconstruction (an unusual diagonal 1) matches what a user would consider normal.([Failure on real-world data: MNIST, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.13864v1#page=9 "We observe that depending on the random seed used for initialization and the digits selected as normal data, the out-of-bounds reconstruction may or may not be easily detected."))
- **c7** On proposed fixes (related work): the authors state that, from their experiments, relying on distances or densities in the latent space (as in Zong et al. 2018 and Cheng et al. 2021) does not always work in practice; they also say memory modules reduce reconstruction ability and that they do not think adversarially robust training (Salehi et al. 2021) results in a reliable autoencoder.([Related work, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.13864v1#page=2 "From our experiments we can glean that relying on distances or densities in the latent space does not always work in practice."))
- **c8** Recommendation: practitioners should at least check whether a trained non-linear autoencoder shows out-of-bounds reconstruction, e.g. by searching for adversarial anomalies in the latent space; for larger latent spaces projected gradient descent 'might be' a feasible strategy (not tested in the paper).([Conclusion, p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.13864v1#page=10 "Good practice would be to at least check whether a trained non-linear autoencoder exhibits the undesirable out-of-bounds reconstruction."))
