## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Definitions: the T-learner estimates the control and treatment response functions separately (possibly with different base learners) and takes their difference; the S-learner fits a single model on all features plus the treatment indicator, without giving the indicator a special role, and takes the difference of predictions with the indicator set to treatment vs control.([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.03461v6#page=2 "We refer to this meta-algorithm as the S-learner, since it uses a “single” estimator."))
- **c2** Estimand and assumptions: the target is the CATE tau(x) = E[Y(1) - Y(0) | X = x]; the individual treatment effect is not identifiable without strong additional assumptions, but the best CATE estimator is also the best ITE estimator in MSE. The authors assume no hidden confounders (Condition 1) and note that this alone is not sufficient to identify the CATE, adding a propensity score bounded away from 0 and 1 (Condition 2).([Framework and Definitions, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.03461v6#page=3 "This assumption is, however, not suﬃcient to identify the CATE."))
- **c3** The X-learner is motivated by the observation that individual treatment effects are never observed: it uses the observed outcomes and first-stage response-function estimates to impute the unobserved individual effects, and then estimates the CATE in a second step as if those effects were observed.([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.03461v6#page=2 "The X-learner uses the observed outcomes to estimate the unobserved individual treatment eﬀects."))
- **c4** Choice of the weight function in the X-learner's final stage: based on their experience, the authors find it good to use an estimated propensity score for g, but say it also makes sense to set g to 1 or 0 when the number of treated units is very large or small relative to the number of controls; with an estimated covariance of the two stage-2 estimates, g could be chosen to minimize variance.([X-learner, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.03461v6#page=4 "Based on our experience, we observe that it is good to use an estimate of the propensity score for g,"))
- **c5** Simulation summary (synthetic data; RF and BART base learners): for each meta-learner there exist cases where it is best. The S-learner can ignore the treatment indicator, which helps when the CATE is often 0 but can bias it toward 0; the T-learner does well when the CATE is very complicated and mu0, mu1 share no trends; the X-learner does particularly well with structural assumptions on the CATE or very unequal group sizes. As a rule of thumb, unless one strongly believes the CATE is mostly 0, the authors suggest the X-learner with BART for small data sets and RF for bigger ones.([Simulation Results, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.03461v6#page=6 "These simulation results lead us to the conclusion that unless one has a strong belief that the CATE is mostly 0, then, as a rule of thumb, one should use the X-learner with BART for small data sets and RF for bigger ones."))
- **c6** Theorem 2: with m control and n treated units, under bounded-variance independent errors, finite second moments of X, ignorability, a control-response estimator with EMSE <= C0 m^(-a), a CATE that is linear in x, and a well-conditioned sample covariance of the treated covariates, the X-learner with OLS in the second stage and g = 0 has EMSE <= C(m^(-a) + n^(-1)); if there are many controls (m >= c3 n^(1/a)) it attains the parametric rate in n. The T-learner, in contrast, in general cannot be expected to beat O(m^(-a_mu) + n^(-a_mu)) (Theorem 1).([Smoothness conditions for the CATE, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.03461v6#page=8 "The following theorem deals carefully with this estimation error when τ is linear, but the response functions can be estimated at any nonparametric rate."))
- **c7** Beyond the linear-CATE case (and a Lipschitz case with no CATE assumptions, where T and X attain the same minimax rate), the faster X-learner rates are stated only as a conjecture: the authors say it is mathematically very challenging to state the extra conditions needed, while believing the conjecture holds much more generally.([Comparison of Convergence Rates, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.03461v6#page=8 "It turns out to be mathematically very challenging to give a satisfying statement of the extra conditions needed on P."))
- **c8** Observational setting: the authors state that all of the meta-learners discussed can handle confounding as long as ignorability holds, and test this in one confounded simulation (propensity depending on an observed covariate, following Wager & Athey); causal forests are excluded there because, as their authors note, they are not designed for observational studies with only conditional unconfoundedness.([Supporting Information, A.3 Confounding, p.19](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.03461v6#page=19 "All of the meta-learners we discuss can handle confounding, as long as the ignorability assumption holds."))
