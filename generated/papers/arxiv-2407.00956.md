## 主張

- **c1** Benchmark composition: datasets from UCI, OpenML and Kaggle, filtered by excluding N < 500 or d < 5, > 20% missing values, trivially easy or majority-dominated classification sets, and duplicates; categorical features are ordinally encoded; 22 mis-specified regression datasets with two unique targets were relabelled as binary classification.([Section 4.2, p.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.00956v4#page=11 "Ultimately, Talent consists of 120 binary-classification, 80 multi-class, and 100 regression datasets, offering both breadth and depth for evaluating tabular learning."))
- **c2** Known data-quality issues are deliberately retained in the general benchmark: 13 datasets with potential leakage (kept for comparability with prior work and to assess robustness to imperfect data) and 25 datasets whose features are extracted from images or audio; all datasets with identified leakage are excluded from Talent-tiny.([Section 4.2, p.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.00956v4#page=11 "In our collection, we identify 13 datasets with potential leakage."))
- **c3** Protocol: each dataset is randomly split 64%/16%/20% into train/val/test (a single fixed hold-out, following Gorishniy et al.); hyperparameters are chosen on validation with Optuna using a fixed budget of 100 trials per method-dataset pair; the best configuration is retrained with 15 random seeds and the mean is reported. Full K-fold CV is described as computationally prohibitive at this scale.([Section 4.3, p.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.00956v4#page=14 "We use Optuna (Akiba et al., 2019) with a fixed budget of 100 trials per method–dataset pair."))
- **c4** Unlike the tuned models, pretrained tabular (foundation) models are evaluated from their latest public checkpoints with default inference hyperparameters, without per-dataset tuning; all deep methods use AdamW with batch size 1024 unless noted otherwise, and CatBoost receives the feature types for native categorical handling.([Section 4.3, p.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.00956v4#page=14 "Pretrained tabular models are evaluated from their latest public checkpoints with default inference hyperparameters (i.e., no per-dataset tuning)."))
- **c5** Summary observations over 40 methods and 300 datasets: tree-based ensembles (CatBoost, LightGBM, XGBoost) remain strong, reliable and statistically robust baselines, while pretrained foundation models (TabPFN v2, TabICL) deliver state-of-the-art performance across many datasets and significantly narrow, but do not entirely close, the gap between tree-based and DNN-based paradigms.([Section 5.1, p.19](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.00956v4#page=19 "Tree-based ensembles (CatBoost, LightGBM, XGBoost) remain strong, reliable, and statistically robust baselines."))
- **c6** Foundation models, ensembles and the top DNNs (RealMLP, ModernNCA) often remain statistically tied, which the authors say suggests universal superiority has not yet been achieved; in the same paragraph they note that scalability and computational cost remain open challenges for foundation models.([Section 5.1, p.19](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.00956v4#page=19 "Despite these advances, the Wilcoxon–Holm tests show that foundation models, ensembles, and top DNNs (RealMLP, ModernNCA) often remain statistically tied, suggesting that universal superiority has not yet been achieved."))
- **c7** Tree-mimic networks (NODE, TabNet) generally underperform relative to ensembles, whereas neighborhood-based models such as ModernNCA are often statistically comparable to CatBoost and LightGBM; ensembling within deep methods (TabM over MLP, MNCA-ens over ModernNCA) further improves performance.([Section 5.1, p.19](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.00956v4#page=19 "Tree-mimic networks such as NODE and TabNet generally underperform relative to ensembles."))
- **c8** Tree-DNN gap and heterogeneity: the per-dataset rank gap between tree-based methods (RF, XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost) and representative DNNs (RealMLP, TabM, ModernNCA, FT-Transformer) correlates most strongly (negatively) with feature-heterogeneity meta-features such as entropy_std (-0.3333), while inst_to_attr (emphasized by McElfresh et al., 2023) correlates more weakly with the gap; the authors say datasets with greater feature heterogeneity tend to favor tree-based methods, likely due to successive partitioning.([Section 6.5, p.35](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.00956v4#page=35 "These findings suggest that the degree of heterogeneity among dataset features is a more critical determinant driving the relative model superiority."))
- **c9** By feature type: raw-feature DNNs such as MLP and RealMLP do worst on datasets with mixed numerical and categorical features and better on purely numerical or purely categorical ones; tree-based methods excel on heterogeneous datasets; FT-Transformer behaves more like the tree-based models, which the authors attribute ('likely') to its learned embeddings of categorical and numerical features; TabPFN v2 is weakest on purely categorical datasets.([Section 6.6, p.36](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.00956v4#page=36 "DNN-based methods such as MLP and RealMLP exhibit their worst performance on datasets with mixed feature types (Mixed Data)."))
