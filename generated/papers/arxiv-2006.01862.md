## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Contribution: predictors that either predict or defer to a downstream expert, learned from samples of the expert's decisions by training a classifier and a rejector, based on a reduction to cost-sensitive learning with a consistent surrogate loss that generalizes cross entropy.([Abstract, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.01862v3#page=1 "Our approach is based on a novel reduction to cost sensitive learning where we give a consistent surrogate loss for cost sensitive learning that generalizes the cross entropy loss."))
- **c2** Guarantee (Theorem 1): L_CE (= L^alpha_CE with alpha = 1) is convex in g, upper-bounds the 0-1 system loss, and is classification-consistent: its minimizer over all measurable functions matches the Bayes classifier and Bayes rejector (defer when the expert's probability of being correct is at least the classifier's maximal class posterior, Proposition 2). Setting (Section 3): samples (x, y, m) with (x, y) i.i.d. from P and m drawn from the expert's distribution given (x, y); the guarantee is asymptotic/population-level, not a finite-sample or restricted-class statement.([Proposed Surrogate Loss, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.01862v3#page=5 "The proposed surrogate LCE is in fact consistent and upper bounds L0−1 as the following theorem demonstrates."))
- **c3** Authors' caveat on the guarantee: with the specific (restricted) hypothesis classes used in practice, if the Bayes predictor is not in the class, consistency might not guarantee much; the paper therefore also defines realizable (H, R)-consistency. It then defines a mixture-of-experts loss L_mix, which in the authors' words extends the approach of [MPZ18] (Madras, Pitassi, Zemel) to the multiclass setting, and shows (Proposition 3) that this L_mix is realizable (H, R)-consistent for classes closed under scaling (including linear models and neural networks) but not classification-consistent; which property matters more in practice is stated to be not immediately clear.([Inconsistency of mixtures of experts loss and Realizable-consistency, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.01862v3#page=8 "So far we have focused on classiﬁcation consistency to verify the soundness of proposed approaches, however, we usually have speciﬁc hypothesis classes H, R in mind, and if the Bayes predictor is not in our class then consistency might not guarantee much [BDLSS12]."))
- **c4** Failure mode of an alternative deferral loss (Mozannar & Sontag's finding about their own multiclass implementation of the [MPZ18] approach, not a result of Madras et al.): in the CIFAR-10 experiment with synthetic experts, the mixture-of-experts loss learns never to defer; the authors' explanation is that the classifier's training loss converges to zero while validation accuracy keeps improving, whereas the expert's loss stays constant.([Experiments: CIFAR-10, p.12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.01862v3#page=12 "the mixtures of experts loss of [MPZ18] fails in this setup and learns never to defer."))
- **c5** How the expert is obtained (Section 6): simulated throughout. CIFAR-10 uses synthetic experts that are perfect on the first k classes and uniform otherwise; CIFAR-100 and CheXpert use probabilistic synthetic experts; hate speech uses a synthetic expert whose probability of being correct depends on whether a language model predicts the tweet to be African-American English (AAE); CIFAR10H simulates an average human annotator by sampling from real crowdworker class counts.([Experiments: CIFAR-10, p.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.01862v3#page=11 "We simulate multiple synthetic experts of varying competence in the following way: let k ∈[10], then if the image belongs to the ﬁrst k classes the expert predicts perfectly, otherwise the expert predicts uniformly over all classes."))
- **c6** Fairness of the combined system (hate speech, synthetic experts): with the synthetic fair expert (correct with probability 0.9 on both AAE and non-AAE tweets), the confidence-score baseline doubles the system's discrimination (difference in false positive rates between AAE and non-AAE tweets) relative to the classifier alone; the proposed method does not achieve significantly lower discrimination than the baseline.([Hate Speech and Offensive Language Detection, p.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.01862v3#page=14 "While our method does not achieve signiﬁcantly lower discrimination than the baseline"))
- **c7** Input-noise robustness test (CheXpert, synthetic expert with q = 0.7, p = 1; main text, Section 6.6 'Further Analysis'): models trained on original X-rays are evaluated on X-rays with a randomly placed rectangle erased (area varied from 0.1 to 0.66). The text for Figure 8 reports that all methods' maximum achievable AU-ROC/AU-PR (averaged over the 5 tasks) degrade with the noise scale, and the gap between methods stays constant in AU-PR but diminishes in AU-ROC.([Synthetic Experts on CheXpert: Further Analysis (Impact of input noise), p.19](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.01862v3#page=19 "In the second experiment, we train with the original chest X-rays but evaluate with noisy X-rays with noise in the form of erasing a randomly placed rectangular region of the X-ray."))
