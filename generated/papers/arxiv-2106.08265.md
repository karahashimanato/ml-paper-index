## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Setting (cold-start, also called one-class classification): the model is fit using nominal (non-defective) example images only; the authors motivate it by industrial scenarios where normal imagery is easy to acquire but expected defect variations are costly and complicated to specify in full.([Introduction, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.08265v2#page=1 "It arises in many industrial scenarios where it is easy to acquire imagery of normal examples but costly and complicated to specify the expected defect variations in full."))
- **c2** Assumption about pretrained features: the last (deepest) feature level used by prior work loses localized nominal information and is biased towards natural image classification, which the authors say has only little overlap with cold-start industrial anomaly detection; PatchCore therefore uses intermediate (mid-level) features with local neighbourhood aggregation to enlarge the receptive field without losing spatial resolution.([Section 3.1, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.08265v2#page=3 "Secondly, very deep and abstract features in ImageNet pretrained networks are biased towards the task of natural image classiﬁcation, which has only little overlap with the cold-start industrial anomaly detection task and the evaluated data at hand."))
- **c3** Memory cost: the memory bank grows with the number of nominal images, making inference time and storage large; random subsampling would lose information about the coverage of nominal features, so a greedy coreset approximation (with random linear projections to speed up selection) is used, which the authors find reduces inference time while retaining performance.([Section 3.2, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.08265v2#page=4 "In this work we use a coreset subsampling mechanism to reduce M, which we ﬁnd reduces inference time while retaining performance."))
- **c4** Protocol: MVTec AD (15 sub-datasets, nominal-only training data, test sets with nominal and anomalous samples plus ground-truth masks), images resized to 256 and center-cropped to 224 as in prior work, and no data augmentation; metrics are class-averaged image-level AUROC, pixel-wise AUROC and PRO. MTD (20% of defect-free images held out for testing, following prior work) and mSTC are also used.([Section 4.1, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.08265v2#page=5 "No data augmentation is applied, as this requires prior knowledge about class-retaining augmentations."))
- **c5** Inference cost: times are compared against the authors' reimplementations of SPADE and PaDiM (WideResNet50, GPU where possible), including the backbone forward pass; PatchCore without subsampling is faster than SPADE, coreset subsampling makes it faster than PaDiM, and approximate nearest-neighbour search (IVFPQ) on the full memory bank causes a performance drop, especially at image level.([Section 4.3, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.08265v2#page=6 "With coreset subsampling, Patchcore can be made even faster, with lower inference times than even PaDiM while retaining state-of-the-art image-level anomaly detection and segmentation performance."))
- **c6** Hyperparameter choice: the neighbourhood size p = 3 and the feature hierarchy 2+3 (the default) are motivated by the ablation in Figure 4, which shows detection performance on MVTec AD over neighbourhood sizes and hierarchy levels; no separate validation split for this choice was found in the text (searched for validation, tuning, held out). The supplementary also suggests image resolution as something to validate over when localization matters.([Section 4.4, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.08265v2#page=7 "Results in the top half of Figure 4 show a clear optimum between locality and global context for patch-based anomaly predictions, thus motivating the neighbourhood size p = 3."))
- **c7** On mSTC (pedestrian videos), which the authors describe as much closer to ImageNet natural images, deeper feature maps (hierarchy levels 3 and 4) are used, with no other hyperparameter tuning; the authors say the results suggest good transferability to such domains.([Section 4.6, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.08265v2#page=8 "As the detection context is much closer to natural image data available in ImageNet, we make use of deeper network feature maps at hierarchy levels 3 and 4, but otherwise do not perform any hyperparameter tuning for PatchCore."))
- **c8** Threshold: AUROC is threshold-free; the reported misclassification counts use an F1-optimal threshold (working point), computed only for PatchCore because the authors did not have individual anomaly scores for competing methods. The remaining false positives are attributed mostly to labelling ambiguity and very high nominal variance.([Appendix C.4, p.12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.08265v2#page=12 "We compute the working point (threshold above which scores are considered anomalous) using the F1-optimal point."))
- **c9** Stated limitation: applicability is generally limited by the transferability of the pretrained features; the authors suggest combining PatchCore with adaptation of the features and leave this to future work.([Section 5 (Conclusion), Limitations, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.08265v2#page=8 "While PatchCore shows high effectiveness for industrial anomaly detection without the need to specifically adapt to the problem domain at hand, applicability is generally limited by the transferability of the pretrained features leveraged."))
