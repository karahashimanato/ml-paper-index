## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** The fundamental problem: data-splitting/cross-validation of prediction risk breaks down for treatment effect models because the true effect is never observed; the quantity of interest is the tau-risk E[l(tau-hat, tau)] (called PEHE by Hill), whose direct validation-set estimator is infeasible.([Introduction, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.05146v2#page=4 "This approach breaks down for treatment eﬀect estimation because the true treatment eﬀect is never observed in any sample."))
- **c2** mu-risk (validation-set prediction error of the outcome models, applicable when the effect model is built from outcome regressions, e.g. S- and T-learners) is easy to estimate, but citing Rolling and Yang the authors note many simple examples where minimizing outcome MSE badly fails to select the model with the most accurate treatment effect.([Section 2.1, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.05146v2#page=5 "There are many simple examples where minimizing the mean-squared error of predicted outcomes badly fails to select the model with the most accurate treatment eﬀect [24]."))
- **c3** Value-based selection: the IPTW value estimator (Kapelner et al.; Zhao et al.) depends on the correctness of the propensity model and only uses the individuals whose treatment matches the policy, discarding the rest; the authors note that Athey et al., in the spirit of Dudík et al., overcome this with a doubly robust estimator.([Section 2.2, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.05146v2#page=7 "A problem with this estimator is that it depends on the correctness of the propensity model."))
- **c4** Plug-in tau-risk estimates: Rolling and Yang's matching-based estimator scales relatively poorly and matches become difficult to find in high-dimensional covariate spaces; an alternative (Gutierrez and Gerardy) uses the IPTW transformed outcome as a plug-in for tau.([Section 2.3, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.05146v2#page=8 "A downside of this approach are that Mahalanobis matching scales relatively poorly and matches become diﬃcult to ﬁnd in high-dimensional covariate spaces."))
- **c5** tau-risk_R: following Nie and Wager, the validation-set metric is the mean of ((y - m(x)) - (w - p(x)) tau-hat(x))^2, with m(x) an estimate of E[Y|X] (regressing Y on X without W) and p(x) a propensity estimate; Nie and Wager used this objective for fitting, and the authors propose using it to select among models fit by arbitrary means.([Section 2.3, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.05146v2#page=8 "We propose using this same construction to select among models ﬁt by arbitrary means."))
- **c6** Protocol: the sixteen simulations of Powers et al., each repeated 100 times with 1000 training, 1000 validation and 1000 test samples; all DGPs satisfy ignorability, SUTVA and positivity and vary in nonlinearity and signal-to-noise ratio.([Section 3.1.1, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.05146v2#page=9 "The data-generating process in each simulation all satisfy the usual assumptions made about ignorability, SUTVA, and positivity, but vary in the amount of nonlinearity in the conditional mean functions and signal-to-noise ratio."))
- **c7** Result as stated in the text: tau-risk_R is the metric that most consistently selects models with low test tau-risk and high value, especially when treatment assignment is randomized but also to a large extent when it is biased; where other approaches win, it is typically by a small margin, and mu-risk and its IPTW version also perform well.([Section 3.2, p.12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.05146v2#page=12 "This is especially true when treatment assignment is randomized, but also to a large extent when the assignment is biased."))
- **c8** Selection on the IPTW or doubly robust value estimators is generally suboptimal even when the goal is to optimize test-set value, which the authors say is likely because these metrics are 'coarser', turning a regression problem into a classification problem; they nonetheless show low empirical bias for value, so they remain appropriate to gauge the value of the final policy.([Section 4, p.15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.05146v2#page=15 "This is likely because these metrics are in a sense “coarser” than the other metrics we consider, eﬀectively turning a regression problem into a classiﬁcation problem."))
- **c9** Limitation: the study relies on simulations, which is necessary because test tau-risk and value cannot be computed from real data; the authors say no set of simulations can mimic the full range of real-world DGPs, though theirs span linear and nonlinear outcome functions, noise levels and degrees of assignment bias.([Section 4, p.16](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.05146v2#page=16 "The primary limitation of our work is that it relies on simulations."))
