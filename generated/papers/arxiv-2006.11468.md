## 主張

- **c1** Definition 1: the edge homophily ratio h is the fraction of edges connecting nodes with the same class label (intra-class edges); graphs with strong homophily have h -> 1 and strong heterophily h -> 0. The authors stress that heterophily is distinct from heterogeneity (multiple node/edge types), and that homophily may vary across class pairs (captured by a class compatibility matrix in App. B).([Section 2, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.11468v2#page=2 "is the fraction of edges in a graph which connect nodes that have the same class label (i.e., intra-class edges)."))
- **c2** Motivating failure: on the synthetic syn-cora benchmark with h = 0.1, all existing GNNs tested fail to beat an MLP with 1 hidden layer that ignores the graph, and GCN and GAT are up to 42% worse than MLP, suggesting that methods that work well under high homophily may not be appropriate under low/medium homophily.([Section 3, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.11468v2#page=3 "Especially, GCN [17] and GAT [36] show up to 42% worse performance than MLP, highlighting that methods that work well under high homophily (h = 0.7) may not be appropriate for networks with low/medium homophily."))
- **c3** D1 and Theorem 1: under specific conditions (no self-loops, one-hot label features, equal class counts in training, all training nodes of degree d with a proportion h of same-class neighbors and the rest uniform over other classes, and h below a threshold depending on |Y| and d), a GCN layer (A + I)XW that co-embeds ego and neighbor features misclassifies under smaller train/test deviations than an AXW layer that separates them; the authors note the theorem applies to specific conditions and support more general cases empirically.([Section 3.1, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.11468v2#page=4 "We prove theoretically that, under some conditions, a GCN layer that co-embeds ego- and neighbor-features is less capable of generalizing to heterophily than a layer that embeds them separately."))
- **c4** D2 and Theorem 2: under heterophily the immediate neighborhood may be heterophily-dominant while higher-order neighborhoods may be homophily-dominant; assuming neighbors' labels are conditionally independent given y_v with P(y_u = y_v) = h and the rest uniform, the 2-hop neighborhood is always homophily-dominant in expectation.([Section 3.1, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.11468v2#page=4 "In the case of heterophily, we have seen empirically that although the immediate neighborhoods may be heterophily-dominant, the higher-order neighborhoods may be homophily-dominant and thus provide more relevant context."))
- **c5** D3 and Theorem 3: assuming GCN-style propagation acts as a low-pass filter (citing [37], the SGC paper), earlier rounds keep more high-frequency components; for label signals with edge homophily h_s < h_t, s has more energy in the high-frequency components of the unnormalized Laplacian spectrum, so combining intermediate representations (as in jumping knowledge networks) captures both low- and high-frequency information.([Section 3.1, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.11468v2#page=5 "In other words, in heterophily settings, the label distribution contains more information at higher than lower frequencies (see proof in App. C.3)."))
- **c6** H2GCN: (S1) a graph-agnostic dense embedding r_v^(0) = sigma(x_v W_e); (S2) K rounds where r_v^(k) concatenates aggregations over the 1-hop and 2-hop neighborhoods excluding v, each a degree-normalized sum with weights d_{v,i}^(-1/2) d_{u,i}^(-1/2), without per-round nonlinear transformations as in SGC (found to work better); (S3) softmax classification of the concatenation of r^(0)..r^(K). Aggregation costs O(2^K (|E| + |E2|) p) plus O(|E| d_max) to derive 2-hop neighborhoods.([Section 3.2, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.11468v2#page=6 "We found that removing the usual nonlinear transformations per round, as in SGC [37], works better (App. D.2), in which case we only need to include the ego-embedding in the ﬁnal representation."))
- **c7** Evaluation protocol and a split discrepancy: synthetic graphs (syn-cora, syn-products, 3 graphs per h) use 25%/25%/50% per-class splits; real benchmarks (except Cora-Full) use the features, labels and 10 random splits provided by Geom-GCN [26], which the authors report as 48%/32%/20%, noting that [26] claims 60%/20%/20%, different from the splits shared on GitHub; Cora-Full uses 3 random 25%/25%/50% splits. All models are tuned on the same train/validation splits (App. F).([Section 5.2, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.11468v2#page=8 "claims that the ratios are 60%/20%/20%, which is different from the actual data splits shared on GitHub."))
- **c8** On real benchmarks, MLP is a competitive baseline under heterophily (average rank 6.2), which the authors read as many existing models not using graph information effectively or the graph being misleading; all models perform poorly on Squirrel and Actor 'likely due to' low-quality node features, and Squirrel and Chameleon are dense with many nodes sharing the same neighbors. GEOM-GCN ranks are taken from its paper rather than re-run.([Section 5.2, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.11468v2#page=9 "We note that MLP is a competitive baseline under heterophily (ranked 6.2), indicating that many existing models do not use the graph information effectively, or the latter is misleading in such cases."))
- **c9** Data limitations: Cora, Pubmed and Citeseer all have h >= 0.7, which the authors say masked the limitations of the homophily assumption, and the OGB datasets they examined have h > 0.5; the heterophily benchmarks used also have limitations - small sizes (WebKB), artificial classes (Squirrel and Chameleon labels from page-traffic ranking) and unusual dense structure.([Appendix H, p.30](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.11468v2#page=30 "As a result, the wide adaptation of these benchmarks have masked the limitations of the homophily assumption in many existing GNN models."))
