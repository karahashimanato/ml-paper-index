## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Choice of acquisition: the paper focuses on expected improvement because it has been shown to be better-behaved than probability of improvement and, unlike GP-UCB, needs no tuning parameter of its own; the authors add that the regret formalization is more appropriate for many settings.([Section 2.2, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.2944v2#page=3 "In this work we will focus on the expected improvement criterion, as it has been shown to be better-behaved than probability of improvement, but unlike the method of GP upper conﬁdence bounds (GP-UCB), it does not require its own tuning parameter."))
- **c2** Kernel: the ARD squared exponential kernel, often a default for GP regression, gives sample functions that are unrealistically smooth for practical optimization problems; the authors instead propose the ARD Matern 5/2 kernel, whose samples are twice differentiable.([Section 3.1, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.2944v2#page=4 "However, sample functions with this covariance function are unrealistically smooth for practical optimization problems."))
- **c3** GP hyperparameters: typically D + 3 of them (D length scales, amplitude, observation noise, constant mean). Instead of the commonly advocated point estimate by maximizing the marginal likelihood, the acquisition is integrated over the GP hyperparameter posterior, estimated by Monte Carlo with slice sampling. The authors argue this is sensible because both optimization and MCMC are dominated by the cubic cost of solving an N-dimensional linear system and function evaluations are assumed to be much more expensive.([Section 3.1, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.2944v2#page=5 "As both optimization and Markov chain Monte Carlo are computationally dominated by the cubic cost of solving an N-dimensional linear system"))
- **c4** Cost-aware acquisition: because wallclock time matters more than the number of evaluations, the authors propose expected improvement per second; the unknown duration function is modelled as ln c(x) with a GP, and it is assumed independent of the objective, although the coupling may be captured with multi-task GP variants.([Section 3.2, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.2944v2#page=6 "In this work, we assume that these functions are independent of each other, although their coupling may be usefully captured using GP variants of multi-task learning (e.g., Teh et al. (2005); Bonilla et al. (2008))."))
- **c5** Parallel BO: roll-outs of the acquisition policy are generally intractable, so the next point is chosen while other evaluations are pending by computing Monte Carlo estimates of the acquisition over possible outcomes of the pending evaluations (a J-dimensional Gaussian under the GP).([Section 3.3, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.2944v2#page=6 "Instead we propose a sequential strategy that takes advantage of the tractable inference properties of the Gaussian process to compute Monte Carlo estimates of the acquisiton function under diﬀerent possible results from pending function evaluations."))
- **c6** Evaluation protocol for online LDA (3 parameters; the original authors' 6 x 6 x 8 grid of 288 configurations, each evaluation taking 5 to 10 hours): the BO strategies were restricted to the exact grid points, each optimization was repeated 100 times with two random initial experiments, and mean and standard error are reported against evaluations and days. The authors state that integrating over GP hyperparameters was superior to a point estimate, and that parallelized GP EI MCMC found the best parameters in significantly less time though single GP EI MCMC was most efficient per evaluation.([Section 4.2, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.2944v2#page=8 "That is, the algorithms were restricted to only the exact parameter settings as evaluated by the grid search."))
- **c7** Kernel comparison on structured SVM (M3E) motif finding, 100 repetitions per covariance: the authors state that the choice of covariance significantly affects performance, that length-scale estimation is critical, and that the infinite differentiability imposed by the squared exponential is too restrictive for this problem.([Section 4.3, p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.2944v2#page=10 "The assumption of the inﬁnite diﬀerentiability of the underlying function as imposed by the commonly used squared exponential is too restrictive for this problem."))
- **c8** Cost-aware vs per-evaluation efficiency on M3E (grid of 1,400 combinations, each evaluated over 5 random 50-50 train/test splits, BO constrained to grid points, 100 repetitions): GP EI MCMC is better per function evaluation, while GP EI per Second finds better parameters faster in wallclock time because it learns to use a less strict convergence tolerance early on.([Section 4.3, p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.2944v2#page=10 "In this case, GP EI MCMC is superior to GP EI per Second in terms of function evaluations but GP EI per Second ﬁnds better parameters faster than GP EI MCMC as it learns to use a less strict convergence tolerance early on while exploring the other parameters."))
- **c9** CIFAR-10 protocol: nine hyperparameters of a three-layer convolutional network (Krizhevsky's code; the expert-tuned setting gives 18% test error) are optimized on a withheld validation set; mean validation error and standard error over five randomly initialized runs are reported, and the best hyperparameters found by GP EI MCMC give 14.98% test error, which the authors describe as over 3% better than the expert and the state of the art (without data augmentation).([Section 4.4, p.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.2944v2#page=11 "We optimize over the nine parameters for each strategy on a withheld validation set and report the mean validation error and standard error over ﬁve separate randomly initialized runs."))
- **c10** Notation in Section 2.2 (minimization): x_best = argmin_{x_n} f(x_n), Phi is the standard normal CDF and phi the standard normal density; acquisition functions depend on the GP only through the predictive mean mu(x) and predictive variance sigma^2(x).([Section 2.2, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.2944v2#page=3 "In the proceeding, we will denote the best current value as xbest = argminxn f(xn), Φ(·) will denote the cumulative distribution function of the standard normal, and φ(·) will denote the standard normal density function."))
- **c11** Probability of improvement (Eq. 1): a_PI(x) = Phi(gamma(x)) with gamma(x) = (f(x_best) - mu(x)) / sigma(x).([Section 2.2, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.2944v2#page=3 "One intuitive strategy is to maximize the probability of improving over the best current value (Kushner, 1964)."))
- **c12** Expected improvement in closed form under the GP (Eq. 2): a_EI(x) = sigma(x) (gamma(x) Phi(gamma(x)) + N(gamma(x); 0, 1)).([Section 2.2, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.2944v2#page=3 "This also has closed form under the Gaussian process:"))
- **c13** GP lower confidence bound (Eq. 3; upper when maximizing): a_LCB(x) = mu(x) - kappa sigma(x), with a tunable kappa to balance exploitation against exploration.([Section 2.2, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1206.2944v2#page=3 "with a tunable κ to balance exploitation against exploration."))
