## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Setup: the target is tau(x) = mu1(x) - mu0(x); under no unmeasured confounding, consistency and positivity/overlap (assumed throughout) it equals the CATE E(Y^1 - Y^0 | X = x). The author notes the results hold regardless of whether the causal assumptions do (i.e., for the difference in regression functions).([Section 2, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.14497v5#page=2 "noting that our results hold regardless of whether the causal assumptions do"))
- **c2** Motivating illustration: when mu1 and mu0 are individually complex and non-smooth but their difference is zero, and treated/untreated units are unevenly spread over x, separately fit regressions over- and under-smooth in different regions, so the plug-in difference is a poor, overly complex CATE estimate; the author calls this an interesting but likely common phenomenon. Pseudo-outcome regressions (IPW, doubly robust) adapt to the CATE's smoothness, with the doubly robust versions more efficient.([Section 2.2, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.14497v5#page=3 "An interesting but likely common phenomenon occurs in this simple example."))
- **c3** The author states that the meta-learners of Künzel et al. (2019) do not include the DR-Learner and none of them are doubly robust, so in general they would inherit larger error rates from the underlying regression estimators.([Section 4.1, p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.14497v5#page=10 "further, none of their methods are doubly robust, and so in general would inherit larger error rates from the underlying regression estimators."))
- **c4** DR-Learner construction (Algorithm 1): estimate the propensity and both outcome regressions on one sample, form the pseudo-outcome (A - pi_hat)/(pi_hat(1 - pi_hat)) (Y - mu_hat_A) + mu_hat_1 - mu_hat_0, regress it on X in an independent sample, and optionally cross-fit; the motivation is that this is the uncentered efficient influence function of the ATE, so where the standard doubly robust ATE estimator averages it, the DR-Learner regresses it on covariates.([Section 4.2, p.12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.14497v5#page=12 "The DR-Learner approach is motivated by the fact that (2) is the (uncentered) efficient influence function for the ATE [Hahn, 1998, Robins and Rotnitzky, 1995]; this drives many of its favorable properties."))
- **c5** Theorem 2: if the second-stage regression is stable (Definition 1) and the estimated pseudo-outcome is consistent, the DR-Learner differs from an oracle that regresses the true pseudo-outcome by a smoothed product of propensity and outcome-regression errors (plus a term of smaller order than the oracle risk), so it is oracle efficient when that product term is small; the author states the result is agnostic about the methods and needs no special tuning or undersmoothing, and contrasts it with Foster & Syrgkanis (2019), whose bounds are second-order but not doubly robust (L4 errors of all nuisances).([Section 4.2, p.12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.14497v5#page=12 "Importantly the result is agnostic about the methods used, and requires no special tuning or undersmoothing."))
- **c6** Corollary 1 (Hölder-smooth propensity alpha, regressions beta, CATE gamma; minimax-optimal linear smoother second stage): the DR-Learner adapts to CATE smoothness, with a sufficient condition for oracle efficiency that approaches the ATE root-n condition sqrt(alpha beta) >= d/2 as the CATE becomes smoother; the author contrasts this with Nie & Wager and Zimmert & Lechner, which required ATE-like n^(-1/4) nuisance rates.([Section 4.3, p.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.14497v5#page=14 "First, as the CATE smoothness gets larger, the suﬃcient condition (4) for the CATE approaches that for the ATE, as should be expected (intuitively, an inﬁnitely smooth CATE should be nearly as easy to estimate as the ATE)."))
- **c7** Caveat for the sparse-model analogue of Corollary 1: the author notes that it has not been proved that second-stage estimators attaining the stated sparse rates satisfy the stability condition.([Section 4.3, p.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.14497v5#page=14 "However we note that we have not proved second-stage estimators attaining these rates satisfy the stability condition of Deﬁnition 1."))
- **c8** Simulations (plug-in/T-learner, X-learner, DR-Learner, oracle DR-Learner): in the piecewise-polynomial model (n = 2000, 500 simulations, propensity estimates with controlled convergence rate, truncated to [0.01, 0.99]) the DR-Learner's MSE approaches the oracle's as the propensity error shrinks, with the X-learner between plug-in and DR; in a high-dimensional sparse logistic model with zero CATE (d = 500, alpha = beta = 50, n = 200 and 2000, 100 simulations, cross-validated lasso, median MSE reported), the DR-Learner is closer to the oracle and its relative performance seems to improve with sample size.([Section 4.4, p.15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.14497v5#page=15 "The relative performance of the DR-Learner seems to improve with sample size."))
- **c9** Limits: the lp-R-Learner attains oracle efficiency under weaker nuisance-smoothness conditions than the DR-Learner bound in a Hölder model (but is harder to compute and requires undersmoothing, Remark 8); the abstract conjectures that these conditions are minimal in a minimax sense, but the Discussion states that whether the rates of Corollaries 2 and 3 are minimax optimal is one of the deepest open questions.([Section 6, p.22](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.14497v5#page=22 "One of the deepest open questions is whether the rates given in Corollaries 2 and 3 are minimax optimal or not (in the ﬁxed and random design setups, respectively)."))
