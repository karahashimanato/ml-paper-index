## 主張

- **c1** Instance-level detection: with guided instruction (dataset name, partition type and a random-length initial segment of a reference instance), an instance is flagged as contaminated if the LLM's output exactly or nearly matches the latter segment of the reference.([Abstract, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.08493v3#page=1 "An instance is ﬂagged as contaminated if the LLM’s output either exactly or nearly matches the latter segment of the reference."))
- **c2** Stated caveat of the replication idea: because of the probabilistic behavior of LLMs, perfect replicas are not always produced even when contamination is certain, and because many datasets draw from web sources, partial replicas can also arise by happenstance; the authors therefore say consistent and significant signs within a partition must be checked.([Approach, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.08493v3#page=3 "Note that, due to the inherent probabilistic behavior of LLMs, achieving perfect replicas is not always possible even when contamination is certain."))
- **c3** Algorithm 1 labels a partition as contaminated if the average BLEURT/ROUGE-L overlap of guided-instruction completions on a sample of ten instances is statistically significantly better than that of general-instruction completions (non-parametric bootstrap); its stated advantage is not needing an arbitrary score threshold, and its stated drawback is that a significant increase in overlap may still come from completions a human would not consider exact or near-exact matches.([Approach (Detecting Partition-Level Contamination), p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.08493v3#page=5 "However, its drawback is that even a signiﬁcant increase in overlap may still come from generated instances that a human would not consider an exact or near-exact match."))
- **c4** Algorithm 2 rule (Idea 2): a partition is likely contaminated if GPT-4 with few-shot ICL prompting detects at least one exact match or at least two near-exact matches (among ten guided-instruction completions); the authors say the number of matches indicating broader contamination can be tailored to research objectives and that they established this criterion intuitively, with an empirical validation in Section 3.3.([Approach (Detecting Partition-Level Contamination), p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.08493v3#page=5 "In this paper, we intuitively establish the above-mentioned criterion to extrapolate from the instance-level to the partition-level contamination."))
- **c5** Validation (Section 3.3): the GPT-3.5 base model was intentionally contaminated by continued training (OpenAI fine-tuning API, default hyperparameters, 100 label-balanced training instances each from AG News, RTE and XSum, formatted with dataset title and partition name) on partitions human evaluators had marked uncontaminated; the contaminated model generated at least one exact match in each setting. A second check on GPT-4 with the GSM8k train split (reported as contaminated in OpenAI's technical report) motivated the threshold of two near-exact matches.([Section 3.3, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.08493v3#page=6 "The key conclusion of this experiment is that the contaminated LLM generated at least one exact match in each setting."))
- **c6** Protocol: seven datasets (IMDB, AG News, Yelp Full Reviews, SAMSum, XSum, WNLI, RTE), train and test splits (validation splits for WNLI and RTE), 10 randomly chosen instances per split; GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 snapshots gpt-3.5-turbo-0613 and gpt-4-0613 via the OpenAI API with temperature 0 and at most 500 completion tokens; the compared ChatGPT-Cheat? method (Sainz et al., 2023) was run in the chat UI versions released July 20, 2023.([Section 4, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.08493v3#page=7 "To obtain deterministic results, we set the temperature to zero and capped the maximum completion length at 500 tokens."))
- **c7** Ground truth is a human evaluation led by two domain experts who label exact and near-exact matches (completions with considerable overlap and significant semantic and structural similarity) and apply the same partition rule as Algorithm 2; a footnote states the two annotators had almost perfect inter-rater agreement, attributed to the small number of instances and contamination being evident when it occurs. No agreement statistic was found in the text (searched for kappa, agreement).([Section 4, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.08493v3#page=7 "The two annotators had almost perfect inter-rater agreement across all settings."))
- **c8** Over 28 settings (2 LLMs x 14 partitions), Algorithm 2 (GPT-4 ICL) matched the human labels in 14/14 partitions for GPT-4 and 13/14 for GPT-3.5, higher than Algorithm 1, whose success varied with the metric and was at chance level with ROUGE-L on GPT-3.5 outputs (7/14).([Section 5, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.08493v3#page=8 "Speciﬁcally, in experiments run on GPT-4 and GPT-3.5, its success rates are 14/14 and 13/14, respectively."))
- **c9** Stated limitation: the best method (guided instruction with GPT-4 ICL) does not distinguish between types of contamination (e.g. direct inclusion of instances vs metadata contamination), treating both exact and near-exact replicas as indicators of contamination.([Conclusion (footnote: Limitations), p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.08493v3#page=9 "Our best-performing method for detecting contamination (guided instruction with GPT-4 ICL) does not distinguish between different types of contamination,"))
