## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Upper bound (Lemma 2): if a GNN maps two non-isomorphic graphs to different embeddings, the WL test also decides they are not isomorphic; hence any aggregation-based GNN is at most as powerful as the WL test in distinguishing graphs. The WL test is described as a heuristic that works well in general with a few exceptions, e.g. regular graphs.([Section 4, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.00826v3#page=4 "Hence, any aggregation-based GNN is at most as powerful as the WL test in distinguishing different graphs."))
- **c2** Theorem 3: with a sufficient number of layers, a GNN maps any two graphs that the WL test decides are non-isomorphic to different embeddings if (a) it updates node features as h_v^(k) = phi(h_v^(k-1), f({h_u^(k-1) : u in N(v)})) with f (on multisets) and phi injective, and (b) its graph-level readout over the multiset of node features is injective.([Section 4, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.00826v3#page=4 "if the neighbor aggregation and graph-level readout functions are injective, then the resulting GNN is as powerful as the WL test."))
- **c3** Assumption of the analysis: input node features are taken from a countable set; the authors state that uncountable (continuous) node features need further considerations, which they leave for future work.([Section 4, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.00826v3#page=4 "Uncountable sets, where node features are continuous, need some further considerations."))
- **c4** GIN layer: Lemma 5 (extending Zaheer et al. (2017) from sets to multisets) states that sum aggregators can represent injective, indeed universal, multiset functions for countable inputs, whereas the mean aggregator, although an injective set function, is not an injective multiset function. Corollary 6 gives (1 + eps) f(c) + sum_{x in X} f(x) as injective for infinitely many eps (including all irrational numbers); f and phi are modeled with MLPs, giving h_v^(k) = MLP^(k)((1 + eps^(k)) h_v^(k-1) + sum_{u in N(v)} h_u^(k-1)) with eps learnable or fixed.([Section 4.1, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.00826v3#page=5 "Our next lemma states that sum aggregators can represent injective, in fact, universal functions over multisets."))
- **c5** Graph readout of GIN: because features from earlier iterations may sometimes generalize better, the graph representation concatenates READOUT over all iterations k = 0..K (similar to Jumping Knowledge Networks); with sum readout, GIN is stated to provably generalize the WL test and the WL subtree kernel.([Section 4.2, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.00826v3#page=5 "To consider all structural information, we use information from all depths/iterations of the model."))
- **c6** 1-layer perceptrons are not sufficient: Lemma 7 shows there exist different finite multisets whose sums of ReLU(Wx) coincide for any linear map W (the proof relies on the missing bias term); the authors note that with a bias and large output dimension 1-layer perceptrons might distinguish multisets, but they are not universal approximators of multiset functions.([Section 5.1, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.00826v3#page=6 "Lemma 7 suggests that there are indeed network neighborhoods (multisets) that models with 1-layer perceptrons can never distinguish."))
- **c7** Mean and max-pooling aggregators (as in GCN and GraphSAGE) are permutation invariant but not injective: with identical node features, mean and max yield the same representation everywhere and capture no structural information, while sum distinguishes them; mean captures the distribution (proportions) of a multiset (Corollary 8) and max only the underlying set (Corollary 9), giving the ranking sum > mean > max.([Section 5.2, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.00826v3#page=7 "Mean and max-pooling aggregators are still well-deﬁned multiset functions because they are permutation invariant."))
- **c8** The authors suggest that the mean aggregator may perform well when distributional information matters more than exact structure, and that when node features are diverse and rarely repeat it is as powerful as sum; they say this may explain why mean-aggregation GNNs are effective for node classification tasks with rich node features.([Section 5.3, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.00826v3#page=7 "Moreover, when node features are diverse and rarely repeat, the mean aggregator is as powerful as the sum aggregator."))
- **c9** Evaluation protocol: 9 graph classification benchmarks (MUTAG, PTC, NCI1, PROTEINS, COLLAB, IMDB-BINARY, IMDB-MULTI, REDDIT-BINARY, REDDIT-MULTI5K), 10-fold cross-validation following Yanardag & Vishwanathan (2015) and Niepert et al. (2016); reported numbers are the mean and standard deviation of validation accuracies across the 10 folds, with hyperparameters (hidden units, batch size, dropout) and the single epoch with the best cross-validation accuracy averaged over folds selected per dataset; a separate validation set was avoided as 'extremely unstable' (MUTAG's would have only 18 data points).([Section 7, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1810.00826v3#page=9 "We report the average and standard deviation of validation accuracies across the 10 folds within the cross-validation."))
