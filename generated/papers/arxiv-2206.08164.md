## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Motivation: an L-layer message-passing GNN aggregates information from up to L hops, but the L-hop neighborhood grows exponentially, leading to 'information oversquashing' (citing Alon and Yahav [2]), so such GNNs fail to capture long-range dependencies as distant information may get lost.([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.08164v4#page=2 "Consequently, such GNNs fail at capturing long-range dependencies as a significant amount of distant information may get lost due to the squashing."))
- **c2** Critique of existing benchmarks: Graph Transformers are often evaluated on datasets (e.g. ZINC, ogbg-molpcba, ogbg-molhiv) whose tasks primarily rely on local structural information, and the leaderboards of these benchmarks are topped by local MP-GNN based models augmented with higher-order structural information.([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.08164v4#page=2 "However, it is often the case that these models are evaluated on datasets where the corresponding tasks primarily rely on local structural information rather than the distant information propagation between nodes."))
- **c3** Graph size as a factor: with small graphs such as ogbg-mol* or ZINC (average 23-26 nodes), the problem radius is effectively small, so local MP-GNNs would succeed largely without being influenced by the bottleneck; the authors conclude LRI benchmarks need sufficiently large graphs, but note size alone may not be enough since the problem radius can be small even in large graphs.([Section 2, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.08164v4#page=3 "Therefore, a direct conclusion of this condition is that for a LRI benchmark, the graph sizes should be sufficiently large in order to separate the local MP-GNNs’ performance from those models which model LRIs."))
- **c4** The authors state they do not aim at 'provable LRI' benchmarks (which would often be toy datasets) but at real-world datasets whose characterizing factors suggest that the tasks would depend on long-range signal propagation.([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.08164v4#page=2 "Note that our characterization henceforth is not directed at proposing ‘provable LRI’ benchmarks, which would often lead to toy datasets (that are useful for quick prototyping of ideas) such as the shortest path prediction task [55] or the color connectivity dataset [50] which rely on LRI."))
- **c5** PCQM-Contact task: predict pairs of atoms more than 5 hops apart in the molecular graph that are in 3D contact (distance below 3.5 Å), framed as inductive link prediction with filtered ranking metrics (Hits@1, Hits@3, MRR); the dataset is split randomly 90/5/5.([Section 3.3, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.08164v4#page=6 "The task is to predict pairs of distant nodes (more than 5 hops away from each other in a molecule graph) that will be contacting with each other in the 3D space, i.e., the 3D distance between atoms will be smaller than 3.5Å."))
- **c6** Experimental setup: hyperparameters of all baselines are chosen to stay within a budget of approximately 500k learnable parameters (4-8 layers, hidden size adjusted), each experiment is run 4 times with different seeds, Adam with default settings and a starting learning rate between 0.0003 and 0.001 depending on model and dataset, and training is limited to 60h (SAN did not converge on COCO-SP within that limit).([Appendix C, p.22](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.08164v4#page=22 "We run each experiment 4 times with different random seeds and report the mean and standard deviation of the respective performance metrics."))
- **c7** Findings: simple local MP-GNNs (GCN, GINE) perform poorly on all datasets except Peptides-func, consistent with Alon and Yahav's finding that GCN and GIN suffer more from over-squashing than GAT; shallow MP-GNNs (L = 2) significantly underfit compared with deeper ones, unlike classical benchmarks such as Cora or Citeseer where shallow GCNs fared better.([Section 4.2, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.08164v4#page=9 "This is consistent with the empirical findings in [2] where GCN and GIN suffer from over-squashing to a greater extent than GAT, an attention based MP-GNN [60]."))
- **c8** Transformers on the fully connected graph rank among the best baselines except GatedGCN vs. Transformer on PascalVOC-SP/COCO-SP, with the gap seeming most distinct on the Peptides datasets; positional encoding alone (GatedGCN with LapPE or RWSE) contributes little or no gain; and O(N^2) Transformers may be computationally inefficient to scale.([Section 4.2, p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.08164v4#page=10 "First, the use of positional encoding alone contributes to little or no gain in performance on the proposed datasets."))

(2023) for a reassessment of the original baselines on all the datasets, and to a community leaderboard.([Section 4.1, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.08164v4#page=8 "For a reassessment of the original baselines on all the datasets as well as extensions of PCQM-Contact’s metric, we refer to the paper [58] by Tönshoff et al., 2023."))
