## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Method: ATC learns a threshold on model confidence and predicts target accuracy as the fraction of unlabeled target examples whose confidence exceeds it, using only labeled source data and unlabeled target data.([Abstract, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.04234v3#page=1 "We propose Average Thresholded Conﬁdence (ATC), a practical method that learns a threshold on the model’s conﬁdence, predicting accuracy as the fraction of unlabeled examples for which model conﬁdence exceeds that threshold."))
- **c2** Threshold choice: the threshold is chosen on held-out source validation data so that the fraction of source examples scoring above it matches their accuracy; the same threshold is then applied to the target (the authors note a different target threshold would in principle exist, but the source one worked well in their experiments).([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.04234v3#page=2 "ATC selects a threshold on validation source data such that the fraction of source examples that receive the score above the threshold match the accuracy of those examples."))
- **c3** Impossibility statement (theory, Section 3.1): the setting is knowledge of the full labeled source distribution p_s(x,y) and the unlabeled target marginal p_t(x), with no assumptions on the classifier f (f may be any classifier on X). Proposition 1: absent further assumptions, target accuracy is identifiable iff p_t(y|x) is uniquely identified given p_s(x,y) and p_t(x) (e.g. it is, under covariate shift with target support inside source support, or under label shift). Corollary 1: absent assumptions on the classifier f, no method of estimating accuracy works for all natures of distribution shift. Example 1 (binary Gaussian mixture) shows the same p_s(x,y), p_t(x) give different target errors under covariate shift vs label shift, so a method consistent under one is wrong under the other.([Accuracy estimation: possibility and impossibility results, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.04234v3#page=5 "Corollary 1. Absent assumptions on the classiﬁer f, no method of estimating accuracy will work in all scenarios, i.e., for different nature of distribution shifts."))
- **c4** Stated limitation following from the theory: ATC, like all methods, must fail (give inconsistent estimates) on certain types of distribution shift. The main-text positive guarantee (Theorem 1, informal) is for a toy setting: binary classification with an invariant feature and a spurious feature, a linear sigmoid classifier with w_inv > 0, the max-confidence score, and target shift only in the degree of spurious correlation; there, ATC is consistent. In that same toy model, shrinking the support of the target class-conditional p_t(x_inv|y) can make ATC over-estimate target accuracy. Appendix D.1 is said to give a more general (less interpretable) classifier-dependent shift condition under which ATC is consistent.([Introduction, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.04234v3#page=3 "Finally, we note that although ATC achieves superior performance in our empirical evaluation, like all methods, it must fail (returns inconsistent estimates) on certain types of distribution shifts, per our impossibility result."))
- **c5** Evaluation protocol: metric is mean absolute error between true target accuracy and the estimate on the same unlabeled examples (target labels used only for the true value); models use standard training with benchmarked hyperparameters (the reproducibility statement says no hyperparameter tuning was done); for all methods, temperature-scaling calibration is fitted on source validation data, and results are reported both before and after it; baselines are AC, DOC (the DOC-Feat variant of Guillory et al.), importance re-weighting (IM) and GDE; MAE aggregated over 4 seeds.([Experiments, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.04234v3#page=6 "To compare methods, we report average absolute difference between the true accuracy on the target data and the estimated accuracy on the same unlabeled examples."))
- **c6** Where it works and fails: MAE is small on BREEDS shifts from the same subpopulation, but on shifts with novel subpopulations MAE is significantly higher for all methods (ATC still best among them); the conclusion repeats that ATC still suffers large estimation error on novel-population datasets such as BREEDS. Shift families tested: synthetic corruptions (ImageNet-C, CIFAR-10-C/100-C), dataset reproduction (ImageNetV2, CIFARv2), renditions/sketch, BREEDS subpopulations, WILDS, MNIST to USPS/SVHN/QMNIST.([Results, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.04234v3#page=8 "While we observe a small MAE (i.e., comparable to our observations on other datasets) on BREEDS with natural and synthetic shifts from the same sub-population, MAE on shifts with novel population is signiﬁcantly higher with all methods."))
- **c7** Garg et al.'s critique and re-evaluation of prior work (Deng et al.; Guillory et al.'s DoC with regression): in this paper's view, methods that fit a regression on labeled target domains rely on unseen target domains being strongly linearly correlated with the seen ones on the distance measure, and can be rendered ineffective otherwise. Garg et al.'s own re-evaluation (Section 5.1, BREEDS): they observe a poor linear correlation across subpopulations (Living-17 shown); they fit a robust (Siegel) linear regression for DOC 'as in Guillory et al.' on labeled natural and synthetic shifts of the source subpopulations, evaluate on shifts with novel subpopulations, and find the fitted DOC improves over unfitted DOC while ATC without any regression performs better or similar. This is Garg et al.'s result in their setting, not a finding of Guillory et al.([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2201.04234v3#page=2 "methods that leverage labeled data from target domains rely on the fact that unseen target domains exhibit strong linear correlation with seen target domains on the underlying distance measure"))
