## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Motivation: predictive probabilities from the softmax are often erroneously interpreted as model confidence; a model can be uncertain in its predictions even with a high softmax output, and passing a point estimate through a softmax gives unjustifiably high confidence far from the training data.([Section 1, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.02142v6#page=1 "A model can be uncertain in its predictions even with a high softmax output (ﬁg. 1)."))
- **c2** Core result: a NN of arbitrary depth and non-linearities with dropout applied before every weight layer is mathematically equivalent to an approximation to the deep Gaussian process (marginalised over its covariance function parameters); the dropout objective in effect minimises the KL divergence between an approximate distribution (weight matrices with randomly zeroed columns) and the deep GP posterior. The derivation is deferred to a separate appendix document.([Section 3, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.02142v6#page=2 "We show that a neural network with arbitrary depth and non-linearities, with dropout applied before every weight layer, is mathematically equivalent to an approximation to the probabilistic deep Gaussian process"))
- **c3** MC dropout estimates the first two moments of the approximate predictive distribution by moment matching: the predictive mean is the average of T stochastic forward passes, and the predictive variance is the sample variance of the passes plus the inverse model precision tau^-1, where tau is obtained from the weight decay, dropout probability and an assumed prior length-scale.([Section 4, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.02142v6#page=4 "In practice this is equivalent to performing T stochastic forward passes through the network and averaging the results."))
- **c4** Hedge: the approximate predictive distribution is expected to be highly multi-modal (the variational distribution over each weight-matrix column is bi-modal), so the mean/variance approximations only give a glimpse into its properties.([Section 4, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.02142v6#page=4 "and the above approximations only give a glimpse into its properties"))
- **c5** Cost as stated by the authors: the dropout model itself is not changed, so the method can be used with existing networks trained with dropout; the forward passes can be done concurrently, giving constant running time identical to standard dropout. T = 1000 passes were used in the regression figures for drawing purposes, and the authors state much smaller numbers (e.g. T = 10) give a reasonable estimate.([Section 4, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.02142v6#page=4 "Furthermore, the forward passes can be done concurrently, resulting in constant running time identical to that of standard dropout."))
- **c6** The uncertainty depends on modelling choices: on the CO2 extrapolation task, the uncertainty grows far from the data for the ReLU model but stays bounded for the TanH model, which the authors attribute to ReLU and TanH approximating different GP covariance functions; none of the models capture the periodicity.([Section 5.1, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.02142v6#page=5 "Note that the uncertainty is increasing far from the data for the ReLU model, whereas for the TanH model it stays bounded."))
- **c7** UCI regression protocol (replicating the PBP setup): 1-hidden-layer networks of 50 units, dropout probabilities 0.05 and 0.005, prior length-scale set to 10^-2 for most datasets based on the range of the data, and the model precision tau chosen by Bayesian optimisation over validation log-likelihood; 20 random splits (5 for Protein, 1 for Year). After BO, 10x more iterations were used because dropout takes longer to converge.([Section 5.3, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.02142v6#page=7 "over validation log-likelihood to ﬁnd optimal τ"))
- **c8** Result as stated in the text: dropout significantly outperforms VI and PBP in RMSE and test log-likelihood on all datasets apart from Yacht, where PBP obtains better RMSE. MC dropout averaging also improved over standard dropout weight averaging and over near-zero dropout probabilities.([Section 5.3, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.02142v6#page=7 "Dropout signiﬁcantly outperforms all other models both in terms of RMSE as well as test log-likelihood on all datasets apart from Yacht, for which PBP obtains better RMSE."))
- **c9** Correction (October 2016 update): in an earlier version the reported dropout standard errors were erroneously scaled up by a factor of 4.5 (e.g. Boston RMSE standard error 0.85 instead of 0.19).([Section 5.3 (footnote 3), p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.02142v6#page=7 "Note that in an earlier version of this paper our reported dropout standard error was erroneously scaled-up by a factor of 4.5"))
