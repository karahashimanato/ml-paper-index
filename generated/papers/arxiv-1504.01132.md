## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Definition of honesty: a model is honest if it does not use the same information for selecting the model structure (here the partition of the covariate space) and for estimation given that structure; this is done by splitting the training sample into one part for building the tree (including cross-validation) and another for estimating leaf treatment effects. The authors present this as an alternative to sparsity assumptions that places no restrictions on model complexity.([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.01132v3#page=2 "We say that a model is “honest” if it does not use the same information for selecting the model structure (in our case, the partition of the covariate space) and for estimation given a model structure."))
- **c2** Trade-off of honesty: sample splitting loses precision because each estimation step uses fewer units, but there is a benefit for fit from eliminating bias that offsets at least part of that cost.([Introduction, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.01132v3#page=3 "Although there is a loss of precision due to sample splitting (which reduces sample size in each step of estimation), there is a beneﬁt for ﬁt in terms of eliminating bias that oﬀsets at least part of the cost."))
- **c3** Evaluation without ground truth: cross-validation normally relies on observed outcomes, but for the MSE of treatment effects the unit-level causal effect is never observed (the 'fundamental problem of causal inference', citing Holland); the authors address this by constructing unbiased estimates of the mean-squared error of the treatment effect.([Introduction, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.01132v3#page=3 "We address this by proposing approaches for constructing unbiased estimates of the mean-squared error of the causal eﬀect of the treatment."))
- **c4** Setting and assumptions: potential outcomes with a binary treatment, i.i.d. exchangeable units and no interference (SUTVA, which the authors note may be violated when units are connected through networks), and unconfoundedness throughout; the estimand is the CATE tau(x) = E[Y(1) - Y(0) | X = x], estimated by functions that are constant within partition elements. For exposition the main body assumes complete randomization, and the authors state that the methods can be adapted to the unconfounded case with propensity score weighting.([The Problem (Unconfoundedness), p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.01132v3#page=4 "Later we show that by using propensity score weighting [19], we can adapt all of the methods to that case."))
- **c5** Honest splitting criterion for causal trees: the estimated EMSE rewards a partition for finding strong heterogeneity in treatment effects and penalizes partitions that create variance in leaf estimates (the variance term uses within-leaf treated and control variances scaled by p and 1-p). Unlike the prediction case, the two terms are not proportional: a split on a covariate that affects mean outcomes but not treatment effects can still reduce the variance of the effect estimate.([Honest Inference for Treatment Effects (Modifying the Honest Approach), p.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.01132v3#page=11 "The criteria reward a partition for ﬁnding strong heterogeneity in treatment eﬀects, and penalize a partition that creates variance in leaf estimates."))
- **c6** Inference: once the tree is built, using a distinct sample reduces the problem to estimating treatment effects in each element of a given partition, so standard confidence intervals are valid for honest estimates with no assumptions about model complexity; for the adaptive methods standard confidence intervals are not generally valid.([Inference, p.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.01132v3#page=14 "For the adaptive methods standard approaches to conﬁdence intervals are not generally valid for the reasons discussed above, and below we document through simulations that this can be important in practice."))
- **c7** Simulation (described by the authors as a small study with three designs; K = 2, 10 or 20 normal covariates, p = 0.5, N^tr = N^est of 500 or 1000, test sample of 6000): honest methods achieved nominal coverage of 90% confidence intervals in all designs while adaptive methods were substantially below nominal; honest estimation had a cost in MSE_tau that varied by design and estimator, which the authors summarize as sacrificing some goodness of fit in exchange for valid confidence intervals.([A Simulation Study, p.15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.01132v3#page=15 "We achieve nominal coverage rates for honest methods in all designs"))
- **c8** Observational studies: under unconfoundedness the leaf estimates must be modified to remove bias from simple treated-control comparisons, e.g. by propensity score weighting (renormalizing weights within leaves and treatment groups improves efficiency; citing Crump et al. for trimming extreme propensity scores); additional conditions are required for asymptotic normality with propensity weighting, and these results apply without modification to the estimation phase of honest partitioning.([Observational Studies with Unconfoundedness, p.15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.01132v3#page=15 "The proposed methods can be adapted to observational studies under the assumption of unconfoundedness."))
