## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Prior on changepoints: each observation carries an independent Bernoulli(p_n) changepoint indicator with p_n of order c_n / n, so c_n is the prior expected number of changepoints; letting c_n grow allows many changepoints for large n. Segment coefficients get spike-and-slab priors (or global-local shrinkage priors as an alternative).([Mathematical/Asymptotic Framework, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.12938v1#page=5 "Here, cn controls the expected numbers of change points apriori, and letting cn increase to inﬁnity provides ﬂexibility to model multiple and potentially large number of change points for large n."))
- **c2** Claimed contributions: posterior consistency for model selection and change point recovery via Bayes factors, for both the piecewise constant model and changing high-dimensional linear regression, and attainment of the O(n^-1) minimax localization rate of Frick et al. (2014) by the Bayes estimators.([Introduction, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.12938v1#page=4 "We also prove that the minimax rate of O(n−1) (Frick et al., 2014) is attained by the Bayes estimators for change point recovery."))
- **c3** Assumptions for the consistency theorems include uniformly bounded covariates (A1), coefficients bounded above and below (A2), a growth condition on the maximum model size q_n (A3), slab variance O(1) (A4), true change points at fixed fractions strictly inside (0, 1) (A5) and singular-value conditions on segment design matrices (A6); under A1-A6, Theorem 3 states that the Bayes factor of a model with a wrong number of change points against the true model goes to 0 in probability.([Section 3, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.12938v1#page=7 "(A1) The covariates Xj’s are uniformly bounded."))
- **c4** Theorem 4 (locations): for models with the true number of change points but some location off by more than eps_n, the Bayes factor against the true model goes to 0; eps_n of order n^-1 up to log factors is allowed if q_n grows logarithmically. The authors state the results hold under misspecification of the variance parameter, and that the bounded-covariate condition can be relaxed to sub-exponential tails.([Section 3, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.12938v1#page=8 "The results hold for misspeciﬁcation of variance parameter."))
- **c5** Covariate-free case (piecewise constant mean model): Theorem 4 holds for eps_n going to zero with eps_n at least n^-1 (log n)^2, which the authors describe as equivalent to the frequentist minimax rate up to logarithmic factors.([Section 3, p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.12938v1#page=10 "which gives us rate equivalent to frequentist minimax rate up to logarithmic factors"))
- **c6** Unknown variance: Theorems 6-7 replace sigma^2 by a residual-based estimator. Theorems 7, 9, 11 and 12 are stated under assumptions 'A1 - A7', but only A1-A6 are listed in the text; no definition of A7 was found (searched A7).([Section 3, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.12938v1#page=9 "Theorem 7. Under A1 −A7, for (2.1) , for the empirical estimator of σ2,"))
- **c7** Simulation scope and protocol: the authors state the goal is not to show superiority over existing methods but satisfactory small-sample performance. Example 1 uses the Frick et al. (2014) piecewise constant signal (n = 497, 6 change points), unknown and possibly segment-specific variance, V = 1, I_i ~ Bernoulli(1/n), posterior summaries from 8,000 MCMC samples with the first 4,000 as burn-in, and is compared with PELT and the empirical Bayes method of Martin and Shen (2017) via figures only.([Section 4, p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.12938v1#page=10 "Our goal here is not to establish superiority of the Bayesian method used here over extant methods"))
- **c8** Changing linear regression example (n = 250, p = 250, true change points at 0.3 and 0.7, spike-and-slab prior, 8,000 MCMC iterations): the text reports that almost 93% of posterior samples give the correct number of partitions and almost 90% select the right model; the authors say this agrees with their consistency theory.([Section 4, p.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.12938v1#page=14 "In particular, from Fig. 4, almost 93% of the posterior samples give the correct number of partitions (3), and almost 90% of the posterior samples select the right model."))
- **c9** Real data (weekly burglary and breaking-and-entering counts in Little Rock, 2017; square-root transformed and standardized): with no ground truth the analysis is described as preliminary and exploratory; detected change points mostly agree with PELT (BIC penalty in the figure) while the empirical Bayes method seems conservative and detects none.([Real Data Applications, p.16](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.12938v1#page=16 "As it is impossible to know if there should be any “true” change-points in 2017 data in the absence of additional information, this can be regarded as a preliminary exploratory analysis."))
