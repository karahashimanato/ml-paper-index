## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** The paper names three drawbacks of deep tabular models, following Grinsztajn et al. [13]: (P1) lack of rotational variance, (P2) large data demand, and (P3) over-smooth solutions; SPA targets P1, the tabular Mixup variants target P2, and the attentive feed-forward network targets P3.([Abstract, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.02819v8#page=1 "We delve into three key drawbacks of deep tabular models, encompassing: (P1) lack of rotational variance property, (P2) large data demand, and (P3) over-smooth solution."))
- **c2** SPA: an unoptimizable mask built from feature importance (mutual information) blocks attention from a less informative feature to a more informative one, so information flows only from more to less informative features while pathways between any two features remain; the authors argue this partially disrupts DNNs' rotational invariance.([Solving (P1) with Semi-Permeable Attention, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.02819v8#page=4 "In this way, only more informative features are permitted to propagate information to the less informative ones, and the reverse is not allowed."))
- **c3** Attentive feed-forward network: because the two-layer MLP feed-forward network of Transformers has lesser non-linear fitting capability for irregular feature-target relationships, it is replaced by a GLU layer with tanh instead of sigmoid; the authors say both use two fully connected layers and have similar computational cost.([Solving (P3) with GLU layer, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.02819v8#page=5 "Therefore, we replace the vanilla FFN by a Gated Linear Unit (GLU) layer."))
- **c4** Data augmentation: the authors say vanilla Mixup regularizes DNNs toward linear behavior between samples, which may conflict with irregular target functions; Hid-Mix swaps random embedding dimensions between two samples and Feat-Mix swaps random features, mixing labels by the mutual-information importance of the swapped features.([Solving (P2) with Data Augmentation, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.02819v8#page=5 "To address this challenge, we introduce two Mixup variants, Hid-Mix and Feat-Mix, which mitigate the conflicts in creating samples."))
- **c5** Datasets: 96 small datasets (fewer than 10,000 samples) from the Taptap dataset benchmark and 21 larger public datasets ranging from over 10,000 to 581,835 samples; data are split randomly 80% train / 20% test (20% of training held out for validation), with the same split for all approaches, and ranks are averaged over 5 seeds.([Experimental Setups, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.02819v8#page=6 "A total of 96 small datasets sourced from the Taptap dataset benchmark2 were utilized."))
- **c6** Tuning protocol: XGBoost and CatBoost follow Gorishniy et al. [12] with the maximum number of trees raised from 2000 to 4096 and tuning iterations from 100 to 500 (Optuna); TransTab and XTab use default hyperparameters because their tuning spaces are not given; FT-Transformer, SAINT and TabPFN on large datasets use the hyperparameters of their papers; on small datasets 50 tuning iterations are run per dataset; ExcelFormer is reported with defaults, 'Mix Tuned' (only augmentation hyperparameters) and 'Fully Tuned' (50 iterations).([Experimental Setups (Compared Models), p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.02819v8#page=6 "from 2000 to 4096 and the number of tuning iterations from 100 to 500, which give a more stringent setting and better performances."))
- **c7** Within-paper relation on the 96 small datasets (average rank): ExcelFormer outperforms all hyperparameter-tuned baselines (XGBoost, CatBoost, FT-Transformer, MLP, DCN v2, AutoInt, SAINT) whether or not its own hyperparameters are tuned; even without Feat-Mix and Hid-Mix it still outperforms previous approaches.([Section 6.2, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.02819v8#page=6 "See Table 1, our ExcelFormer consistently outperforms other models that undergo dataset-adaptive hyperparameter tuning, regardless of whether the hyperparameters of the ExcelFormer are tuned or not, which underscores the superiority of our proposed ExcelFormer."))
- **c8** On the 21 larger datasets only XGBoost, CatBoost and FT-Transformer are compared (others excluded for relatively inferior performance and computational load), each with default and tuned hyperparameters; the authors report ExcelFormer outperforms them under both settings and that the default Hid-Mix version is comparable to tuned prior models.([Section 6.2, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.02819v8#page=6 "We excluded other models from the comparison due to their relatively inferior performances and the significant computational load when dealing with large datasets."))
- **c9** Subgroup analysis (task type, sample size, number of features; all existing models tuned): default Hid-Mix ExcelFormer has the best rank in all subgroups except regression, where it slightly lags behind tuned XGBoost, while Mix Tuned ExcelFormer is best in all subgroups; runner-up positions are held by different models (TabPFN, FT-Transformer, XGBoost, CatBoost) in different subgroups.([Can ExcelFormer be a sure bet solution across various types of datasets?, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.02819v8#page=7 "As shown in Table 3, ExcelFormer with Hid-Mix (default) exhibits the best performance in all subgroups except for regression tasks, where it slightly lags behind hyperparameter-tuned XGboost."))
