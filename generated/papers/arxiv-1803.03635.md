## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** The lottery ticket hypothesis is stated as: a randomly-initialized dense network contains a subnetwork, initialized such that, trained in isolation it can match the original network's test accuracy after at most the same number of iterations. Formally, with a mask m on the parameters and initialization m ⊙ theta_0, it predicts a mask exists with j' <= j (commensurate training time, measured as the iteration of minimum validation loss), a' >= a (commensurate accuracy) and ||m||_0 << |theta| (fewer parameters).([Section 1, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.03635v5#page=2 "A randomly-initialized, dense neural network contains a subnet-work that is initialized such that—when trained in isolation—it can match the test accuracy of the original network after training for at most the same number of iterations."))
- **c2** Procedure for identifying winning tickets: randomly initialize f(x; theta_0), train for j iterations, prune p% of the parameters (smallest magnitudes) to create a mask m, and reset the remaining parameters to their values in theta_0. The authors describe the reset to the original initialization as unique to their work. This is one-shot; the paper focuses on iterative pruning, which repeats train-prune-reset over n rounds, each round pruning p^(1/n)% of the surviving weights.([Section 1, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.03635v5#page=2 "Unique to our work, each unpruned connection’s value is then reset to its initialization from original network before it was trained."))
- **c3** Iterative pruning finds winning tickets that match the original network's accuracy at smaller sizes than one-shot pruning does; in the Lenet experiments the authors add that iteratively pruned tickets are costly to find because of repeated training, whereas one-shot pruning also finds winning tickets without it, and they focus on iterative pruning because their goal is the smallest possible tickets.([Section 1, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.03635v5#page=2 "Our results show that iterative pruning ﬁnds winning tickets that match the accuracy of the original network at smaller sizes than does one-shot pruning."))
- **c4** Random reinitialization control: keeping the winning ticket's mask but sampling a new initialization theta'_0 ~ D_theta makes the subnetworks perform far worse, which the authors take to mean that structure alone cannot explain a winning ticket's success.([Section 1, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.03635v5#page=3 "When randomly reinitialized, winning tickets perform far worse, meaning structure alone cannot explain a winning ticket’s success."))
- **c5** Learning-rate sensitivity on deeper networks: for Resnet-18 and VGG-19 on CIFAR10, iterative pruning finds winning tickets only when training uses learning rate warmup (at the original learning rate 0.1 without warmup, iterative pruning did not find winning tickets and performed no better than random reinitialization, Section 4); the authors list explaining why warmup is necessary as future work.([Section 6, p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.03635v5#page=10 "On deeper networks (Resnet-18 and VGG-19), iterative pruning is unable to ﬁnd winning tickets unless we train the networks with learning rate warmup."))
- **c6** Relation to Liu et al. (2019), who find pruned networks trainable when randomly reinitialized: the authors say their VGG-19 experiments confirm this at that level of sparsity (pruned by up to 80%), but that after further pruning initialization matters; they hypothesize that up to a certain sparsity highly overparameterized networks can be pruned, reinitialized and retrained successfully, while beyond it extremely pruned networks only maintain accuracy with fortuitous initialization.([Section 5, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.03635v5#page=9 "We hypothesize that—up to a certain level of sparsity—highly overparameterized networks can be pruned, reinitialized, and retrained successfully; however, beyond this point, extremely pruned, less severely overparamterized networks only maintain accuracy with fortuitous initialization."))
- **c7** Scale limitation: only vision classification on smaller datasets (MNIST, CIFAR10) is considered; larger datasets such as Imagenet are not investigated because iterative pruning is computationally intensive, requiring training a network 15 or more times consecutively for multiple trials.([Section 6, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.03635v5#page=9 "We only consider vision-centric classiﬁcation tasks on smaller datasets (MNIST, CIFAR10)."))
- **c8** Hardware limitation: sparse (unstructured magnitude) pruning is the only method used to find winning tickets; although parameter counts fall, the resulting architectures are not optimized for modern libraries or hardware, and structured pruning (which would produce hardware-friendly networks) and non-magnitude pruning are left for future work.([Section 6, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1803.03635v5#page=9 "Although we reduce parameter-counts, the resulting architectures are not optimized for modern libraries or hardware."))
