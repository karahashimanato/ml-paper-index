## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Bottleneck argument: with problem radius r, a GNN needs K >= r layers (otherwise distant nodes cannot interact, which the authors later call under-reaching), but the receptive field grows exponentially with K (citing Chen et al., 2018), so an exponentially growing amount of information is squashed into a fixed-length vector; the model then learns only short-range signals and 'might generalize poorly at test time'.([Section 3, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.05205v4#page=3 "When a prediction problem relies on long-range interaction between nodes, the GNN must have as many layers K as the estimated range of these interactions, or otherwise, these distant nodes would not be able to interact."))
- **c2** Layer definitions used in the analysis: a GNN layer is h_v^(k) = f_k(h_v^(k-1), {h_u^(k-1) | u in N_v}; theta_k); GCN sums c_uv W^(k) h_u^(k-1) over N_v together with v itself, with normalization c_uv often set to sqrt(|N_v||N_u|) or |N_v| (citing Hamilton et al., 2017), followed by a nonlinearity; GIN applies an MLP to (1 + eps) h_v plus the sum of neighbor states. For graph prediction a permutation-invariant readout (sum, average or weighted sum) aggregates the final-layer nodes.([Preliminaries, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.05205v4#page=3 "The design of the function f is what mostly distinguishes one type of GNN from the other."))
- **c3** On Tree-NeighborsMatch (binary trees, r = depth, r+1 layers, training accuracy measured), GCN and GIN perfectly fit r = 3 at most, while GGNN and GAT also reach 100% at r = 4, and at r = 5 no GNN fits the data perfectly. The authors explain this by aggregation: GCN and GIN aggregate all neighbors before combining with the target node, whereas GAT's attention lets the target ignore the irrelevant incoming edge at the last layer; they hypothesize (following Levy et al., 2018) that the GRU in GGNN filters edges similarly.([Section 4.1, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.05205v4#page=5 "GCN and GIN managed to perfectly ﬁt r=3 at most, while GGNN and GAT also reached 100% accuracy at r=4."))
- **c4** FA-layer protocol: the K-th layer of an existing, extensively tuned model is turned into a fully-adjacent layer (no change of layer type, no added weights), and models are re-trained with the original authors' code without additional tuning, to rule out hyperparameter tuning as the source of improvement. The authors stress that the FA layer is merely a simple demonstration, not their main contribution.([Section 4.2, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.05205v4#page=5 "A fully-adjacent layer is a GNN layer in which every pair of nodes is connected by an edge."))
- **c5** Over-squashing vs. under-reaching on QM9 (models and 500-configuration tuning from Brockschmidt, 2020; 5 runs per property): the average graph diameter is 6.35±0.91 (maximum 10, 90th percentile 8) while most models use K = 8; models with 10 layers did not improve over the base, models with doubled hidden size improved by only 5.5% versus 42% for the FA layer, and making all K layers fully adjacent gave 1500% higher error. The authors conclude the gain comes from reduced over-squashing, not increased reachability.([Section 4.2, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.05205v4#page=7 "We conclude that the source of improvement was clearly not the increased reachability, but instead, the reduction in over-squashing."))
- **c6** Biological benchmarks: using the implementation, 10 folds and splits of Errica et al. (2020) (30 test runs = 10 folds x 3 seeds), converting the last layer to FA gave GIN+FA an improvement of 1.5% over GIN on NCI1, and on ENZYMES (where Errica et al. found the structure-agnostic baseline better than all GNNs) GIN+FA improved by 8.1% over GIN and by 2.5% over the No Struct baseline; on average FA reduced the error rate by 12% on ENZYMES and 4.8% on NCI1.([Biological benchmarks, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.05205v4#page=7 "We used the same 10-folds and split as Errica et al. (2020)."))
- **c7** Combinatorial lower bound (analysis stated to hold for GCN and GIN): a d-dimensional vector of f-bit floats must distinguish all (m^r)!/(m!)^(m^r - 1) label assignments of the tree leaves, so with d = 32 the maximal radius is r = 7. Empirically the required d is higher because gradient descent need not find a solution; even d = 512 fits r = 7 at most. Attention-based aggregation like GAT compresses only half of the leaves, raising the bound on r by up to 1.([Section 5, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.05205v4#page=9 "That is, a model with d=32 cannot obtain 100% accuracy for r>7."))
- **c8** Relation to over-smoothing: the authors hypothesize that in long-range problems the degraded performance is explained by over-squashing rather than over-smoothing, which was mostly demonstrated on short-range tasks; in Appendix E they argue the two are disparate (a 3-node triangle graph with r = 1 can over-smooth without over-squashing, while Tree-NeighborsMatch can over-squash without over-smoothing).([Section 6, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.05205v4#page=9 "We hypothesize that in long-range problems, the explanation for the degraded performance is over-squashing rather than over-smoothing."))
