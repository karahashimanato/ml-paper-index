## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** The KL(q||p) objective cannot be computed because it depends on the evidence log p(x); VI instead maximizes the ELBO = E[log p(z,x)] - E[log q(z)], which equals the negative KL plus log p(x), so maximizing the ELBO is equivalent to minimizing the KL divergence.([Section 2.2, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.00670v9#page=6 "Because we cannot compute the KL, we optimize an alternative objective that is equivalent to the KL up to an added constant,"))
- **c2** Using the ELBO as a model-selection criterion (premise: the bound approximates the marginal likelihood) sometimes works in practice, but the authors state that selecting based on a bound is not justified in theory.([Section 2.2, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.00670v9#page=7 "Though this sometimes works in practice, selecting based on a bound is not justiﬁed in theory."))
- **c3** When to use which: MCMC tends to be more computationally intensive but provides guarantees of (asymptotically) exact samples, while VI has no such guarantee and only finds a density close to the target but tends to be faster. Hence VI suits large data sets and quickly exploring many models; MCMC suits smaller data sets where a heavier computational cost is acceptable for more precise samples.([Introduction, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.00670v9#page=3 "Thus, variational inference is suited to large data sets and scenarios where we want to quickly explore many models; MCMC is suited to smaller data sets and scenarios where we happily pay a heavier computational cost for more precise samples."))
- **c4** Data set size is not the only consideration; posterior geometry matters too. For mixture models (multiple modes from label permutations), Gibbs sampling quickly focuses on one of the modes if the model permits it; where Gibbs is not an option, VI may perform better than a more general MCMC technique such as HMC, even for small datasets (citing Kucukelbir et al., 2015).([Introduction, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.00670v9#page=3 "For mixture models where Gibbs sampling is not an option, variational inference may perform better than a more general MCMC technique (e.g., Hamiltonian Monte Carlo), even for small datasets (Kucukelbir et al., 2015)."))
- **c5** The relative accuracy of VI and MCMC is stated to be still unknown; what is known is that VI generally underestimates the posterior variance as a consequence of its objective. Depending on the task this may be acceptable; the authors cite empirical work in which VI does not necessarily suffer in accuracy (e.g. posterior predictive densities) and other work (Giordano et al., 2015) focusing on where it falls short, especially around the posterior variance.([Introduction, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.00670v9#page=3 "We do know that variational inference generally underestimates the variance of the posterior density; this is a consequence of its objective function."))
- **c6** Mean-field family: it can capture any marginal density but not correlation between latent variables. On a correlated 2D Gaussian, the optimal mean-field approximation has the same mean but decoupled covariance and under-represents the marginal variances, a common effect explained by KL(q||p) penalizing mass of q where p has little mass but penalizing the reverse less.([Section 2.3, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.00670v9#page=9 "Further, the marginal variances of the approximation under-represent those of the target density."))
- **c7** The ELBO is (generally) non-convex and CAVI only guarantees convergence to a local optimum that can be sensitive to initialization; on the mixture example, random initializations reach different ELBO values, indicating many local optima. For assessing convergence the authors suggest monitoring the average log predictive of a small held-out set as a proxy for the full-data ELBO (which, unlike the ELBO, is not guaranteed to increase monotonically under CAVI).([Section 2.5, p.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.00670v9#page=11 "Each initialization reaches a different value, indicating the presence of many local optima in the ELBO."))
- **c8** SVI (for conditionally conjugate models): subsample a data point, compute its optimal local parameters, and follow a noisy natural gradient of the global parameters that is unbiased; step sizes must satisfy the Robbins-Monro conditions (e.g. t^-kappa with kappa in (0.5, 1]); stochastic optimization of the ELBO reaches a local optimum. The authors emphasize SVI needs no derivation beyond CAVI.([Section 4.3, p.21](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.00670v9#page=21 "We emphasize that SVI requires no new derivation beyond what is needed for CAVI."))
- **c9** Open problems: the interface between VI and MCMC remains relatively unexplored, and the authors state that a principled analysis of when to use (and combine) VI and MCMC would have both theoretical and practical impact.([Section 5.4, p.26](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1601.00670v9#page=26 "A principled analysis of when to use (and combine) variational inference and MCMC would have both theoretical and practical impact in the ﬁeld."))
