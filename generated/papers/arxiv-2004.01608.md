- **c1** Contribution: the paper learns a local search (improvement) heuristic based on 2-opt operators via deep RL, in contrast to most learned TSP methods, which learn construction heuristics.([Abstract, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.01608v3#page=1 "In this work, we propose to learn a local search heuristic based on 2-opt operators via deep reinforcement learning."))
- **c2** How the move is learned: a policy gradient algorithm learns a stochastic policy selecting the 2-opt operation (a pair of node positions) given the current solution; the reward is the improvement of the best-found tour.([Abstract, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.01608v3#page=1 "We propose a policy gradient algorithm to learn a stochastic policy that selects 2-opt operations given a current solution."))
- **c3** Comparison with classical 2-opt: learned policies and 2-opt first-improvement / best-improvement heuristics (with and without random restarts) are run on 512 TSP100 instances from the same starting solutions with an equal step budget of 1,000 steps (steps, not wall-clock time, are matched).([Experimental Results and Analysis, p.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.01608v3#page=11 "We run all heuristics and learned policies on 512 TSP100 instances for a maximum of 1,000 steps starting from the same solutions."))
- **c4** Protocol: training instances are uniform random in the unit square (TSP20/50/100); test uses the 10,000-instance sets of Kool et al. and Joshi et al., with Concorde optima rerun to compute optimality gaps.([Experiments and Results, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.01608v3#page=9 "we use the same test dataset as reported in Kool et al. (2019); Joshi et al. (2019) containing 10,000 instances for each TSP size."))
- **c5** Evaluation practice: results for Wu et al. (2019) are copied from the original paper (no implementation was available) and are on a different test set generated by the same process; most baseline running times are also taken from earlier papers, and only Kool et al.'s method is rerun on the authors' hardware.([Experiments and Results, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.01608v3#page=9 "Since at the time of submission no implementation is available, we report the optimality gaps reported in the original paper."))
- **c6** Generalization to TSPLIB: the TSP100-trained policy never sees instances over 100 nodes; on the extended set of 35 TSPLIB instances (supplement) with 2,000 sampling steps its average optimality gap is larger than OR-Tools' (in the main text, on 10 instances, it beats OR-Tools on 5).([Generalization to Real-world TSP instances, p.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.01608v3#page=14 "On the 35 instances the gaps are 8.61% (ours) and 3.70% (OR-Tools)."))
- **c7** Limitation: the policy gradient method needs a large number of samples to train a good policy.([Conclusions and Future Work, p.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.01608v3#page=14 "One drawback of our policy gradient method is the large number of samples required to train a good policy."))
