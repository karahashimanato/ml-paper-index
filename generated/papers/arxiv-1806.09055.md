## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Continuous relaxation: the categorical choice of an operation on each edge is replaced by a softmax over all candidate operations, weighted by architecture parameters alpha; at the end of search a discrete architecture is obtained by replacing each mixed operation with its most likely operation.([Section 2.2, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.09055v2#page=3 "To make the search space continuous, we relax the categorical choice of a particular operation to a softmax over all possible operations:"))
- **c2** Bilevel formulation: the architecture alpha (upper level) minimizes the validation loss of weights w*(alpha) that minimize the training loss (lower level); the authors relate this to gradient-based hyperparameter optimization, noting alpha can be viewed as a special type of hyperparameter of substantially higher dimension and harder to optimize.([Section 2.2, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.09055v2#page=3 "This implies a bilevel optimization problem (Anandalingam & Friesz, 1992; Colson et al., 2007) with α as the upper-level variable and w as the lower-level variable:"))
- **c3** Approximation: because the exact architecture gradient requires the expensive inner optimization, w*(alpha) is approximated by a single training step; the authors state they are not aware of convergence guarantees for the resulting algorithm but that in practice it reaches a fixed point with a suitable choice of the inner step size. The second-order term is approximated by finite differences, reducing complexity from O(|alpha||w|) to O(|alpha| + |w|).([Section 2.3, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.09055v2#page=4 "While we are not currently aware of the convergence guarantees for our optimization algorithm, in practice it is able to reach a ﬁxed point with a suitable choice of"))
- **c4** First-order variant: with the inner step size set to 0, the architecture gradient is simply the validation-loss gradient at the current weights (treating w as w*(alpha)), which the authors report gives some speed-up but empirically worse performance in their CIFAR-10 and PTB experiments.([Section 2.3, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.09055v2#page=4 "This leads to some speed-up but empirically worse performance, according to our experimental results in Table 1 and Table 2."))
- **c5** Selection and evaluation protocol: DARTS is run four times with different random seeds and the best cell is chosen by validation performance after a short training from scratch (100 epochs on CIFAR-10, 300 on PTB), which the authors say is particularly important for recurrent cells because outcomes can be initialization-sensitive; the selected architecture is re-initialized and trained from scratch, and the test set is never used for search or selection. The table captions state that reported DARTS search costs exclude the selection cost and the final evaluation cost.([Section 3.2, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.09055v2#page=7 "We note the test set is never used for architecture search or architecture selection."))
- **c6** CIFAR-10 setup: half of the CIFAR-10 training data is held out as the validation set for search; an 8-cell network is searched for 50 epochs (one day on a single GPU; all experiments on GTX 1080Ti); the evaluation network has 20 cells trained for 600 epochs with cutout, path dropout and auxiliary towers. Because CIFAR results have high variance even with the same setup (citing Liu et al., 2018b), the mean and standard deviation of 10 independent runs are reported for the full model, and the NASNet-A and AmoebaNet-A cells were retrained in the authors' framework under the same settings.([Appendix A.2.1, p.12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.09055v2#page=12 "Since the CIFAR results are subject to high variance even with exactly the same setup (Liu et al., 2018b), we report the mean and standard deviation of 10 independent runs for our full model."))
- **c7** Random search baseline: the authors note random search is competitive for both convolutional and recurrent models, which they say reflects the importance of search space design; DARTS improves on it with comparable or less search cost (2.76 +/- 0.09 vs 3.29 +/- 0.15 test error on CIFAR-10; 55.7 vs 59.4 perplexity on PTB). Per the table footnotes, the random baseline is the best of 24 (CIFAR-10) or 8 (PTB) sampled architectures selected by short-training validation error.([Section 3.3, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.09055v2#page=8 "It is also interesting to note that random search is competitive for both convolutional and recurrent models, which reﬂects the importance of the search space design."))
- **c8** Ablation of the bilevel formulation: jointly optimizing alpha and w over the union of training and validation sets by coordinate descent gave a best cell (of 4 runs) with 4.16 +/- 0.16% test error, worse than random search, and simultaneous SGD on alpha and w over all data gave 3.56 +/- 0.10%; the authors hypothesize these heuristics make alpha overfit the training data.([Section 3.3, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.09055v2#page=8 "We hypothesize that these heuristics would cause α (analogous to hyperparameters) to overﬁt the training data, leading to poor generalization."))
- **c9** Stated limitation: the method may suffer from discrepancies between the continuous architecture encoding and the derived discrete architecture, which the authors suggest could be alleviated, e.g., by annealing the softmax temperature to enforce one-hot selection.([Section 4, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.09055v2#page=9 "For example, the current method may suffer from discrepancies between the continuous architecture encoding and the derived discrete architecture."))
- **c10** Mixed operation (Eq. 2): on edge (i, j), o_bar(x) = sum_{o in O} [exp(alpha_o^(i,j)) / sum_{o' in O} exp(alpha_o'^(i,j))] o(x), a softmax-weighted sum of all candidate operations; the bilevel problem is min_alpha L_val(w*(alpha), alpha) subject to w*(alpha) = argmin_w L_train(w, alpha).([Section 2.2, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.09055v2#page=3 "To make the search space continuous, we relax the categorical choice of a particular operation to a softmax over all possible operations:"))
- **c11** One-step approximation (Eq. 6-7): the architecture gradient grad_alpha L_val(w*(alpha), alpha) is approximated by grad_alpha L_val(w - xi grad_w L_train(w, alpha), alpha), whose chain-rule expansion grad_alpha L_val(w', alpha) - xi grad^2_{alpha,w} L_train(w, alpha) grad_w' L_val(w', alpha) uses the one-step forward weights w' = w - xi grad_w L_train(w, alpha); the second-order term is approximated by finite differences.([Section 2.3, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.09055v2#page=4 "denotes the weights for a one-step forward model."))
