## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Goal and setting: i.i.d. regression data (X_i, Y_i) with no assumptions on the regression function or P; the band must satisfy P(Y_{n+1} in C(X_{n+1})) >= 1 - alpha with the probability taken over the n + 1 i.i.d. draws, with finite-sample (nonasymptotic) validity.([Introduction, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.04173v2#page=3 "The main goal of this paper is to construct prediction bands as in (1) that have ﬁnite-sample (nonasymptotic) validity, without assumptions on P."))
- **c2** The naive interval from quantiles of the fitted (in-sample) residuals is only approximately valid for large samples under regularity conditions (e.g. a correctly specified model or appropriate tuning) and can grossly undercover because the fitted residual distribution is often biased downwards.([Conformal Prediction Sets (Section 2), p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.04173v2#page=7 "In general, the naive method (3) can grossly undercover since the ﬁtted residual distribution can often be biased downwards."))
- **c3** Theorem 2.1 (full conformal): with i.i.d. data, coverage is at least 1 - alpha; if the fitted absolute residuals have a continuous joint distribution, coverage is also at most 1 - alpha + 1/(n+1). The lower bound is a standard property due to Vovk; the upper bound is new, and a random tie-breaking rule could remove the continuity assumption. The estimator must be a symmetric function of the data points, and the i.i.d. assumption can be weakened to exchangeability.([Section 2, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.04173v2#page=8 "The ﬁrst part of the theorem, on the ﬁnite-sample validity of conformal intervals in regression, is a standard property of all conformal inference procedures and is due to Vovk."))
- **c4** Remark 2.3: the guarantees are marginal (average) coverage and must not be confused with conditional coverage given X_{n+1} = x, a much stronger property that, citing Lei & Wasserman (2014, an earlier paper by an overlapping author), cannot be achieved by finite-length prediction intervals without regularity and consistency assumptions on the model and the estimator; it holds asymptotically under certain conditions (Theorem 3.5).([Section 2, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.04173v2#page=9 "Conditional coverage does hold asymptotically under certain conditions; see Theorem 3.5 in Section 3."))
- **c5** Split conformal (Algorithm 2): randomly split the data into two equal halves, fit on one half and rank absolute residuals on the other; its computational cost is that of the fitting step. Theorem 2.2: with i.i.d. data coverage is at least 1 - alpha, and at most 1 - alpha + 2/(n+2) if the residuals have a continuous joint distribution.([Section 2.2, p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.04173v2#page=10 "The split conformal method separates the ﬁtting and ranking steps using sample splitting, and its computational cost is simply that of the ﬁtting step."))
- **c6** Multiple splits: splitting introduces extra randomness; intersecting N split-conformal bands at level 1 - alpha/N keeps marginal coverage (Bonferroni), but Theorem 2.4 shows that under the paper's assumptions the band becomes wider with probability tending to 1, so the authors suggest using a single split.([Multiple Splits, p.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.04173v2#page=11 "Therefore, we suggest using a single split."))
- **c7** Simulations (Settings A-C, 50 repetitions, 90% nominal level): in the low-dimensional linear-regression comparison, full conformal intervals were shorter than split conformal intervals but at a higher computational cost; in the high-dimensional case (n = 500, d = 490) split conformal with linear regression had to be omitted because the regression is not well defined on half the sample.([Section 4, p.22](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.04173v2#page=22 "The full conformal interval is shorter than the split conformal interval, but comes at a higher computational cost."))
- **c8** Constant width: full and split conformal bands have roughly constant width over x (exactly constant for split conformal), which is undesirable when the residual spread varies with X. The locally-weighted extension divides residuals by an estimated conditional mean absolute deviation (fit on the first half for split conformal), and the validity properties carry over.([Section 5.2, p.30](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.04173v2#page=30 "In fact, for split conformal, the width is exactly constant over x."))
- **c9** Conclusion: the distribution-free coverage is marginal; conditional coverage may be above 1 - alpha at some x and below at others. The authors do not view this as a disadvantage, since accuracy of the band is tied to the base estimator: an accurate estimator gives a near-optimal band and a bad one still gives valid marginal coverage.([Conclusion, p.37](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.04173v2#page=37 "The distribution-free coverage oﬀered by conformal intervals is marginal."))
