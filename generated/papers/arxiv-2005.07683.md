## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Motivation: magnitude pruning is described as highly effective in standard supervised learning but inherently less useful under transfer learning, because fine-tuned weights are mostly predetermined by the pretrained model, so magnitude selection cannot learn to prune based on the fine-tuning step.([Introduction, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.07683v2#page=1 "In transfer learning, weight values are mostly predetermined by the original model and are only ﬁne-tuned on the end task."))
- **c2** Definition: magnitude pruning uses zeroth-order information (|W|); movement pruning uses first-order information, retaining connections that move away from zero during training. Masks are Top_v(S) over learned scores S, the gradient through Top_v is approximated straight-through, so scores of masked weights are still updated; after T updates S_ij = -alpha_S sum_t (dL/dW_ij)^(t) W_ij^(t).([Movement Pruning, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.07683v2#page=3 "Intuitively, instead of selecting weights that are far from zero, we retain connections that are moving away from zero during the training process."))
- **c3** Soft movement pruning replaces the top-v% selection with a fixed global threshold tau and adds a regularizer lambda_mvp * sum sigma(S_ij) that pushes scores down, so lambda_mvp controls the penalty intensity and thus the sparsity level (rather than setting it directly); an L1 penalty on S was reported as harder to tune with similar results.([Movement Pruning, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.07683v2#page=3 "Here we replace hyperparameter v with a ﬁxed global threshold value τ that controls the binary mask."))
- **c4** Budget: task-specific pruning of BERT-base-uncased (about 84M parameters, embeddings frozen) on SQuAD v1.1, MNLI and QQP; for each task all pruning methods are fine-tuned for the same number of updates (6-10 epochs), with the cubic sparsity schedule of Zhu and Gupta plus a few cool-down steps for magnitude, movement and soft movement pruning; sparsity percentages are relative to BERT-base. A footnote says preliminary experiments suggested more pruning steps tended to improve end performance.([Experimental Setup, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.07683v2#page=5 "we ﬁne-tune the pre-trained model for the same number of updates (between 6 and 10 epochs) across pruning methods"))
- **c5** Baselines: Reweighted Proximal Pruning (RPP) and LayerDrop numbers are taken from their authors rather than rerun; for structured L0 regularization (McCarley, 2019) no implementation was found, so an unstructured L0 regularization baseline was used instead; mini-BERT models (Turc et al., 2019) are also compared.([Experimental Setup, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.07683v2#page=5 "For RPP and LayerDrop, we report results from authors."))
- **c6** Sparsity-dependent ranking: at low sparsity (more than 70% of weights remaining) magnitude pruning outperforms all methods with little or no loss versus the dense model, while movement pruning methods degrade quickly even at low sparsity; magnitude pruning performs poorly at high sparsity, and first-order methods are strong with less than 15% of weights remaining.([Results, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.07683v2#page=6 "magnitude pruning outperforms all methods with little or no loss with respect to the dense model whereas the performance of movement pruning methods quickly decreases even for low sparsity levels"))
- **c7** Hardware caveat: although movement pruning also beats fine-tuned mini-BERT models of the same size, the authors note that current hardware does not support optimized inference for sparse models, so for inference speed a small dense model such as mini-BERT might often be preferable; Appendix A.3 adds that their sparse models gave no significant inference speedup with standard PyTorch.([Results, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.07683v2#page=6 "We do note though that current hardware does not support optimized inference for sparse models: from an inference speed perspective, it might often desirable to use a small dense model such as mini-BERT over a sparse alternative of the same size."))
- **c8** Distillation combination: using the fine-tuned BERT-base model as teacher, the training objective is a convex combination of the training loss and a distillation loss on output distributions; the authors report that the relative ranking of pruning methods is unchanged while all performances increase.([Results (Distillation further boosts performance), p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.07683v2#page=7 "Overall, we observe that the relative comparisons of the pruning methods remain unchanged while the performances are strictly increased."))
