## 主張

- **c1** Architecture: following GShard, the feed-forward component of every other Transformer layer is replaced by an MoE layer of independent feed-forward experts; a gating function uses a softmax to model a distribution over experts, and only a subset of size two is activated per token (the learned gating network picks the best two experts per token at inference), with the token representation being the weighted combination of the selected experts' outputs.([Section 4, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.06905v2#page=3 "Similar to the GShard MoE Transformer (Lepikhin et al., 2021), we replace the feed-forward component of every other Transformer layer with an MoE layer, as shown in Figure 2."))
- **c2** Load balancing: on top of the cross-entropy loss, the MoE auxiliary loss described in GShard is added with coefficient 0.01 to encourage the gating function to distribute tokens more evenly across experts. All GLaM models use the same learning hyperparameters (Adafactor, max sequence length 1024, up to 1 million tokens per batch, dropout 0); the largest model was trained on 1,024 Cloud TPU-V4 chips.([Section 5.2, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.06905v2#page=4 "On top of the standard cross-entropy loss, we add the MoE auxiliary loss as described in GShard (Lepikhin et al., 2021) with a 0.01 coefﬁcient to encourage expert load balancing so that the gating function will distribute tokens more evenly across all experts."))
- **c3** Comparison budget: several MoE and dense variants are trained on the same training data, and dense models with comparable numbers of activated parameters per token during inference (and thus similar per-token FLOPs) are included as references; GLaM (Base Dense Size/E) denotes a dense architecture with every other layer replaced by an E-expert MoE layer.([Section 5.1, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.06905v2#page=4 "We also include the respective dense models with comparable numbers of activated parameters per-token during inference (and thus similar numbers of per-token FLOPs) as references."))
- **c4** Scaling observation (shown in figures, averaged over the generative tasks): performance scales with effective FLOPs per prediction (determined by activated parameters), and GLaM MoE models perform consistently better than GLaM dense models of similar effective FLOPs per token; on understanding tasks the authors observe MoE and dense models perform similarly at smaller scales while MoE models outperform at larger scales.([Section 6.3, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.06905v2#page=6 "We also ﬁnd that GLaM MoE models perform consistently better than GLaM dense models for similar effective FLOPs per token."))
- **c5** Evaluation protocol versus GPT-3: to allow an apples-to-apples comparison the same task suite as Brown et al. (2020) is used, but 7 synthetic tasks (arithmetic and word unscramble) and 6 machine translation datasets are excluded for simplicity, leaving 29 datasets (8 generative, 21 understanding) in 7 categories; one/few-shot demonstrations are drawn mainly from each task's training set, and evaluation is mainly on development sets (Section 6.1).([Section 5.3, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.06905v2#page=5 "But for simplicity, we exclude 7 synthetic tasks (arithmetic and word unscramble) and 6 machine translation datasets."))
- **c6** Energy: GLaM (64B/64E) training after 600B tokens consumes 456 MWh, about 1/3 of the 1287 MWh used by GPT-3; to reach scores similar to (slightly exceeding) GPT-3, training used 1,024 TPU-v4 chips for 574 hours with 280B tokens, consuming 213 MWh or 1/6 of the GPT-3 energy cost.([Section 6.4, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.06905v2#page=7 "This consumes 213 MWh or 1/6 of the GPT-3 energy cost."))
- **c7** The authors attribute the reduced energy consumption jointly to the MoE architecture and to computation-efficiency optimizations from TPU-v4 hardware and GSPMD software, i.e. not to the architecture alone.([Section 6.4, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.06905v2#page=7 "The reduced energy consumption of GLaM is due to the MoE architecture and computation efﬁciency optimizations from TPU-v4 hardware and GSPMD software."))
- **c8** Training stability: stating that trillion-parameter training leaves little room for hyperparameter tuning, the authors train smaller models to convergence first, skip weight updates for a batch if gradients contain NaNs or Infs, and restart from an earlier healthy checkpoint when encountering rare large fluctuations or NaN/Inf during training.([Section 5.2, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.06905v2#page=5 "We restart from an early healthy checkpoint when encountering rare large ﬂuctuations or even NaN/Inf during training."))
- **c9** Stated limitation: despite in-context learning and training efficiency advantages, sparsely activated models have more parameters and thus require more devices, which limits resource accessibility and increases serving cost, especially when serving traffic is low.([Section 8, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.06905v2#page=9 "This limits the resource accessibility and increases the serving cost especially when the serving trafﬁc is low."))
