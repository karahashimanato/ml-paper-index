## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Critique of common practice: many proposed models were tested only on the same train/validation/test splits of CORA, CiteSeer and PubMed from Yang et al. (2016), which the authors say favors the model that overfits the most; second, new models are often trained with a procedure different from the baselines', making it hard to tell whether gains come from the architecture or from better-tuned training/hyperparameters (citing Lipton and Steinhardt, 2018).([Section 1, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.05868v2#page=1 "Such experimental setup favors the model that overﬁts the most and defeats the main purpose of using a train/validation/test split — ﬁnding the model with the best generalization properties [Friedman et al., 2001]."))
- **c2** Training procedure: model architectures are kept as in the original papers (2 layers; 8 attention heads for GAT, 2 Gaussian kernels for MoNet), but all models use the same optimizer (Adam with default parameters), Glorot initialization with zero biases, no learning-rate decay, the same maximum epochs, early-stopping criterion, patience and validation frequency, and full-batch training. Appendix A documents that the original procedures differed (e.g. GraphSAGE used mini-batches of 512 and no early stopping).([Section 3, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.05868v2#page=2 "For a more balanced comparison, however, we use the same training procedure for all the models."))
- **c3** Split protocol: for every dataset, 20 labeled nodes per class for training, 30 per class for validation and the rest for test, with 100 random splits and 20 random weight initializations per split; graphs are treated as undirected and restricted to the largest connected component.([Section 3, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.05868v2#page=3 "In all cases, we use 20 labeled nodes per class as the training set, 30 nodes per class as the validation set, and the rest as the test set."))
- **c4** Hyperparameter selection: the same grid search (learning rate, hidden size, L2 strength, dropout) for every model, restricted so that every model has at most the same number of trainable parameters; the configuration with the best average accuracy on Cora and CiteSeer (over 100 splits x 20 initializations) is then used for all datasets, so the other six datasets are not tuned individually.([Section 3, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.05868v2#page=3 "For every model, we picked the hyperparameter conﬁguration that achieved the best average accuracy on Cora and CiteSeer datasets (averaged over 100 train/validation/test splits and 20 random initializations for each)."))
- **c5** Baselines and overall result: the GNNs (GCN, MoNet, GAT, GraphSAGE) significantly outperform all baselines - attribute-only MLP and logistic regression, and structure-only label propagation (plain and normalized-Laplacian) - on all 8 datasets, but among the GNNs there is no clear winner across datasets; for 5 of 8 datasets the 2nd and 3rd best are within 1% of the best average.([Section 3, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.05868v2#page=3 "Among the GNN approaches, there is no clear winner that dominates across all the datasets."))
- **c6** Averaged over datasets and splits (relative accuracy and average rank), GCN achieves the best performance; the authors relate this to findings in other fields that simpler models often outperform more sophisticated ones if hyperparameter tuning is performed equally carefully (citing Melis et al., 2018 and Lucic et al., 2017).([Section 3, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.05868v2#page=4 "We observe that GCN is able to achieve the best performance across all models."))
- **c7** Effect of the split: on the Planetoid split of Yang et al. (2016), GAT scores best on CORA and CiteSeer and GCN on PubMed, but on a different random split with the same set sizes GCN is first on CORA and CiteSeer and MoNet wins on PubMed; the authors conclude that results from a single split are fragile and misleading and that multiple-split evaluation is needed.([Section 3, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.05868v2#page=4 "This shows how fragile and misleading results obtained on a single split can be."))
- **c8** GAT's lower average and high variance on Amazon Computers and Amazon Photo comes from rare outliers: median scores of all GNNs are very close, but GAT produces extremely low scores (below 40%) for some weight initializations, in 138 of 2000 runs on Amazon Photo.([Section 3, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.05868v2#page=4 "While these outliers occur rarely (for 138 out of 2000 runs), they signiﬁcantly lower the average score of GAT."))
