## 主張

- **c1** Heteroscedasticity (Section 3.2): applying an output transformation (Box-Cox/Yeo-Johnson) to a vanilla GP gave a more favourable log probability in 70 of 108 Bayesmark tasks, significantly in 58 (two-sided paired t-test); Levene's and Fligner-Killeen tests rejected homoscedasticity in 72 and 73 of 108 tasks, which the authors read as at least 66% of tasks being heteroscedastic.([Section 3.2, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.03826v6#page=8 "In 58/108 tasks, the gain is signiﬁcant at the 95% level of conﬁdence (p-value < 0.025)."))
- **c2** Non-stationarity (Section 3.1): with Kumaraswamy input warping the stationary GP's log probability was more favourable in 106 of 108 tasks and significantly so in 79 (described as a one-sided t-test, whereas the output-transform test in Section 3.2 is described as two-sided paired).([Section 3.1, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.03826v6#page=7 "In 79/108 tasks, the gain is signiﬁcant at the 95% level of conﬁdence (p-value < 0.025)."))
- **c3** Modelling fix: Box-Cox (Yeo-Johnson for labels of arbitrary sign) output transformations together with Kumaraswamy input warping are chosen as a balance between simplicity and empirical performance; the output-transform parameter is refit before the GP, and the warping parameters are optimized under the GP marginal likelihood (similar to Balandat et al., 2020).([Section 4.1, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.03826v6#page=9 "We observe that the well-known Box-Cox (Box & Cox, 1964) and Yeo-Jonhson (Yeo & Johnson, 2000) output transformations in conjunction with the Kumaraswamy (Kumaraswamy, 1980) input transformation, oﬀer a balance between simplicity of implementation and empirical performance."))
- **c4** Robust acquisition: instead of a worst-case max-min formulation (conservative, no known global convergence guarantees in the non-convex non-concave case), HEBO targets the expected acquisition over random perturbations of the GP parameters and approximates it by adding Gaussian noise to the acquisition value; an informal theorem states the approximation holds with high probability for suitable noise scales.([Section 4.2.1, p.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.03826v6#page=11 "Importantly, this problem seeks to ﬁnd new query locations that perform well on average over a distribution of surrogate models in favour of assuming a perfect surrogate."))
- **c5** Acquisition ensemble: EI, PI and UCB (which the authors show can conflict in their optima) are optimized jointly as a multi-objective problem for a Pareto front with NSGA-II (pymoo), chosen because evolutionary solvers handle categorical and integer hyperparameters; the authors observed the GP-Hedge alternative to perform poorly compared with individual acquisitions.([Section 4.2.2, p.12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.03826v6#page=12 "We however, observed this formulation to perform poorly when compared against individual acquisitions."))
- **c6** Evaluation protocol: 108 Bayesmark tasks (nine models, six UCI datasets, two metrics each; objective = cross-validation loss, noise from train-test splits; 2 to 9 hyperparameters), 16 iterations of batch 8 (or 100 iterations of batch 1), 20 random seeds, and a normalised score relative to an estimated optimal loss and the mean loss of random search. The same setup (Section 3) was used for the assumption analysis that motivated HEBO's design. Baselines include SkOpt, pySOT, HyperOpt, OpenTuner, Nevergrad (1+1), BOHB (modified to batch, TPE without Hyperband fidelities), Dragonfly, TuRBO and TuRBO+ (TuRBO with the authors' modelling improvements); no statement on how baseline settings were chosen was found (searched for default, tuned).([Section 5, p.12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.03826v6#page=12 "Each experiment is repeated for 20 random seeds."))
- **c7** Ablation: removing or adding each component one at a time, the observed order of importance is heteroscedasticity (output transform), multi-objective acquisition, non-stationarity (input warping) and robustness.([Section 5.3, p.16](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.03826v6#page=16 "The precedence order observed is: heteroscedasticity, multi-objective acquisition functions, non-stationarity and robustness."))
- **c8** Black-box challenge: the abstract states that HEBO placed first in the NeurIPS 2020 Black-Box Optimisation challenge; the main text describes the challenge only as using the same UCI testbed (citing Turner et al., 2021) and gives no description of the challenge's evaluation protocol (searched for challenge).([Abstract, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.03826v6#page=1 "We demonstrate HEBO’s empirical eﬃcacy on the NeurIPS 2020 Black-Box Optimisation challenge, where HEBO placed ﬁrst."))
