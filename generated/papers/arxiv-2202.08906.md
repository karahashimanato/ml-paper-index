## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Router z-loss: L_z(x) = (1/B) sum_i (log sum_j exp(x_ij))^2 over the B tokens and N experts' router logits; it penalizes large logits into the gating network. The router already computes in float32 (selective precision), which the authors find insufficient at the largest scales.([Section 3.3, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.08906v2#page=7 "This penalizes large logits into the gating network and Section 3.4 contains a more detailed explanation of why the z-loss before the router is useful."))
- **c2** Stability vs quality: in the stability study (sparse models FLOP-matched to T5-XL, 32 experts, MoE every fourth FFN, mC4, 20k steps; 6 seeds for the baseline and 3 per technique), tightening Adafactor update clipping and the router z-loss both stabilized all 3 runs, but update clipping significantly hurt quality; the z-loss caused no quality degradation (a slight boost in this case). Removing GEGLU or RMS-norm scale parameters, input jitter and dropout also improved stability at a quality cost.([Section 3.3, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.08906v2#page=8 "Table 4 shows that both update clipping and the router z-loss stabilize the model in all 3 runs, but the update clipping signiﬁcantly hurts the model quality."))
- **c3** Roundoff explanation: the router adds exponential functions that can amplify bfloat16 roundoff errors (affecting second-expert routing and the probability scaling the expert output); the authors conjecture that the higher stability they observed for decoder-only models (not shown) was because those have fewer exponential functions.([Section 3.4, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.08906v2#page=9 "Finally, we conjecture that the higher stability we observed for decoder-only models (not shown here) was because they had fewer exponential functions."))
- **c4** Overfitting hypothesis: fine-tuning Dense-L and the roughly FLOP-matched ST-MoE-L (both pre-trained on 500B C4 tokens), the sparse model reaches 100% train accuracy faster on both tasks and clearly exceeds the dense model on ReCoRD (over 100,000 examples), but lags it on held-out data for CB (250 training examples). Higher expert dropout only moderately helps at this scale.([Section 4.1, p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.08906v2#page=10 "However, on the smaller task, CB, the sparse model lags its dense counterpart on held-out data."))
- **c5** Fine-tuning protocol: across the tested batch sizes and learning rates the sparse model outperformed the dense one, but sparse models benefit from smaller batch sizes and higher learning rates (dense models nearly the opposite); reusing fine-tuning hyperparameters that worked for the dense model can mask the pre-training improvements of the sparse model.([Section 4.3, p.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.08906v2#page=11 "Sparse models beneﬁt from smaller batch sizes and a higher learning rate."))
- **c6** Dropped tokens: varying the capacity factor and turning off the auxiliary loss during fine-tuning, SuperGLUE fine-tuning quality was not materially impacted by dropping up to 10-15% of tokens, and studies on ST-MoE-32B corroborate that high capacity factors do not improve fine-tuning quality; the authors note this is in line with Yang et al. (2021), that unequal load balance may not significantly impact quality.([Section 4.4, p.12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.08906v2#page=12 "Studies on ST-MoE-32B corroborate that high capacity factors do not improve ﬁne-tuning quality."))
- **c7** Routing and capacity factor (32-expert models FLOP-matched to T5-Large, 100k steps, 1M tokens/batch): increasing train and eval capacity factors improves quality but linearly increases einsum, activation-memory and communication costs; at fixed capacity factor there are small gains of top-(n+1) over top-n routing, which revises an earlier recommendation from Fedus et al. (2021), attributed to the 8x larger training scale here.([Section 5.2, p.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.08906v2#page=14 "This revises an earlier recommendation from Fedus et al. (2021)."))
- **c8** FLOP-matched comparison with dense T5-Large (both pre-trained 524B tokens on C4, fine-tuned on a task mixture): the sparse model improves dev results across many tasks, but the two smallest tasks CB and WSC are the only ones without gains, which the authors say again suggests improved regularization for sparse models may unleash greater performance.([Section 6, p.16](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.08906v2#page=16 "we observe two of the smallest tasks CB and WSC (250 and 259 training examples, respectively), are the only ones where the sparse model does not yield gains over its dense counterpart."))
