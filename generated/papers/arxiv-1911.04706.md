## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Problem framing: the cost of one trial is jointly decided by the learner, a subset of its hyperparameters, the training data size and the resampling strategy, which also jointly affect the trial error; the goal is to minimize the total cost before finding a model with the lowest test error.([Section 1, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.04706v3#page=1 "We recognize that the cost of one trial is jointly decided by the following variables: the choice of learner, a subset of the hyperparameters for the chosen learner, the size of the training data, and the resampling strategy."))
- **c2** FLAML deliberately does not rely on meta-learning or ensembles at first order, for usability reasons: no need to collect meta-training datasets, easy customization of learners/search spaces/metrics without re-running meta-learning, and the authors' customers preferring single learners for complexity, latency, deployment, debuggability and explainability.([Section 2, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.04706v3#page=3 "FLAML is designed to perform efﬁciently and robustly without relying on meta-learning or ensemble at ﬁrst order, for several usability reasons."))
- **c3** Step 0 (resampling): cross-validation is used if the training set has fewer than 100K instances and #instances x #features / budget is below 10M/hour, otherwise holdout; defaults are 5-fold cross-validation and a holdout ratio of 0.1. The authors note this simple rule could be replaced by more complex rules, e.g. from meta-learning.([Section 4.2, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.04706v3#page=6 "If the training dataset has fewer than 100K instances and # instances × # features / budget is smaller than 10M/hour, we use cross validation."))
- **c4** Step 1 (learner choice): each learner is sampled with probability proportional to 1/ECI rather than taking the minimum ECI, so every learner keeps a chance to be searched again and estimation errors are tolerated; ECI is updated dynamically, which the authors describe as self-correcting.([Section 4.2, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.04706v3#page=6 "With the concept of ECI introduced, we design a search strategy where each learner l is chosen with probability proportional to 1/ECI(l)."))
- **c5** Step 2 (hyperparameters and sample size): each learner starts at a small sample size (10K); if ECI1 >= ECI2 the current hyperparameters are kept and the sample size increased, otherwise randomized direct search proposes new hyperparameters at the current size. Once a learner reaches the full data size it keeps it, which the authors say reduces the risk of pruning good configurations compared to multi-fidelity pruning.([Section 4.2, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.04706v3#page=7 "Speciﬁcally, we begin with a small sample size (10K) for each l."))
- **c6** Protocol: 53 datasets (39 classification tasks from the AutoML Benchmark of Gijsbers et al. (2019) and 14 PMLB regression datasets with more than 10,000 instances), 10 OpenML folds averaged, 1 CPU core per solution on an Intel Xeon Gold 6140 server with 512GB RAM, budgets from one minute to one hour; scores are calibrated so a constant class-prior predictor is 0 and a tuned random forest is 1. Compared: auto-sklearn 0.9.0, H2O AutoML 3.30.0.3, TPOT 0.10.1, an unnamed commercial cloud-automl, and HpBandSter 0.7.4 (BOHB).([Section 5, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.04706v3#page=8 "We use 1 CPU core for each compared solution and vary the time budget from one minute to one hour."))
- **c7** Baseline configuration: all libraries use different search spaces by design except HpBandSter, which uses FLAML's search space and resampling strategy; FLAML's space neither subsumes nor is subsumed by the others', and the authors consider equalizing search spaces very challenging, if not impossible. Competitors are run with their default settings.([Section 5, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.04706v3#page=8 "We use their default setting and do not introduce our own bias."))
- **c8** Results framing and caveat: under equal budgets the authors report that FLAML clearly outperforms every competitor with large margins in most cases and underperforms by a small margin in a small fraction of cases; they add that the prior libraries are not primarily designed for the same low-resource setting.([Section 5.1, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.04706v3#page=8 "To be fair, the prior libraries are not primarily designed for the same low-resource setting as ours."))
