## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Claim about scaling methods: Platt scaling and temperature scaling are less calibrated than reported, and it is difficult to tell how miscalibrated they are; the authors argue that, if f(X) is continuous, estimating E[Y | f(X)] (needed for the calibration error) is impossible without smoothness assumptions that cannot be verified in practice.([Section 3 (Is Platt scaling calibrated?), p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.10155v2#page=4 "In this section, we show that methods like Platt scaling and temperature scaling are (i) less calibrated than reported and (ii) it is difﬁcult to tell how miscalibrated they are."))
- **c2** Multiclass definitions: the paper distinguishes top-label calibration error (top prediction only) from marginal calibration error (each class, one-vs-all, weighted), and notes that prior multiclass methods were evaluated only on top-label calibration; citing other work, temperature scaling is worse than vector scaling on per-class metrics despite better top-label calibration.([Section 2 (Setup and background), p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.10155v2#page=3 "Prior works [9, 15, 17] propose methods for multiclass calibration but only measure top-label calibration"))
- **c3** Binned estimates can severely underestimate calibration error: for any binning scheme there is a distribution where the binned calibration error is 0 while the true error is at least 0.49 (Example 3.2); binning never increases calibration error (Proposition 3.3). Both hold for any lp calibration error including ECE.([Section 3 (Is Platt scaling calibrated?), p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.10155v2#page=4 "A simple example shows that using binning to estimate the calibration error can severely underestimate the true calibration error."))
- **c4** Experiment protocol (ImageNet, VGG16): the ImageNet validation set was split into three sets, used respectively to recalibrate with Platt scaling, to select an equal-mass binning scheme, and to measure the binned calibration error, with 1,000-bootstrap 90% intervals. Because binning lower-bounds calibration error, finer bins give a tighter bound; measured error grew with the number of bins (the authors say with 15 bins as in Guo et al. one would think it is around 0.02 when it is at least twice as high), and Appendix C reportedly extends this to ECE and other binning strategies.([Section 3 (Experiments), p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.10155v2#page=5 "We split the validation set into 3 sets of sizes (20000, 5000, 25000)."))
- **c5** Scaling-binning calibrator guarantee (Theorem 4.1): with n of order B log B + log B / eps^2 samples, its squared calibration error is at most twice the best squared calibration error in the scaling family G plus eps^2, with probability 1 - delta; this assumes regularity conditions on G and requires splitting the recalibration data into three sets (function fitting, uniform-mass bin construction, discretization). The uniform-mass binning is described as essential for estimating the calibration error.([The scaling-binning calibrator, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.10155v2#page=6 "Assume regularity conditions on G (ﬁnite parameters, injectivity, Lipschitz-continuity, consistency, twice differentiability)."))
- **c6** Cost of binning: binning worsens sharpness and can increase the mean-squared error; the authors show (Proposition D.4) that with many bins the MSE cannot increase by much, and list better binning schemes as future work.([The scaling-binning calibrator (Analysis), p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.10155v2#page=6 "While binning worsens the sharpness and can increase the mean-squared error of the model, in Proposition D.4 we show that if we use many bins, binning the outputs cannot increase the mean-squared error by much."))
- **c7** Verification: citing older meteorology work, the plugin estimator of squared calibration error is biased and a debiased estimator subtracts an approximate correction term; the authors' contribution is to show the plugin estimator needs samples proportional to B while the debiased one needs samples proportional to sqrt(B), for binned models with a 2-well-balanced binning scheme. A debiasing heuristic for the l1 error (ECE) is proposed in Appendix G without a proof.([Section 5 (Verifying calibration), p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.10155v2#page=8 "Older work in meteorology [18, 19] notices that this is a biased estimate, and proposes a debiased estimator that subtracts off an approximate correction term to reduce the bias."))
- **c8** CIFAR-10 recalibration protocol: from the 10,000-point validation set, a recalibration set of 1,000 points was sampled with replacement; each class was recalibrated separately with B bins per class (sigmoid as the scaling function), marginal calibration error was measured on the entire 10K set, and the procedure was repeated 100 times. The authors report 35% lower calibration error than histogram binning at 100 bins per class.([The scaling-binning calibrator (Experiments), p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.10155v2#page=7 "We sampled, with replacement, a recalibration set of 1,000 points."))
- **c9** Open problem on dataset shift: with a small amount of labeled target data one can simply re-calibrate since only a few parameters are tuned, but the authors ask whether calibration can be maintained without labeled target examples.([Conclusion, p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.10155v2#page=10 "However, can we maintain calibration when we do not have labeled examples from the target dataset?"))
