## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Setup: instead of compressing, the student is parameterized identically to the teacher; after the teacher converges a new student is trained to predict the correct labels and match the teacher's output distribution. The authors report that applied to DenseNets, ResNets and LSTM-based sequence models, BANs consistently have lower validation errors than their teachers (the result tables report test error).([Introduction, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.04770v2#page=1 "We call these students Born-Again Networks (BANs) and show that applied to DenseNets, ResNets and LSTM-based sequence models, BANs consistently have lower validation errors than their teachers."))
- **c2** Training protocol: BAN re-training is done after convergence with the same training schedule originally used for the teacher. Other forms of transfer were tried (progressively constraining students to be similar to teachers, sharing first and last layers, L2 losses on activations), but these systematically performed slightly worse than simple KD via cross-entropy.([Section 4.1, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.04770v2#page=5 "However, we found these variations to systematically perform slightly worse than the simple KD via cross entropy."))
- **c3** Gradient analysis: the KD gradient splits into a dark-knowledge term from the wrong outputs and a term for the correct class that equals the ground-truth gradient rescaled by the teacher's confidence p_*, which resembles importance weighting of samples (citing Hinton et al., 2015 for the dark-knowledge explanation). The authors ask whether KD's success is due to the non-argmax outputs or simply to importance weighting.([Section 3.2, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.04770v2#page=4 "Or is dark knowledge simply performing a kind of importance weighting?"))
- **c4** Dissecting dark knowledge on CIFAR-100: CWTM (label loss weighted by the teacher's max output, no wrong-class information) gave weak improvements over the teacher in all but the largest DenseNet; DKPP (non-argmax teacher outputs permuted) gave a comparable but systematic improvement. The authors conclude KD does not simply contribute information on each specific non-correct output, and that CWTM's result suggests pre-trained models can be used to rebalance the training set.([Section 5.2, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.04770v2#page=7 "These results demonstrate that KD does not simply contribute information on each speciﬁc non-correct output."))
- **c5** Multiple generations: the k-th model is trained from the (k-1)-th, and Born-Again Network Ensembles average the predictions of several generations; the authors find the improvements of the sequence saturate but report significant gains through ensembling.([Section 3.1, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.04770v2#page=4 "We ﬁnd the improvements of the sequence to saturate, but we are able to produce signiﬁcant gains through ensembling."))
- **c6** Weak teachers: DenseNet students trained from Wide-ResNet teachers and ResNet students trained from a DenseNet teacher of comparable complexity were used to argue that weak masters can still improve students and that KD need not be used with strong masters.([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.04770v2#page=2 "Thus, we demonstrate that weak masters can still improve performance of students, and KD need not be used with strong masters."))
- **c7** Exceptions stated in the text: on CIFAR-100 all but one Wide-ResNet student improved over its identical teacher; for BAN-Wide-ResNets with a DenseNet teacher, limiting the number of blocks to 1 per stage led to inferior performance compared to the teacher (Section 5.2).([Section 5.2, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.04770v2#page=6 "In Table 3 all but one Wide-ResNnet student improve over their identical teacher."))
- **c8** Penn Tree Bank language models (single-layer 1500-unit LSTM and a CNN-LSTM; KD applied between softmax outputs of unrolled teacher and student): test perplexity decreased from 80.05 to 76.97 (CNN-LSTM) and from 71.87 to 68.56 (LSTM), without the state-of-the-art bag of tricks or sequence-level KD. Unlike the CIFAR CNNs, the LSTMs worked only with teacher outputs plus label loss (BAN+L); a potential explanation offered is that teachers generally reach 100% training accuracy on CIFAR while PTB training perplexity is far from minimized.([Section 5.3, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.04770v2#page=8 "Unlike the CNNs trained for CIFAR classiﬁcation, we ﬁnd that LSTM models work only when trained with a combination of teacher outputs and label loss (BAN+L)."))
- **c9** The authors state that their work is, to their knowledge, the first to show that dark knowledge applied for self-distillation, even without softening the logits, gives significant boosts in performance; they contrast it with Yim et al. (2017), whose identical-architecture transfer loss they say is not demonstrated to outperform more standard KD.([Section 2.1, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1805.04770v2#page=3 "Our work is the ﬁrst, to our knowledge, to demonstrate that dark knowledge, applied for self-distillation, even without softening the logits re-sults in signiﬁcant boosts in performance."))
