## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Two core designs: an asymmetric encoder-decoder (encoder on visible patches only, lightweight decoder with mask tokens) and a high masking ratio, e.g. 75%, which the authors find yields a nontrivial and meaningful self-supervisory task; together they accelerate training (by 3x or more) and improve accuracy.([Abstract, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.06377v3#page=1 "Second, we ﬁnd that masking a high proportion of the input image, e.g., 75%, yields a nontrivial and meaningful self-supervisory task."))
- **c2** Why a high ratio: images have heavy spatial redundancy (unlike information-dense language); random sampling with a high masking ratio largely eliminates redundancy so the task cannot be solved by extrapolating from visible neighbours. The optimal ratio (75%) is much higher than BERT's typical 15% and related vision works (20% to 50%).([Section 3 (Masking), p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.06377v3#page=3 "Random sampling with a high masking ratio (i.e., the ratio of removed patches) largely eliminates redundancy, thus creating a task that cannot be easily solved by extrapolation from visible neighboring patches (see Figures 2 – 4)."))
- **c3** Mask tokens in the encoder hurt: if the encoder uses mask tokens, linear probing accuracy drops by 14%; the authors explain that such an encoder sees many mask tokens in pre-training that never appear in uncorrupted images, a gap that may degrade accuracy in deployment. Skipping them also reduces training FLOPs by 3.3x.([Section 4.1 (Mask token), p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.06377v3#page=5 "This gap may degrade accuracy in deployment."))
- **c4** Masking ratio and protocol: linear probing and fine-tuning follow different trends; linear probing accuracy increases steadily with the masking ratio up to the sweet point, while fine-tuning is less sensitive and a wide range (40-80%) works well.([Section 4.1 (Masking ratio), p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.06377v3#page=4 "Figure 5 also shows that linear probing and ﬁne-tuning results follow different trends."))
- **c5** Decoder depth matters for linear probing but less for fine-tuning: the last layers of an autoencoder specialize in reconstruction, so a deeper decoder keeps the latent representation more abstract; under fine-tuning, the last encoder layers can adapt instead.([Section 4.1 (Decoder design), p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.06377v3#page=5 "A sufﬁciently deep decoder is important for linear probing."))
- **c6** Linear probing vs fine-tuning: Table 1 shows the two are largely uncorrelated; in partial fine-tuning (ViT-L), tuning a single Transformer block raises accuracy substantially over linear probing, and MoCo v3 has higher linear probing accuracy but worse partial fine-tuning results than MAE.([Section 4.3, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.06377v3#page=7 "While the MAE representations are less linearly separable, they are stronger non-linear features and perform well when a non-linear head is tuned."))
- **c7** The authors conclude that linear separability is not the sole metric of representation quality, citing [8] that linear probing is not well correlated with transfer performance (e.g. detection), and note that to their knowledge linear evaluation is not often used in NLP for benchmarking pre-training.([Section 4.3, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.06377v3#page=8 "These observations suggest that linear separability is not the sole metric for evaluating representation quality."))
- **c8** Linear probe recipe: follows MoCo v3 [9]; regularization (mixup, cutmix, drop path, color jitter, weight decay) is disabled as generally harmful; an extra BatchNorm without affine parameters is applied to the features before the linear classifier, which helps calibrate feature magnitudes so variants can share one setting without further lr search. Ablations report top-1 accuracy on the ImageNet-1K validation set.([Appendix A.1, p.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.06377v3#page=11 "Introducing this layer helps calibrate the feature magnitudes across different variants in our ablations, so that they can use the same setting without further lr search."))
- **c9** Augmentation: MAE works well with cropping-only augmentation (fixed or random size, with flipping), color jittering degrades results, and it behaves decently even with no augmentation; the authors contrast this with contrastive methods, citing [21] that cropping-only reduces BYOL and SimCLR accuracy, and attribute the regularizing role to random masking.([Section 4.1 (Data augmentation), p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.06377v3#page=6 "Our MAE works well using cropping-only augmentation, either ﬁxed-size or random-size (both having random horizontal ﬂipping)."))
- **c10** Tokens vs pixels: a dVAE-token target (as in BEiT) is not better than normalized pixels and reduces linear probing; the dVAE tokenizer needs an extra pre-training stage that may depend on extra data, and its encoder adds overhead.([Section 4.1 (Reconstruction target), p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.06377v3#page=6 "The dVAE tokenizer requires one more pre-training stage, which may depend on extra data (250M images [50])."))
- **c11** Compute: on 128 TPU-v3 cores, MAE ViT-L pre-training takes 31 hours for 1600 epochs versus 36 hours for 300 epochs of MoCo v3; pre-training is on the ImageNet-1K training set only.([Section 4.2, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.06377v3#page=7 "For example, training ViT-L on 128 TPU-v3 cores, our MAE’s training time is 31 hours for 1600 epochs and MoCo v3’s is 36 hours for 300 epochs [9]."))
