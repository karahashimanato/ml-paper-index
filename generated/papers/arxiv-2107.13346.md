## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Main claim: even under ignorability, empirical evaluations on semi-synthetic benchmarks can be misleading if the assumptions of the benchmark DGPs and their interplay with baseline algorithms are not discussed; for IHDP and ACIC2016 the inherent characteristics of the datasets favor some algorithms over others, which the authors say is rarely acknowledged.([Abstract, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.13346v1#page=1 "We identify problems with their current use and highlight that the inherent characteristics of the benchmark datasets favor some algorithms over others – a fact that is rarely acknowledged but of immense relevance for interpretation of empirical results."))
- **c2** Theoretical argument used to predict benchmark outcomes (citing [1, 9, 23, 25]): for indirect estimators (differencing two outcome regressions) the error is driven by the more complex of the two regression surfaces, while some direct estimators can attain the error rate of the effect function f_tau; direct learners thus have a theoretical advantage whenever f_tau is easier to estimate, which diminishes as f_1 and f_0 become less similar.([Section 2.2, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.13346v1#page=3 "direct learners thus have a clear theoretical advantage – which diminishes as f1(x) and f0(x) become less similar (making fτ(x) increasingly complex)."))
- **c3** Protocol: indirect estimators T-learner RF (TRF), S-learner RF (SRF), TNet and TARNet, and direct estimators Causal Forest and an NN-based R-learner (RNet), implemented with the R package grf and the python package catenets; all models were used off-the-shelf (2000 trees for RFs, NN hyperparameters similar to those of Shalit et al. for IHDP).([Section 3, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.13346v1#page=3 "We use all models off-the-shelf; for all RFs this entails using 2000 trees, and all NNs have hyperparameter settings similar to those used in [36] for the IHDP experiments."))
- **c4** IHDP reporting problem: the IHDP DGP (setup 'B' of Hill, 2011) fixes the ATT at 4 across runs but lets CATE vary freely, so the within-test-set spread of CATE ranges between 0.04 and 51.51 across the 100 simulations; simply averaging RMSE across runs gives algorithms that perform well mainly in the tails a clear advantage, and the authors suggest it might be more appropriate to use alternatives such as normalized RMSE or paired sample test statistics.([Section 3.1.1, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.13346v1#page=4 "The common practice to simply report an average RMSE score across all runs gives algorithms that perform well mainly in the tails a clear advantage; thus it might be more appropriate to consider alternative metrics (e.g. normalized RMSE or paired sample test statistics)."))
- **c5** IHDP conclusion: the advantage of TARNet over Causal Forest has multiple sources; the IHDP DGP favours NNs over RFs and indirect over direct estimators, and Causal Forest does worse on runs with extreme CATE spread, which get much more weight. When the DGP was changed so that the treatment effect is additive and linear, the relative performance reversed.([Section 3.1.2, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.13346v1#page=5 "When tweaking the DGP slightly to create a simple and additive treatment effect, the relative performance reverses, highlighting that performance is indeed determined by the interplay between assumptions underlying a learning algorithm and the DGP."))
- **c6** ACIC2016 hidden DGP choice: the simulated response surfaces are built not from the raw covariates but after the 27 count variables are dichotomized by the organizers (Dorie et al.); the authors describe this as the most natural pattern for an RF and found that feeding the transformed data improved the NNs substantially while the RFs did not change, concluding that some aspects of the DGP may inherently favor tree-based methods.([Section 3.2.1, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.13346v1#page=5 "We found that the simulated response surfaces are not created using the raw data as input, instead the 27 count variables are dichotomized by [12] before they are used in the DGP."))
- **c7** Composition of ACIC2016: of its 77 settings, only 2 have no treatment-effect heterogeneity, 32 low and 43 high heterogeneity, so the authors expect that indirect learners will generally be favoured on this benchmark on average.([Section 3.2.2, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.13346v1#page=6 "we would expect that – on average – indirect learners will generally be favoured on this benchmark."))
- **c8** Implications: because relative performance can change across DGPs/assumptions, assessments based on a single dataset/DGP such as IHDP capture only one setting, which the authors think warrants reconsidering the label 'state-of-the-art'.([Section 4, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.13346v1#page=6 "This means that performance assessments based on a single dataset/DGP (e.g. IHDP) capture only one speciﬁc setting of many conﬁgurations of possible drivers of relative performance."))
