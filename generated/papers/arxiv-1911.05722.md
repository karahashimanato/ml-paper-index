## 主張

- **c1** Hypothesis: contrastive dictionaries should be (i) large, to better sample the continuous high-dimensional visual space, and (ii) consistent, with keys represented by the same or similar encoder; existing contrastive mechanisms can be limited in one of these two aspects.([Introduction, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.05722v3#page=1 "From this perspective, we hypothesize that it is desirable to build dictionaries that are: (i) large and (ii) consistent as they evolve during training."))
- **c2** Queue: encoded keys of the current mini-batch are enqueued and the oldest are dequeued, so the dictionary size can be much larger than the mini-batch and is set as an independent hyper-parameter; the negatives come from the queue.([Section 3.2, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.05722v3#page=3 "The introduction of a queue decouples the dictionary size from the mini-batch size."))
- **c3** Momentum encoder: the key encoder cannot be updated by back-propagation through the queue; copying the query encoder yields poor results, which the authors hypothesize is caused by a rapidly changing encoder. A momentum update is used instead; a relatively large momentum (default 0.999) works much better than a smaller one (0.9), and with no momentum the training loss oscillates and fails to converge (Section 4.1).([Section 3.2, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.05722v3#page=3 "In experiments, a relatively large momentum (e.g., m = 0.999, our default) works much better than a smaller value (e.g., m = 0.9), suggesting that a slowly evolving key encoder is a core to making use of a queue."))
- **c4** Shuffling BN: with standard BN the model appears to cheat the pretext task and easily find a low-loss solution, possibly because intra-batch communication leaks information; shuffling the sample order for the key encoder across GPUs addresses this.([Section 3.3, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.05722v3#page=4 "The model appears to “cheat” the pretext task and easily ﬁnds a low-loss solution."))
- **c5** Linear classification protocol: unsupervised pre-training on ImageNet-1M (ResNet-50, batch 256 on 8 GPUs, 200 epochs, about 53 hours; augmentation: random resized crop, color jitter, horizontal flip, grayscale; temperature 0.07), then a linear classifier on frozen global-average-pooled features for 100 epochs, reporting 1-crop top-1 on the ImageNet validation set. The classifier's learning rate (30) and weight decay (0) came from a grid search; the data used for that search is not stated (searched for grid, validation, held out).([Section 4.1, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.05722v3#page=5 "For this classiﬁer, we perform a grid search and ﬁnd the optimal initial learning rate is 30 and weight decay is 0 (similarly reported in [56])."))
- **c6** Mechanism ablation (same pretext task and InfoNCE loss): all three mechanisms (end-to-end, memory bank, MoCo) benefit from a larger number of negatives K. End-to-end is limited by the mini-batch size (1024 on 8 Volta 32GB GPUs) and needs linear learning-rate scaling; the memory bank (the authors' improved reimplementation) supports a large dictionary but is 2.6% worse than MoCo, attributed to inconsistent keys.([Section 4.1, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.05722v3#page=5 "Overall, all three mecha-nisms beneﬁt from a larger K."))
- **c7** Transfer protocol: for detection/segmentation fine-tuning, MoCo uses the same hyper-parameters and schedule as the ImageNet supervised counterpart (with BN fine-tuned and synchronized), which the authors note may place MoCo at a disadvantage but avoids extra hyper-parameter search; random-initialization results are given as references.([Section 4.2, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.05722v3#page=7 "This may place MoCo at a disadvantage."))
- **c8** Negative case: MoCo is worse than supervised pre-training on VOC semantic segmentation (by at least 0.8 point) and on par on Cityscapes instance segmentation, while it outperforms supervised pre-training in 7 detection or segmentation tasks.([Section 4.2 (More Downstream Tasks), p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.05722v3#page=8 "But on VOC semantic segmentation, MoCo is worse by at least 0.8 point, a negative case we have observed."))
- **c9** Caveat on protocols: linear classification on frozen features is a common protocol for evaluating unsupervised pre-training, but in practice it is more common to fine-tune end-to-end; the authors add ImageNet fine-tuning results and note that pre-training on IN-1M and fine-tuning on ImageNet does not represent a real scenario.([Appendix A.7, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1911.05722v3#page=9 "However, in practice, it is more common to ﬁne-tune the features end-to-end in a downstream task."))
