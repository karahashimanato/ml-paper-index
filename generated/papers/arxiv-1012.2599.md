## 主張

- **c1** The acquisition function automatically trades off exploration (where the objective is very uncertain) and exploitation (where it is expected to be high); the technique aims to minimize the number of objective evaluations and, the authors say, is likely to do well even with multiple local maxima.([Introduction, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1012.2599v1#page=3 "This optimization technique has the nice property that it aims to minimize the number of objective function evaluations."))
- **c2** Problem assumptions: the objective is a black box, assumed Lipschitz-continuous with a typically unknown constant, over a bounded axis-aligned hyperrectangle. Citing Betro (1991), guaranteeing an epsilon-optimal observation in the worst case requires (C/2 epsilon)^d samples, which the authors call an incredibly expensive premium against unlikely scenarios; BO instead relaxes worst-case guarantees toward an average-case approach.([Section 2 (The Bayesian Optimization Approach), p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1012.2599v1#page=5 "This can be an incredibly expensive premium to pay for insurance against unlikely scenarios."))
- **c3** The choice of GP covariance function is crucial because it determines the smoothness of samples; the plain squared exponential is called a little naive since all features affect the covariance equally, so length-scale (including ARD) hyperparameters are added, and the Matern kernel adds a smoothness parameter.([Section 2.2, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1012.2599v1#page=9 "The choice of covariance function for the Gaussian Process is crucial, as it determines the smoothness properties of samples drawn from it."))
- **c4** GP hyperparameters are typically learned by seeding with a few random samples and maximizing the log-likelihood of the evidence (citing several works), often aided by an informative hyperprior such as a log-normal prior; more efficient ways of learning them are described as an active subfield of research.([Section 2.2, p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1012.2599v1#page=10 "Typically, the hyperparameter values are learned by “seeding” with a few random samples and maximizing the log-likelihood of the evidence given θ"))
- **c5** Exploration parameter of EI: citing Lizotte (2008), setting xi = 0.01 (scaled by the signal variance if necessary) works well in almost all cases, while a cooling schedule for xi (explore early, exploit later) does not work well empirically, contrary to intuition (a schedule might slightly help short runs of PI).([Section 2.3, p.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1012.2599v1#page=14 "Lizotte’s experiments suggest that setting ξ = 0.01 (scaled by the signal variance if necessary) works well in almost all cases"))
- **c6** GP-UCB as summarized by the tutorial: for the confidence-bound criteria kappa is otherwise left to the user; with the schedule of Srinivas et al. (2010) the method is no-regret with high probability. A footnote cautions that these bounds hold for reasonably smooth kernels and their exact form depends on the kernel, referring readers to the original paper.([Section 2.3 (footnote 2), p.16](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1012.2599v1#page=16 "These bounds hold for reasonably smooth kernel functions, where the exact formulation of the bounds depends upon the form of kernel used."))
- **c7** Noise: additive Gaussian noise is handled by adding sigma_noise^2 I to the kernel matrix; for PI and EI the incumbent is redefined as the sampled point with the highest posterior mean rather than the best noisy observation (resampling incumbents, 'intensification', is also mentioned).([Section 2.4, p.19](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1012.2599v1#page=19 "This avoids the problem of attempting to maximize probability or expected improvement over an unreliable sample."))
- **c8** Stated shortcomings: the design of the prior is absolutely critical; GPs are not always the best or easiest solution and kernels need great care; with little known about an expensive objective, either strong unverified assumptions or a weak prior must be used; and it is often unclear how to set the exploration-exploitation trade-off.([Section 5, p.43](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1012.2599v1#page=43 "A particular issue is that the design of the prior is absolutely critical to eﬃcient Bayesian optimization."))
- **c9** Further shortcomings: these problems are exacerbated as dimensionality grows (more samples to cover the space, more parameters and hyperparameters to tune), which may require automatic feature selection or optimizing each dimension independently; and the acquisition is currently myopic and allows only one sample per iteration, whereas looking ahead to a horizon or selecting batches would be valuable.([Section 5, p.43](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1012.2599v1#page=43 "In order to deal with this problem eﬀectively, it may be necessary to do automatic feature selection, or assume independence and optimize each dimension individually."))
