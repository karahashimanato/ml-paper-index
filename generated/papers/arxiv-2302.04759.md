## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Motivation: BOCD inherits from Bayesian inference a lack of robustness to outliers or model misspecification, which can lead to failures where most points inferred to be CPs are simply mild heterogeneities in the data (Figure 1: standard BOCD flags the Twitter flash-crash blip in the DJIA as a CP, Dm-BOCD does not).([Introduction, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.04759v2#page=1 "This can lead to failures, where most data points inferred to be CPs are simply mild heterogeneities in the data."))
- **c2** Critique of β-BOCD (Knoblauch et al., 2018, which shares the last author): the β-divergence loss contains an integral that is intractable unless available in closed form; β enters as the exponent of a likelihood, which makes the loss extremely sensitive to very small changes in β and hence very difficult to tune; and the posteriors often need variational approximations for every run length at every step, which the authors say often renders it computationally infeasible, especially in high dimensions.([Section 2.3, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.04759v2#page=3 "Numerically, this makes the loss extremely sensitive to even very minor changes in β, which makes it very difﬁcult to tune β and counteracts the very robustness one hopes to achieve."))
- **c3** Scalability: for natural-form exponential family likelihoods with a squared exponential prior the diffusion-score-matching posterior is a (truncated) normal whose parameters are updated exactly by adding terms per observation; with the Sherman-Morrison formula each update costs O(p^2 + d^2), which the authors say is also the complexity of standard BOCD with a Gaussian likelihood and conjugate prior.([Section 3.4, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.04759v2#page=6 "This is also the complexity of standard BOCD with the Gaussian likelihood and conjugate prior (Adams & MacKay, 2007)."))
- **c4** Robustness guarantee and its conditions: Proposition 3.2 gives a diffusion matrix m (depending on a fixed θ* ≠ 0, not on θ) that makes the posterior globally bias-robust when η(θ) = θ and ∇b = 0 with a squared exponential prior. The authors note this m applies only to models with support R^d unless additional boundary conditions hold (appendix shows it also works for some distributions on subsets, e.g. Gamma and exponential).([Section 3.3, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.04759v2#page=6 "Note also that m is only applicable to models with support X = Rd, as the expansion in (3) is otherwise not valid without additional boundary conditions."))
- **c5** Choice of θ*: the authors found Dm-BOCD not very sensitive to θ*, likely because tuning ω offsets any sensitivity; in all experiments θ* was set to the maximum likelihood estimate computed on the full data set (i.e. using the whole stream, not only past data).([Section 3.4 (Choice of m), p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.04759v2#page=6 "In all experiments, we thus picked θ⋆as the maximum likelihood estimate computed on the full data set."))
- **c6** Choice of the learning rate ω: existing methods are computationally expensive and asymptotically motivated, so ω* minimizes the KL divergence between the Dm-posterior and the standard Bayes posterior on the first t* observations (computed with automatic differentiation in jax). The authors say this yields reasonable uncertainty quantification if misspecification is mild at the beginning of the stream.([Section 3.4 (Choice of ω), p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.04759v2#page=7 "we match the uncertainty of the generalised posterior to that of its standard counterpart on the ﬁrst t⋆observations of the data stream."))
- **c7** Detection latency hedge: the authors note a known issue of robust CP methods is latency in detecting actual CPs, and say this is not something they observe in their experiments with their choice of m and θ*.([Section 3.4 (Choice of m), p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.04759v2#page=7 "known issue with robust CP detection method is that they can experience a latency when it comes to detecting actual CPs."))
- **c8** Accuracy/delay protocol: synthetic data with 600 samples, 2% outliers and 6 CPs, 10 realisations varying the outlier positions; PPV, TPR and detection delay are reported for Dm-BOCD and standard BOCD only. A detection counts as a true positive if it lies in a small neighbourhood of the true CP; no numeric tolerance was found in the text (searched for neighbourhood, margin, tolerance). No hazard (changepoint prior) value was found in the text (searched for hazard, CP prior).([Appendix C.3, p.20](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.04759v2#page=20 "We say the method detects a TP changepoint when the changepoint detected for the method is in a small neighbourhood of the true CP."))
- **c9** Well-log segmentation figure (appendix): the caption states that Dm-BOCD is more robust to outliers than standard BOCD but more sensitive than β-BOCD.([Appendix C (Figure 13 caption), p.22](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.04759v2#page=22 "We observe that our method is more robust to outliers than the standard BOCD, but is more sensitive than β-BOCD."))
