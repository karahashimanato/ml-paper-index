- **c1** The BYOL objective admits collapsed solutions (e.g. the same output for every image); the authors show empirically that BYOL does not converge to them and hypothesize that the combination of the predictor and the slow-moving-average target network avoids collapse.([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.07733v3#page=2 "While this objective admits collapsed solutions, e.g., outputting the same vector for all images, we empirically show that BYOL does not converge to such solutions."))
- **c2** Status of the explanation: BYOL's dynamics still admit undesirable equilibria; the authors did not observe convergence to them, and their argument that collapsed equilibria are unstable assumes an optimal predictor (and, for simplicity, no normalization or symmetrization).([Section 3.2, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.07733v3#page=4 "While BYOL’s dynamics still admit undesirable equilibria, we did not observe convergence to such equilibria in our experiments."))
- **c3** Hypothesis on the role of the moving-average target: to keep the predictor near-optimal during training; the authors say Section 5 and Appendix I give some empirical support for this interpretation.([Section 3.2, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.07733v3#page=5 "We hypothesize that the main role of BYOL’s moving-averaged target network is to ensure the near-optimality of the predictor over training; Section 5 and Appendix I provide some empirical support of this interpretation."))
- **c4** Ablation of the target decay rate (300-epoch runs, linear evaluation): updating the target instantaneously (tau = 0) destabilizes training, while never updating it (tau = 1) is stable but gives a low-quality representation; a trade-off exists between the two.([Section 5 (Bootstrapping), p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.07733v3#page=8 "Instantaneously updating the target network (τ = 0) destabilizes training, yielding very poor performance while never updating the target (τ = 1) makes the training stable but prevents iterative improvement, ending with low-quality ﬁnal representation."))
- **c5** Ablation between BYOL and SimCLR (Table 5b): without negative examples (beta = 0) the only variant that performs well is BYOL with both a target network and a predictor; adding negatives to BYOL without re-tuning the temperature hurts it.([Section 5 (Ablation to contrastive methods), p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.07733v3#page=9 "The only variant that performs well without negative examples (i.e., with β = 0) is BYOL, using both a bootstrap target network and a predictor."))
- **c6** Without a target network, collapse could be avoided by making the predictor near-optimal (an optimal linear predictor fitted on the batch, or a higher predictor learning rate); the authors say this seems to indicate that keeping the predictor near-optimal is important to prevent collapse, which may be one role of the target network.([Section 5 (Importance of a near-optimal predictor), p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.07733v3#page=10 "This seems to indicate that keeping the predictor near-optimal at all times is important to preventing collapse, which may be one of the roles of BYOL’s target network."))
- **c7** Batch size (Figure 3a, 128 to 4096, 300 epochs): BYOL stays stable from 256 to 4096 and drops only for smaller values, which the authors attribute to batch normalization. To avoid re-tuning, smaller batches were emulated by averaging gradients over N steps, so only the batch-normalization statistics see the smaller batch.([Section 5 (Batch size), p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.07733v3#page=8 "In contrast, the performance of BYOL remains stable over a wide range of batch sizes from 256 to 4096, and only drops for smaller values due to batch normalization layers in the encoder."))
- **c8** Linear evaluation protocol on ImageNet: linear classifier on the frozen representation (no update of weights or batch statistics), SGD with Nesterov momentum for 80 epochs without regularization; the learning rate is chosen from 5 values on a local validation set of 10009 images held out from the ImageNet train set, and accuracy is reported on the public ILSVRC2012 validation set as test set.([Appendix C.1, p.17](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.07733v3#page=17 "We ﬁnally sweep over 5 learning rates {0.4, 0.3, 0.2, 0.1, 0.05} on a local validation set (10009 images from ImageNet train set), and report the accuracy of the best validation hyperparameter on the test set"))
- **c9** Stated limitation: BYOL depends on augmentation sets specific to vision; other modalities would need similarly suitable augmentations, whose design may require significant effort and expertise.([Conclusion, p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.07733v3#page=10 "Nevertheless, BYOL remains dependent on existing sets of augmentations that are speciﬁc to vision applications."))
