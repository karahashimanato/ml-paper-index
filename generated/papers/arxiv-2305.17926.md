## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Positional bias: with the same evaluation template (from Zheng et al., 2023), GPT-4 tends to favor the response in the first position while ChatGPT prefers the second. For 'Vicuna-13B vs. ChatGPT', GPT-4 gives Vicuna-13B win rates of 51.3% as Assistant 1 and 23.8% as Assistant 2, while ChatGPT gives 2.5% and 82.5%.([Revealing the Positional Bias, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.17926v2#page=3 "Based on the same evaluation template T in Table 1, GPT-4 tends to favor the response in the first position, while ChatGPT shows a preference for the response in the second position."))
- **c2** Conflict Rate is defined as the fraction of N examples where querying with T(q, r1, r2) and T(q, r2, r1) gives different evaluation results. GPT-4 had conflict rates of 46.3% (Vicuna-13B vs. ChatGPT) and 5.0% (Vicuna-13B vs. Alpaca-13B), ChatGPT 82.5% and 52.5%; the authors read this as LLMs being self-conflicting due to order sensitivity, with stronger models less influenced.([Revealing the Positional Bias, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.17926v2#page=3 "These findings indicate that LLMs can be self-conflicting due to the sensitivity of the response order in the template, with stronger models being less influenced by the placement of responses."))
- **c3** The degree of positional bias varies with the quality difference: the lower conflict rate for 'Vicuna-13B vs. Alpaca-13B' suggests positional bias may not affect all assessments equally (one potential reason given is the large quality gap between Alpaca and Vicuna). Grouping examples by score difference, GPT-4's results are significantly affected by position when the gap is small (e.g. <= 1) and relatively stable when it is large (e.g. >= 3).([Revealing the Positional Bias, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.17926v2#page=4 "On the other hand, when the score difference between the two responses is large (e.g., score gap ≥3), GPT-4’s evaluation results are relatively stable."))
- **c4** Multiple Evidence Calibration: previous templates (Zheng et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2023b) draw the conclusion first and explain afterwards; because of the auto-regressive nature, that conclusion is not supported by the later explanation, so the evidence-calibration template asks for the explanation (evaluation evidence) first and then the scores, and MEC samples k such scores per response.([Multiple Evidence Calibration, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.17926v2#page=4 "However, due to the nature of the auto-regressive model, the conclusions generated by the model are not supported by the explanation generated afterward."))
- **c5** Balanced Position Calibration additionally queries with the two responses swapped; combined with MEC this gives 2k scores per response, whose average is the calibrated score, and the response with the higher average score is regarded as better.([Balanced Position Calibration, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.17926v2#page=5 "and we regard the response with the higher average score as the better response."))
- **c6** Human-in-the-Loop Calibration (inspired by Cai, Chang, and Han, 2023) computes the Balanced Position Diversity Entropy, the entropy of the win/tie/lose distribution over the 2k MEC+BPC evaluation results; a higher BPDE means the evaluation more likely needs manual correction, and a threshold (hyper-parameter) selects the top-beta most likely biased examples for human annotation.([Human-in-the-Loop Calibration, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.17926v2#page=5 "A higher BPDE score indicates that it is more likely the evaluation requires manual correction."))
- **c7** Human annotation protocol: three of the authors (researchers familiar with AI) independently annotated win/tie/lose for ChatGPT vs. Vicuna-13B responses on all 80 Vicuna Benchmark questions, judging helpfulness, relevance, accuracy and level of detail, with responses shown in random order (about three minutes per example); the final label is the majority of the three annotators.([Section 4.1, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.17926v2#page=5 "three of the authors manually annotate the “win/tie/lose” outcomes of responses from ChatGPT and Vicuna-13B independently in all 80 Vicuna Benchmark questions."))
- **c8** Main results stated in the text: individual human annotators reach on average 71.7% accuracy and 0.54 kappa against the majority; with MEC and BPC, ChatGPT's accuracy improves by 14.3% and kappa rises from 0.06 to 0.31; 'MEC (k = 3) + BPC (k = 3)' outperforms 'MEC (k = 6)', which the authors take as showing that LLMs are affected by positional bias; with 20% human assistance (HITLC), ChatGPT attains accuracy comparable to the Human Average while cost falls from $30 to $18.3 (cost based on a near-$7.5 US minimum hourly wage and 3 minutes per example).([Main Results, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.17926v2#page=6 "Specifically, the accuracy is improved by 14.3%, and the kappa correlation coefficient is increased from 0.06 to 0.31;"))
