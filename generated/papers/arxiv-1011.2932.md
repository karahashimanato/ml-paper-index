## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Model assumptions: a changepoint at t means y_1..y_t and y_{t+1}..y_n are generated differently; conditional on a segmentation the data within each segment are independent, segment parameters are independent across segments, and a prior on the changepoint configuration (hyperparameter xi) and a prior on the number of changepoints k complete a hierarchical model similar to Green (1995).([Section 2, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.2932v1#page=2 "Conditional on a segmentation, the data within each segment is assumed independent."))
- **c2** Collapsing: with conjugate priors the segment parameters are integrated out, so the posterior over (k, z) is a product of closed-form segment marginal likelihoods and a dimension change needs no bijection (unlike RJMCMC). The first term of the acceptance ratio is the Bayes factor between the k+1 and k changepoint configurations, so sampling is a model search over a large space (the authors note up to 5 changepoints in a series of length 200 gives a space of dimension about 2.5 x 10^9).([Section 3, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.2932v1#page=4 "Noting this, it becomes apparent that sampling k and z is equivalent to a model search over large model space."))
- **c3** Sampler moves and cost: each sweep adds or deletes a changepoint (acceptance needs at most three marginal likelihoods) and moves a changepoint within its neighbours. The Gibbs position update costs O(tau_{j+1} - tau_{j-1}) and may be expensive for long series with distant changepoints or many changepoints, in which case a local random walk may be preferred; the authors suggest a mixture of the two should improve mixing.([Section 4, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.2932v1#page=5 "In this situation a local random walk update may be preferred."))
- **c4** Prior sensitivity: with a geometric segment-duration prior, the choice of p may impact the analysis: too small and small changes cannot be detected with high power, too large and spurious changepoints are inferred. A hyperprior on p (e.g. Beta, or non-informative Beta(1,1)) and on the segment hyperparameters gamma is therefore introduced and sampled within the MCMC sweep.([Section 5, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.2932v1#page=6 "If too large, then spurious changepoints are inferred."))
- **c5** Exact filtering recursions (Fearnhead 2006) give independent posterior samples and fast sampling, but their main weakness is that samples are conditional on fixed hyperparameters; updating gamma under a hyperprior would require recomputing recursions that are quadratic in n for each new value, which the authors describe as infeasible for any reasonably large posterior sample.([Section 6, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.2932v1#page=7 "The main weakness of this approach is that the generated samples are dependent on a ﬁxed value of the hyperparameters γ."))
- **c6** Validation on the coal-mining disasters data (Poisson model, weekly discretisation, Gamma(1, 200/7) intensity prior, the Fearnhead (2006) order-statistics position prior): 500,000 sweeps after 10,000 burn-in, keeping every 50th sample, took 10 seconds on a 2.5GHz processor; the posterior number of changepoints is described as almost identical to long RJMCMC runs and recursion-based methods (comparison shown in a figure).([Section 7, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.2932v1#page=7 "This took 10 seconds on a 2.5GHz processor."))
- **c7** Streakiness data (Bernoulli segments, Beta(1,1) success prior, geometric segment lengths with a uniform hyperprior on p, 100 sampler runs): the modal number of changepoints from filtering recursions computed over a grid of p is very sensitive to p, whereas the sampler with a hyperprior suggests two changepoints is most likely with relatively strong support for up to five; the authors question whether a fixed-p analysis would be useful to a practitioner.([Streakiness in sports, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.2932v1#page=9 "It is clear that the number of changepoints inferred in the ﬁltering recursions analysis is very sensitive to the value of p for this data."))
- **c8** Well-log data (outliers removed before analysis; Gaussian piecewise-constant mean with common variance): the modal number of changepoints from filtering recursions is sensitive to both p and sigma over small grids. The Fearnhead (2006) independence-proposal MCMC built from recursions at fixed hyperparameters had a high acceptance rate but a highly degenerate proposal, which the authors say could lead to highly biased sampling on other datasets.([Application to Well-log data, p.12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.2932v1#page=12 "Only ten unique changepoint conﬁgurations were sampled in the 50,000 iterations of the MCMC scheme."))
- **c9** Conclusion of the comparison with a cost caveat: the proposed sampler explores the posterior with hyperpriors better than the independence proposal, but generating independent samples may be more costly in large datasets with many changepoints; including hyperpriors circumvents the sensitivity of the changepoint posterior to hyperparameter choices, which the authors call the main advantage.([Application to Well-log data, p.12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1011.2932v1#page=12 "Nonetheless, it is clear that the inclusion of hyperpriors circumvents the sensitivity of posterior distribution of the changepoints to speciﬁcation of the hyperparameters."))
