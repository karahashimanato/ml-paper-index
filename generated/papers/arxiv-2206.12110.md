## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Contribution (classical structure: treap / binary search tree): node priorities of a treap are set from a frequency-estimation (rank-prediction) oracle instead of random draws, so frequently accessed keys sit near the root while BST operations such as range queries and successor remain supported.([Abstract, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.12110v1#page=1 "Given recent advances in algorithms with predictions, we propose pairing treaps with machine advice to form a learning-augmented treap."))
- **c2** Guarantee with a perfect oracle (Theorem 3.1): the expected depth of the i-th most frequent key is 2H_i - 1; the analysis assumes a perfect rank oracle, that the rank order of the keys is a random permutation, that the input distribution is the same throughout the query sequence and that observed frequencies exactly reflect it, and unique frequencies (ties broken randomly). Section 3.6 removes the random-order assumption by remapping keys to random reals (with a 4-wise independent hash version giving expected depth O(log i) rather than 2H_i - 1).([Access, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.12110v1#page=4 "The expected depth of ei in a learned Treap is 2Hi−1, where Hi is the i-th Harmonic number."))
- **c3** Zipfian inputs (theoretical, under the perfect-oracle and random-rank-order assumptions of Section 3): for Zipf parameter alpha >= 1 the learned treap is statically optimal in expectation, i.e. within a constant factor of the entropy lower bound; for alpha > 1 the expected access cost is constant, and for alpha = 1 it is approximately a factor 2 less than for a random treap (Corollary 3.9).([Zipfian Distributions, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.12110v1#page=8 "Theorem 3.11. The learned Treap is statically optimal in expectation for α ≥1."))
- **c4** Robustness to noisy ranks (theoretical, Theorem 3.12): if predicted ranks satisfy r_hat <= eps*r + delta for constants eps, delta >= 1 (a 'noisy' oracle), the learned treap's expected access cost is at most an additive constant (2(1 + ln(eps + delta))) worse than with a perfect oracle, still under the Section 3 assumptions.([Noisy Oracles and Robustness to Errors, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.12110v1#page=8 "Therefore, under a noisy oracle, the learned Treap is at most an additive constant worse than a learned Treap with a perfect oracle."))
- **c5** Worst case is conditional (Theorem 3.13): with an inaccurate but non-adversarial oracle (defined as one that, for a distribution with a random rank ordering, outputs a random rank ordering) the learned treap's expected performance is no worse than a random treap's, up to a small additive constant. The guarantee does not cover adversarial oracles; the paper motivates the random-remapping modification of Section 3.6 partly by the possibility that the oracle is adversarial.([Noisy Oracles and Robustness to Errors, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.12110v1#page=9 "A learned Treap based on an inaccurate but non-adversarial oracle has expected performance no worse than that of a random Treap, up to a small additive constant."))
- **c6** Protocol (empirical): baselines are Red-Black Trees, AVL Trees, Splay Trees, B-Trees of order 3 and random Treaps; only query operations are considered and the total number of comparisons (not runtime) is reported; insertion-order-sensitive trees get keys in random order. The authors note comparisons are not a precise runtime measure (for Splay Trees they expect a constant factor more runtime due to rotations).([Experiments, p.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.12110v1#page=11 "For these experiments, we only consider query operations and report the total number of comparisons made by each data structure."))
- **c7** Real-data limitation (empirical): on CAIDA internet traffic with the learned frequency oracle of Hsu et al. [HIKV19], the authors were unable to beat Red-Black Trees, although the shuffled learned treap (keys remapped to a random permutation) was comparable; they say that with a better oracle it could be possible to outperform a Red-Black Tree.([Internet Traffic Data, p.13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.12110v1#page=13 "We note that using the oracle from [HIKV19], we are unable to beat Red-Black Trees; however, the shuﬄed learned Treap with the learned oracle is comparable and with a better oracle, it could be possible to outperform a Red-Black Tree."))
