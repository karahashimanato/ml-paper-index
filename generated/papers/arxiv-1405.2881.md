## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Contribution: a consistency result for Breiman's original random forest algorithm in the context of additive regression models, with insight into how forests adapt to sparsity.([Abstract, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.2881v4#page=1 "In the present paper, we take a step forward in forest exploration by proving a consistency result for Breiman’s [Mach. Learn. 45 (2001) 5–32] original algorithm in the context of additive regression models."))
- **c2** Modification of the algorithm analysed: each tree uses a_n points drawn without replacement instead of a bootstrap sample of n points drawn with replacement; the authors state this is harmless and only avoids the mathematical difficulties of the bootstrap. Trees stop when the number of cells reaches t_n.([Section 2, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.2881v4#page=6 "is harmless—in fact, it is just a means to avoid mathematical diﬃculties induced by the bootstrap"))
- **c3** Assumption (H1): Y is a sum of continuous univariate functions of the coordinates plus independent centered Gaussian noise, with X uniform on [0,1]^p; the authors say the analysis is facilitated within additive models.([Section 3, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.2881v4#page=7 "Although random forests fall into the family of nonparametric procedures, it turns out that the analysis of their properties is facilitated within the framework of additive models."))
- **c4** Theorem 1 (under H1): forests are consistent if a_n -> infinity, t_n -> infinity and t_n (log a_n)^9 / a_n -> 0; it still holds with a_n = n (subsampling plays no role, depth control suffices), and an easy adaptation shows CART is consistent under the same assumptions.([Section 3, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.2881v4#page=7 "We note in passing that an easy adaptation of Theorem 1 shows that the CART algorithm is consistent under the same assumptions."))
- **c5** Theorem 2 (fully grown trees, t_n = a_n) additionally requires (H2), a condition on how Y-values influence connection probabilities; it holds when the partition is independent of the Y_i or without noise, but in general the authors do not know whether (H2) holds.([Section 3, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.2881v4#page=9 "However, in the most general context, the partitions strongly depend on the whole sample Dn, and unfortunately, we do not know whether or not (H2) is satisﬁed."))
- **c6** The authors state that, apart from replacing bootstrapping by subsampling, Theorems 1 and 2 are the first consistency results for Breiman's forests, since most previously studied models are built independently of the data.([Section 3, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.2881v4#page=9 "To our knowledge, apart from the fact that bootstrapping is replaced by subsampling, Theorems 1 and 2 are the ﬁrst consistency results for Breiman’s (2001) forests."))
- **c7** In Theorem 2 the estimation error is controlled by a subsampling rate a_n/n = o(1/log n) (a_n/n -> 0 with bounded noise), ensuring a query point is not connected to the same observation in many trees; subsampling is the key component imposing tree diversity, and the individual one-point-per-leaf trees are not consistent.([Section 4, p.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.2881v4#page=11 "In our approach, the subsampling is the key component for imposing tree diversity."))
- **c8** Proposition 1 (H1, mtry = p, no strong component constant on any interval): with probability 1 - xi, for n large enough, the first k cuts are along the S informative variables, so forests behave as if data were projected onto those variables.([Section 3, p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.2881v4#page=10 "Since the algorithm selects splits mostly along informative variables, everything happens as if data were projected onto the vector space generated by the S informative variables."))
- **c9** Limitation: the analysis does not carry over to high dimensions with p = p_n growing with n (possibly larger than n); (H2.1) may then be too restrictive.([Section 3, p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1405.2881v4#page=10 "Unfortunately, our analysis does not carry over to this context."))
