## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Definition used by this paper: an ability is emergent if it is not present in smaller models but is present in larger models; the paper says such abilities therefore cannot be predicted simply by extrapolating smaller models' performance.([Abstract, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.07682v2#page=1 "We consider an ability to be emergent if it is not present in smaller models but is present in larger models."))
- **c2** Operational pattern: on a scaling curve (model scale vs performance), the paper describes emergent abilities as near-random performance until a critical threshold of scale, after which performance rises substantially above random (it calls this a phase transition).([Emergent Abilities Definition, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.07682v2#page=2 "When visualized via a scaling curve (x-axis: model scale, y-axis: performance), emergent abilities show a clear pattern—performance is near-random until a certain critical threshold of scale is reached, after which performance increases to substantially above random."))
- **c3** Scale measure: scaling curves use training compute in FLOPs on the x-axis (parameter-count plots are given in an appendix); the paper says training dataset size is not plotted because many model families use a fixed number of training examples for all sizes, and that no single proxy captures all aspects of scale.([Emergent Abilities Definition, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.07682v2#page=2 "In this paper, we will analyze scaling curves by plotting the performance of diﬀerent models where training compute for each model is measured in FLOPs on the x-axis (Hoﬀmann et al., 2022)."))
- **c4** Nature of the evidence: the paper is a survey of results in the existing literature and does not propose new methods or models (the surveyed examples include model families such as GPT-3, LaMDA, Gopher, Chinchilla and PaLM; the paper adds its own cross-entropy re-analysis of six BIG-Bench tasks, see c7).([Broader Impact Statement, p.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.07682v2#page=11 "In this paper, we surveyed results in the existing literature, without proposing new methods or models."))
- **c5** Scope caveat: the authors state that the scale at which an ability emerges is not an immutable property (e.g. emergence may occur with less training compute or fewer parameters for models trained on higher-quality data; today's models are likely not trained optimally), and that they do not claim a specific scale is required.([Emergent Abilities Definition, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.07682v2#page=3 "Our goal in this paper is not to characterize or claim that a speciﬁc scale is required to observe emergent abilities, but rather, we aim to discuss examples of emergent behavior in prior work."))
- **c6** Metric caveat stated by the authors themselves (citing BIG-Bench): exact string match on long targets may disguise compounding incremental improvements as emergence; but they argue metrics without partial credit are at best an incomplete explanation because emergence is also observed on many classification tasks.([Potential explanations of emergence (Discussion), p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.07682v2#page=7 "using evaluation metrics that do not give partial credit are at best an incomplete explanation, because emergent abilities are still observed on many classiﬁcation tasks (e.g., the tasks in Figure 2D–H)"))
- **c7** Cross-entropy analysis on six emergent BIG-Bench tasks, which the paper says follows the BIG-Bench (2022) setup and affirms BIG-Bench's conclusions (i.e. the observation originates with BIG-Bench): cross-entropy loss improves even at scales where exact match / BLEU / accuracy stay near random, i.e. log-likelihood improvements can be masked by downstream metrics; the authors add that this does not explain why downstream metrics are emergent or predict the emergence scale.([Potential explanations of emergence (Discussion), p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.07682v2#page=7 "Namely, cross-entropy loss improves even for small model scales where the downstream metrics (exact match, BLEU, and accuracy) are close to random and do not improve, which shows that improvements in the log-likelihood of the target sequence can be masked by such downstream metrics."))
