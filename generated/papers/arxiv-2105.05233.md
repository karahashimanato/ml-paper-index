## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Overview: the authors state that a single hyperparameter, the scale of the classifier gradients, can be tuned to trade off diversity for fidelity, and that this scale can be increased by an order of magnitude without obtaining adversarial examples.([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.05233v4#page=2 "We ﬁnd that a single hyperparameter, the scale of the classiﬁer gradients, can be tuned to trade off diversity for ﬁdelity, and we can increase this gradient scale factor by an order of magnitude without obtaining adversarial examples [61]."))
- **c2** Approximation in the derivation (following Sohl-Dickstein et al.): the classifier-conditioned reverse transition is approximated by a Gaussian whose mean is shifted by Sigma times the classifier gradient, assuming log p(y|x_t) has low curvature compared to Sigma^-1; the authors call this assumption reasonable in the limit of infinite diffusion steps. For deterministic DDIM sampling this derivation does not apply, and a score-based conditioning trick adapted from Song et al. is used instead.([Classifier Guidance (Conditional Reverse Noising Process), p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.05233v4#page=7 "This assumption is reasonable in the limit of inﬁnite diffusion steps, where //Σ// →0."))
- **c3** Extra model and why the scale exceeds 1: the classifier (the UNet downsampling trunk with an attention pool) is trained on the same noising distribution as the diffusion model; with an unconditional ImageNet model and scale 1 the classifier gave about 50% probability to the desired classes but samples did not match the classes on visual inspection, and scaling up the gradients remedied this.([Section 4.3, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.05233v4#page=8 "When using a scale of 1, we observed that the classiﬁer assigned reasonable probabilities (around 50%) to the desired classes for the ﬁnal samples, but these samples did not match the intended classes upon visual inspection."))
- **c4** Interpretation of the scale: s times the classifier gradient equals the gradient of a renormalized distribution proportional to p(y|x)^s, which is sharper than p(y|x) when s > 1; the authors say a larger scale focuses more on the classifier's modes, which is potentially desirable for higher-fidelity but less diverse samples.([Section 4.3, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.05233v4#page=8 "In other words, using a larger gradient scale focuses more on the modes of the classiﬁer, which is potentially desirable for producing higher ﬁdelity (but less diverse) samples."))
- **c5** Diversity-fidelity trade-off: for a class-conditional ImageNet 128x128 model (Figure 4), raising the gradient scale beyond 1.0 smoothly trades recall (diversity) for higher precision and IS (fidelity); FID and sFID, which depend on both, are best at an intermediate scale. The trade-off is shown in figures; no values are recorded here.([Section 4.3, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.05233v4#page=9 "We see that scaling the gradients beyond 1.0 smoothly trades off recall (a measure of diversity) for higher precision and IS (measures of ﬁdelity)."))
- **c6** Compared with BigGAN-deep's truncation trick on ImageNet 128x128 (Figure 5), classifier guidance is reported as strictly better when trading FID for IS, but on the precision/recall trade-off it is only better up to a certain precision threshold, beyond which it cannot achieve better precision.([Section 4.3, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.05233v4#page=9 "Less clear cut is the precision/recall trade-off, which shows that classiﬁer guidance is only a better choice up until a certain precision threshold, after which point it cannot achieve better precision."))
- **c7** Evaluation protocol: FID is the default metric (Precision or IS for fidelity, Recall for diversity); metrics for other methods are recomputed from public samples or models whenever possible, using the entire training set as the reference batch and the same codebase for all models. Table 5 marks some baseline values as taken from previous papers because public models or samples were lacking, and the architecture ablation curves in Figure 2 use FID over 10k instead of 50k samples.([Sample Quality Metrics, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.05233v4#page=4 "To ensure consistent comparisons, we use the entire training set as the reference batch [23, 5], and evaluate metrics for all models using the same codebase."))
- **c8** Guidance-scale selection: the 'optimal' classifier scales were chosen by sweeping a small grid per resolution and sampler (e.g. [0.5, 1, 2] for ImageNet 128 and 256 with the 250-step sampler). The text does not state which metric or which data were used to define 'optimal' or that a held-out set was used (searched for swept, optimal, select).([Appendix I (Hyperparameters), p.27](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.05233v4#page=27 "When choosing optimal classiﬁer scales for our sampler, we swept over [0.5, 1, 2] for ImageNet 128×128 and ImageNet 256×256, and [1, 2, 3, 3.5, 4, 4.5, 5] for ImageNet 512×512."))
- **c9** Compute accounting: training compute is compared in V100-days with StyleGAN2 and BigGAN-deep; BigGAN-deep's TPU-v3 estimates were converted at 2 TPU-v3 days = 1 V100 day and StyleGAN2's from reported throughput. The authors note that classifier training is relatively lightweight compared with training the generative model.([Appendix A.3, p.18](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.05233v4#page=18 "We note that our classiﬁer training is relatively lightweight compared to training the generative model."))
- **c10** Stated limitations: diffusion models remain slower than GANs at sampling time because of multiple denoising steps, and classifier guidance is limited to labeled datasets, with no effective strategy provided for trading off diversity for fidelity on unlabeled data.([Limitations and Future Work, p.12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.05233v4#page=12 "Our proposed classiﬁer guidance technique is currently limited to labeled datasets, and we have provided no effective strategy for trading off diversity for ﬁdelity on unlabeled datasets."))
