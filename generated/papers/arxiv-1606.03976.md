## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Estimand: tau(x) = E[Y1 - Y0 | x] = m1(x) - m0(x), called the Individual Treatment Effect (a footnote says it is sometimes known as CATE); under the Consistency assumption the observed outcome is y = Y0 if t = 0 and y = Y1 if t = 1, so only one potential outcome is seen per unit.([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.03976v5#page=2 "this is known as the Consistency assumption."))
- **c2** Identification: the paper assumes 'no hidden confounders', formalized as strong ignorability ((Y1, Y0) independent of t given x, and 0 < p(t = 1|x) < 1 for all x), which is sufficient for tau(x) to be identifiable (proof in the supplement); the authors state that its validity cannot be checked from data.([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.03976v5#page=2 "The validity of strong ignorability cannot be assessed from data, and must be determined by domain knowledge and understanding of the causal relationships between the variables."))
- **c3** Theorem 1: under the conditions of Lemma 1 (one-to-one, twice-differentiable representation Phi; a constant B_Phi such that B_Phi times the per-unit expected loss lies in the IPM function family G) and squared loss, PEHE is bounded by twice the sum of the factual treated and control losses plus B_Phi * IPM_G(treated, control distributions over Phi), minus a variance term; the proof bounds PEHE by factual plus counterfactual loss and the unestimable counterfactual-minus-factual gap by an IPM.([Section 3.2, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.03976v5#page=5 "We therefore bound the difference ϵCF −ϵF using an IPM."))
- **c4** Gap between theory and algorithm: B_Phi can be made explicit only under further assumptions (for Wasserstein: the loss, Lipschitz constants of p(Y_t|x) and h, and the condition number of the Jacobian of Phi; for MMD: RKHS representability and norms); in the algorithm B_Phi is generally not computed but absorbed into alpha, which makes the objective sensitive to the scaling of Phi, so Phi is normalized by projection or batch normalization with fixed scale.([Section 4, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.03976v5#page=6 "For most IPMs, we cannot compute the factor Bφ in Equation 2, but treat it as part of the hyperparameter α."))
- **c5** Architecture choice: Johansson et al. (2016) fed the concatenation of Phi and t to a single network; the authors argue this risks losing the influence of t when Phi is high-dimensional, so they use two separate heads h1(Phi) and h0(Phi) on a shared representation, and each sample updates only the head of its observed treatment.([Section 4, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.03976v5#page=6 "When the dimension of Φ is high, this risks losing the inﬂuence of t on h during training."))
- **c6** Objective: minimize the weighted factual loss (weights w_i = t_i/(2u) + (1-t_i)/(2(1-u)) compensating for treatment group sizes, u the treated proportion) + lambda * model complexity + alpha * empirical IPM between control and treated representations, by SGD on mini-batches; with alpha > 0 this is CFR and with alpha = 0 it is named TARNet.([Section 4, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.03976v5#page=6 "We refer to the model minimizing (3) with α > 0 as Counterfactual Regression (CFR) and the variant without balance regularization (α = 0) as Treatment-Agnostic Representation Network (TARNet)."))
- **c7** Evaluation: real data rarely have ground-truth effects, so the paper uses semi-synthetic IHDP (Hill, 2011) and the Jobs data (LaLonde, 1986), which has randomized and non-randomized components; both are used for training, but only the randomized component can be used for evaluation (ATT error and a 'policy risk' measure, since PEHE cannot be evaluated on Jobs).([Section 5, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.03976v5#page=6 "This alleviates, but does not solve, the issue of a completely balanced dataset being unsuited for our method."))
- **c8** Hyperparameter selection: because only one potential outcome is observed, the authors use a nearest-neighbour approximation of PEHE (the observed outcome of the nearest unit in the opposite treatment group as a surrogate counterfactual) for hyperparameter selection on IHDP, the validation objective for early stopping, and validation policy risk on Jobs; the same selection procedure is applied to all methods.([Appendix C.1, p.19](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.03976v5#page=19 "Standard methods for hyperparameter selection, such as cross-validation, are not generally applicable for estimating the PEHE loss since only one potential outcome is observed (unless the outcome is simulated)."))
- **c9** Relation to the authors' earlier work (overlapping authors: Johansson, Shalit, Sontag): the paper builds on Johansson et al. (2016), which bounded the relative error of ridge regression under the reversed treatment assignment; the authors say that relative bound is not informative about the absolute quality of the representation, and here give a bound on the absolute quality.([Section 2, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.03976v5#page=3 "Unfortunately, the relative error bound is not at all informative regarding the absolute quality of the representation."))
