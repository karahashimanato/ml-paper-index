## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Motivation: without a community-standard benchmark, models evaluated on traditionally used datasets with inconsistent experimental comparisons make it difficult to differentiate complex, simple and graph-agnostic architectures (citing Hoang and Maehara, 2019; Chen et al., 2019a; Errica et al., 2019).([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.00982v5#page=2 "However, tracking progress is often challenging in the absence of a community-standard benchmark as models that are evaluated on traditionally-used datasets with inconsistent experimental comparisons make it diﬃcult to diﬀerentiate complex, simple and graph-agnostic architectures"))
- **c2** Fixed parameter budgets: the goal is not to find optimal hyperparameters per model but to compare models and building blocks within a parameter budget, 100k for every GNN on all datasets and 500k where scaling to more parameters and deeper layers is studied; layers and hidden dimensions are chosen to match the budgets. The authors argue that without this it is difficult to tell whether a gain comes from the architecture or from extra capacity.([Section 2, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.00982v5#page=3 "In the absence of such design choice, it is comparatively diﬃcult to conclude whether a better performing model’s gain arises from its architectural design or extra learning capacity brought by additional model parameters."))
- **c3** Training protocol shared by all models: Adam with the same learning-rate decay (initial rate from {1e-2, 1e-3, 1e-4}, halved when validation loss stops improving for 5-25 epochs), no maximum number of epochs (training stops when the rate reaches 1e-6 or after 12 hours), and each experiment is run with 4 seeds.([Appendix C, p.17](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.00982v5#page=17 "We run each experiment with 4 diﬀerent seeds and report the statistics of the 4 results."))
- **c4** The authors state the parameter budgeting means the framework may not be ideal for optimizing a model to state-of-the-art by tuning hyperparameters or increasing model size; it is designed to identify performance trends and first principles.([Appendix H, p.40](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.00982v5#page=40 "The parameter budgeting also tells that the proposed framework may not be ideal to optimize a model to achieve SOTA by tuning hyperparameters, increasing model size to as much parameters as a server can ﬁt, etc."))
- **c5** A graph-agnostic MLP baseline (each node updated independently) presents consistently low scores across all datasets, which the authors take as showing that graph structure is needed for these tasks and that the datasets can statistically separate GNN performance.([Appendix D, p.29](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.00982v5#page=29 "MLP presents consistently low scores across all datasets (Tables 3-10), which shows the necessity to use graph structure for these tasks."))
- **c6** Although provably more powerful in terms of isomorphism tests, 3WL-GNNs and RingGNNs did not outperform message-passing GCNs on these medium-scale datasets; they have O(n^2)/O(n^3) space/time complexity, face loss divergence and/or out-of-memory errors when made deeper, and show relatively high standard deviation across seeds, which the authors attribute to the lack of batching and batch normalization for dense tensors.([Appendix D, p.29](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.00982v5#page=29 "These new models are limited in terms of space/time complexities, with O(n2)/O(n3) respectively, not allowing them to scale to larger datasets."))
- **c7** Among GCN-class models, anisotropic models (GCN with symmetric normalization, GAT, MoNet, GatedGCN) are consistently better than isotropic ones (vanilla GCN, GraphSage) at the 100K budget on several datasets, with stated exceptions; the authors suggest that understanding the expressive power of attention-based aggregation is a meaningful research avenue.([Appendix D, p.29](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.00982v5#page=29 "Overall, our results suggest that understanding the expressive power of attention-based neighborhood aggregation functions is a meaningful avenue of research."))
- **c8** Laplacian positional encoding: the k smallest non-trivial eigenvectors of the normalized Laplacian I - D^{-1/2} A D^{-1/2} are added to node features (x_i = x_i + p_i); since eigenvectors are defined only up to sign, their sign is randomly flipped during training. The motivation is that MP-GCNs are limited by their equivalence to the 1-WL test when nodes are anonymous.([Appendix E.1.2, p.31](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.00982v5#page=31 "The eigenvectors are deﬁned up to the factor ±1 (after being normalized to unit length), so the sign of eigenvectors will be randomly ﬂipped during training."))
- **c9** On three small TU datasets (ENZYMES, DD, PROTEINS) with 10-fold stratified splits, all networks have statistically similar test performance because the standard deviation is large; a second run with a different seed changes the model ranking, and for DD and PROTEINS the graph-agnostic MLP performs as well as GNNs.([Appendix F, p.38](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.00982v5#page=38 "We observe all NNs have similar statistical test performance as the standard deviation is quite large."))
