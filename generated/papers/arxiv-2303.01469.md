## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Contribution: consistency models map noise directly to data, support one-step generation by design, and allow multistep sampling that trades compute for sample quality; they can be trained by distilling a pre-trained diffusion model or as standalone generative models.([Abstract, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.01469v2#page=1 "They support fast one-step generation by design, while still al-lowing multistep sampling to trade compute for sample quality."))
- **c2** Requirements: consistency distillation uses a numerical ODE solver and a pre-trained diffusion model to generate adjacent points on a PF ODE trajectory, whereas consistency training needs no pre-trained diffusion model; neither uses adversarial training. CD models are initialized from the pre-trained diffusion models; CT models are randomly initialized.([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.01469v2#page=2 "By contrast, our second method eliminates the need for a pre-trained diffusion model altogether, allowing us to train a consistency model in isolation."))
- **c3** Positioning: citing Luhman & Luhman (2021) and Zheng et al. (2022), most distillation methods collect a large dataset of diffusion-model samples beforehand, which is computationally expensive; the authors state progressive distillation is the only one they know without this drawback, and that ODE solvers still need more than 10 steps for competitive samples.([Diffusion Models (Section 2), p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.01469v2#page=3 "However, ODE solvers still need more than 10 evaluation steps to generate competitive samples."))
- **c4** Multistep sampling protocol: the intermediate time points of Algorithm 1 are found greedily, one at a time, by ternary search optimizing the FID of the samples, assuming FID is unimodal in the next time point (which they found to hold empirically).([Section 3 (Sampling), p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.01469v2#page=4 "where the time points are pinpointed one at a time using ternary search to optimize the FID of samples obtained from Algorithm 1."))
- **c5** Theory (Theorem 1): assuming the consistency model is Lipschitz and the ODE solver has bounded local error of order p+1, zero CD loss implies the estimation error is O((Delta t)^p); the authors say that under some regularity conditions the model can become arbitrarily accurate if the ODE step size is small enough. Theorem 2 relates CT to CD assuming the Euler solver and a score model equal to the ground truth.([Section 4, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.01469v2#page=5 "the estimated consistency model can become arbitrarily accurate, as long as the step size of the ODE solver is sufficiently small."))
- **c6** CT trade-off: convergence of consistency training is highly sensitive to the number of discretization steps N (smaller N converges faster but gives worse samples), which motivates progressively increasing N and the EMA rate during training; these schedules were tuned separately per image resolution.([Section 6.1, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.01469v2#page=7 "As demonstrated in Fig. 3d, the con-vergence of CT is highly sensitive to N—smaller N leads to faster convergence but worse samples, whereas larger N leads to slower convergence but better samples upon convergence."))
- **c7** Continuous-time CD and CT losses avoid choosing N but require Jacobian-vector products and forward-mode automatic differentiation, which may not be well supported in some deep learning frameworks.([Section 4, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.01469v2#page=5 "Nonetheless, they involve Jacobian-vector products and require forward-mode automatic dif-ferentiation for efficient implementation, which may not be well-supported in some deep learning frameworks."))
- **c8** Comparison protocol with progressive distillation: both PD and CD distill the same EDM models pre-trained in-house; LPIPS improved PD over the squared L2 distance used originally, but the tables report PD with L2 (its default). CD outperforms PD across datasets, steps and metrics considered, except single-step generation on LSUN Bedroom 256x256 where CD with L2 slightly underperforms PD with L2. CD settings (LPIPS, Heun, N = 18) were chosen on CIFAR-10 and N was tuned separately on other datasets.([Section 6.2, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.01469v2#page=7 "All methods distill from an EDM (Karras et al., 2022) model that we pre-trained in-house."))
- **c9** As standalone models, the authors conclude that consistency models generate better samples than existing single-step generation models except for GANs (CT reaching quality comparable to one-step PD without distillation).([Section 7 (Conclusion), p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.01469v2#page=9 "Furthermore, as a standalone generative model, consistency models generate better samples than existing single-step generation models except for GANs."))
