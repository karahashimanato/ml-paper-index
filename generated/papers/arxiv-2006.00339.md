## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Terminology: the paper delineates unsupervised AD (trained on mostly nominal data), unsupervised OE (unsupervised methods adapted to incorporate auxiliary non-nominal data; elsewhere called semi-supervised AD) and supervised OE (a standard classifier discerning nominal data from an auxiliary non-nominal dataset).([Section 1 (Introduction), p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.00339v3#page=1 "Unsupervised OE: Adaptations of unsupervised methods that incorporate auxiliary data that is not nominal."))
- **c2** Main finding: using the same experimental OE setup as Hendrycks et al. (2019b), a standard classifier is able to outperform the authors' listed state-of-the-art AD methods on the one-vs-rest benchmarks on MNIST and CIFAR-10.([Section 1 (Introduction), p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.00339v3#page=2 "We ﬁnd that, using the same experimental OE setup as Hendrycks et al. (2019b), a standard classiﬁer is able to outperform current state-of-the-art AD methods on the one vs. rest AD benchmarks on MNIST and CIFAR-10."))
- **c3** Few OE samples suffice: with 128 OE samples a classifier is competitive with state-of-the-art unsupervised methods on CIFAR-10 one-vs-rest, and with only 64 OE samples a classifier outperforms unsupervised methods (with or without OE) on the ImageNet one-vs-rest benchmark of Hendrycks et al. (2019b). The authors say this seems to contradict the common wisdom that anomalies are unconcentrated and hard to characterize with data.([Section 1 (Introduction), p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.00339v3#page=2 "With only 64 OE samples a classiﬁer outperforms unsupervised methods (with or without OE) on the ImageNet one vs. rest benchmark (Hendrycks et al., 2019b)."))
- **c4** Benchmark critique: the one-vs-rest benchmark (one class nominal, the remaining classes anomalous at test time) on these datasets is, according to the authors, used as a litmus test in virtually all deep AD papers published at top-tier venues.([Section 1 (Introduction), p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.00339v3#page=2 "This benchmark applied to the aforementioned datasets is used as a litmus test in virtually all deep AD papers published at top-tier venues"))
- **c5** OE data assumption: the nominal and OE training data are exactly those used by Hendrycks et al. (2019b) and contain no representatives of the ground-truth anomaly classes (EMNIST-Letters for MNIST, 80 Million Tiny Images without CIFAR-10/100 for CIFAR-10, ImageNet-22K without ImageNet-1K for 30 ImageNet-1K classes); the OE data is not tailored to the test anomalies. Drawing OE samples from just one class (not present as anomaly at test time) already gave good performance on CIFAR-10 (Appendix B).([Section 1 (Introduction), p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.00339v3#page=2 "The OE data is not tailored to be representative of the anomalies used at test time."))
- **c6** Deep SAD (Ruff et al., 2020, by overlapping authors) is classed as an unsupervised OE approach; the authors' HSC modification is reported to significantly improve on Deep SAD and is used as the representative unsupervised OE method.([Section 2 (Deep One-Class Classification), p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.00339v3#page=2 "We found this modiﬁcation to signiﬁcantly improve upon the performance of Deep SAD and use it in our experiments as a representative of the unsupervised OE approach to AD."))
- **c7** Multiscale hypothesis: increasingly blurring OE images (removing small-scale features) quickly drops performance on ImageNet and CIFAR-10, and with sufficient blurring HSC performs better than BCE; on MNIST HSC outperforms BCE at any degree of blurring. The authors take this to suggest two regimes: on high-dimensional multiscale images OE samples are highly informative and standard and hypersphere classification perform similarly well, while on low-dimensional images like MNIST hypersphere classification performs better, reflecting the classic intuition that AD requires a compact model.([Section 3.3, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.00339v3#page=4 "This suggests that their are two regimes in deep image AD"))
- **c8** Protocol: all of the authors' implementations (HSC, Deep SAD, Focal, BCE) use the same network per setting (LeNet-style for MNIST/CIFAR-10, the WideResNet of Hendrycks et al. (2019b) for ImageNet), Adam with fixed epoch counts and learning-rate milestones, batches balanced to 128 nominal and 128 OE samples, and data augmentation; Focal gamma = 2 as recommended by Lin et al. (2017). Evaluation is mean AUC over classes and seeds; no threshold is chosen. No description of how these hyperparameters were selected or of a validation set was found in the text (searched for valid, tun, select, hyperparam, grid, search).([Appendix F, p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.00339v3#page=10 "We always use the same underlying network φθ in each experimental setting for our HSC, Deep SAD, Focal, and BCE implementations to control architectural effects."))
- **c9** Scope caveat: the authors do not claim that supervised OE is the solution to AD in general or that unsupervised OE has no utility, but suggest it may be time to move to more challenging benchmarks such as MVTec-AD.([Section 4 (Conclusion), p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.00339v3#page=4 "that it may be time for the community to move to more challenging benchmarks (e.g., MVTec-AD (Bergmann et al., 2019)) to gauge the signiﬁcance of deep AD works."))
