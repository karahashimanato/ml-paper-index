## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** MC acquisition functions: analytic forms are not available for arbitrary objectives or utilities, non-Gaussian posteriors, or batches of points evaluated in parallel or asynchronously, so the expectation defining the acquisition function is approximated by averaging over posterior samples; randomized quasi-MC can significantly reduce the variance of the estimate and its gradient.([Section 3 (Monte-Carlo Acquisition Functions), p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.06403v3#page=3 "Instead, MC integration can be used to approximate the expectation (1) using samples from the posterior."))
- **c2** Sample average approximation (SAA): instead of redrawing samples at each evaluation and using stochastic first-order methods, BoTorch draws base samples once and holds them fixed, making the MC estimate deterministic so that deterministic quasi-Newton optimizers (by default multi-start L-BFGS-B) can be used.([Section 4 (MC Bayesian Optimization via Sample Average Approximation), p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.06403v3#page=3 "and hold it ﬁxed between evaluations throughout the course of optimization"))
- **c3** Theory (Theorem 1): assuming a compact feasible set, a GP prior with continuously differentiable mean and covariance, Lipschitz objective and utility, and i.i.d. normal base samples, the SAA optimum converges almost surely, and under additional regularity conditions at an exponential rate in probability. The authors do not characterize theoretical rate improvements of RQMC over MC, but observe empirically that RQMC works remarkably well.([Section 4 (MC Bayesian Optimization via Sample Average Approximation), p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.06403v3#page=4 "While at this point we do not characterize improvements in theoretical convergence rates of RQMC over MC for SAA, we observe empirically that RQMC methods work remarkably well in practice"))
- **c4** The authors find that in practice the bias from SAA has only a minor effect relative to the analytic ground truth, often improves on stochastic approaches, and avoids tuning optimization hyperparameters such as learning rates.([Section 4 (MC Bayesian Optimization via Sample Average Approximation), p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.06403v3#page=4 "We ﬁnd that in practice the bias from using SAA only has a minor effect on the performance relative to using the analytic ground truth"))
- **c5** One-Shot KG: the nested knowledge-gradient problem is solved jointly over candidates and N fantasy points (dimension (q + N)d instead of qd) as a single deterministic problem; its main limitation is the linear growth of the problem dimension in N, though the authors observe good performance for moderate N.([Section 4.2 (One-Shot Formulation of the Knowledge Gradient using SAA), p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.06403v3#page=5 "The main limitation of OKG is the linear growth of the dimension of the optimization problem in N"))
- **c6** Library comparison protocol: BoTorch is compared with GPyOpt, Cornell MOE and Dragonfly, chosen as the most popular libraries with ongoing support (GPFlowOpt could not be installed), with all packages using their default models and settings; the authors report that BoTorch's algorithms tend to achieve greater sample efficiency.([Section 6 (Experiments), p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.06403v3#page=9 "First, we ﬁnd that BOTORCH’s algorithms tend to achieve greater sample efﬁciency compared to those of other packages (all packages use their default models and settings)."))
- **c7** Synthetic-benchmark protocol: noisy Branin, Rosenbrock, Ackley and Hartmann with q = 4, means and 95% confidence intervals over 100 trials, all algorithms starting from the same 2d + 2 QMC initial points; performance is the true noiseless value at the suggested point, which is out-of-sample for OKG, MOE KG and NEI and in-sample for the others.([Appendix C.1, p.17](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.06403v3#page=17 "We evaluate based on the true noiseless function value at the “suggested point” (i.e., the point to be chosen if BO were to end at this batch)."))
- **c8** HPO protocol: for hyperparameter tuning the configuration with the best observed performance is reported rather than a suggested point; e.g. DQN on Cartpole (5 hyperparameters, each evaluation an average of 10 training runs, 15 rounds of q = 4, 100 trials) and an HPOlib2 neural-network surrogate for UCI Adult (200 trials of 75 iterations, q = 1).([Appendix C.4, p.18](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.06403v3#page=18 "As HPO typically involves long and resource intensive training jobs, it is standard to select the conﬁguration with the best observed performance, rather than to evaluate a “suggested” conﬁguration (we cannot perform noiseless function evaluations)."))
