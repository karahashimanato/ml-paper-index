## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Survey's description of Breiman's algorithm: three parameters a_n (points sampled per tree), mtry (directions tried per node) and nodesize; in the regression mode of the R package randomForest the defaults are mtry = ceil(p/3), a_n = n (bootstrap) and nodesize = 5.([The random forest estimate (Algorithm), p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.05741v1#page=6 "an is set to n, and nodesize is set to 5."))
- **c2** Theoretical studies have so far considered simplified versions because bagging and the CART split are hard to analyse: typically the bagging step is ignored and/or CART-split selection is replaced by a more elementary cut protocol; in Breiman's forests, leaves typically contain between 1 and 5 observations.([Introduction, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.05741v1#page=3 "This is often done by simply ignoring the bagging step and/or replacing the CART-split selection by a more elementary cut protocol."))
- **c3** Centered (purely random) forests: no resampling, uniformly chosen coordinate, split at the cell centre, k levels; they are consistent if k -> infinity and n/2^k -> infinity, but the unpruned choice k ~ log n violates this condition, there is no bagging, and forest consistency follows from tree consistency, so the authors judge the model too simple to explain Breiman's forests.([Simplified models, p.13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.05741v1#page=13 "Overall, this model does not demonstrate the beneﬁt of using forests in place of individual trees and is too simple to explain the mathematical forces driving Breiman’s forests."))
- **c4** The sparsity-adaptive rate for centered forests (Breiman 2004; Biau 2012), depending only on the number of strong variables |S|, is obtained by assuming that the splitting probabilities on informative variables tend to 1/|S|, i.e. that the procedure selects the informative variables, which the authors call a strong assumption.([Simplified models, p.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.05741v1#page=14 "Of course, this is achieved by assuming that the procedure succeeds in selecting the informative variables for splitting, which is indeed a strong assumption."))
- **c5** The role of the bootstrap is still poorly understood: most analyses (citing Mentch and Hooker 2015; Wager 2014; Scornet et al. 2015) replace it by subsampling without replacement with a_n < n, usually with a_n/n -> 0, which excludes the bootstrap regime.([The resampling mechanism, p.19](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.05741v1#page=19 "The role of the bootstrap in random forests is still poorly understood and, to date, most analyses are doomed to replace the bootstrap by a subsampling scheme"))
- **c6** Little has been proven for Breiman's original procedure; citing Breiman (2001), the forest error is bounded in terms of the correlation between tree errors and the strength of individual trees; consistency and asymptotic normality results (Wager 2014; Mentch and Hooker 2015) replace bootstrap by subsampling and simplify the splitting step.([Consistency, asymptotic normality, and more, p.24](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.05741v1#page=24 "All in all, little has been proven mathematically for the original procedure of Breiman."))
- **c7** Citing Scornet et al. (2015): consistency in additive regression models was proved for a pruned version of Breiman's forest, while consistency of the unpruned procedure depends on a conjecture about CART that is difficult to verify.([Consistency, asymptotic normality, and more, p.25](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.05741v1#page=25 "Unfortunately, the consistency of the unpruned procedure comes at the price of a conjecture regarding the behavior of the CART algorithm that is diﬃcult to verify."))
- **c8** Open problems: the default bootstrap mode (a_n = n with replacement) seems to give excellent results but has no supporting theory; the impact of tree depth on statistical performance is also stated to be an open question.([Conclusion and perspectives, p.32](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.05741v1#page=32 "By default, the algorithm runs in bootstrap mode (i.e., an = n points selected with replacement) and although this seems to give excellent results, there is to date no theory to support this choice."))
- **c9** Variable importance caveats (citing empirical studies): correlated variables negatively affect MDA (permutation importance), and Archer and Kimes (2008) found both MDA and MDI behave poorly as correlation increases; Strobl et al. (2008) attribute MDA's bias to evaluating marginal rather than conditional importance.([Variable importance (Related works), p.29](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.05741v1#page=29 "Similarly, the empirical study of Archer and Kimes (2008) points out that both MDA and MDI behave poorly when correlation increases"))
- **c10** Split-selection bias (citing Breiman et al. 1984; Segal 1988; Kim and Loh 2001): splits in random forests are known to be biased toward covariates with many possible splits or with missing values; Hothorn et al. (2006) correct this by first selecting the variable and then the cut position.([Decision splits, p.23](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.05741v1#page=23 "Splits in random forests are known to be biased toward covariates with many possible splits (Breiman et al., 1984; Segal, 1988) or with missing values (Kim and Loh, 2001)."))
- **c11** The authors conclude that present results are insufficient to explain random forests' behaviour in full generality; their intuition (stated as such) is that tree aggregation can estimate patterns not characterized by standard sparsity or smoothness conditions.([Conclusion and perspectives, p.32](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1511.05741v1#page=32 "It remains however that the present results are insuﬃcient to explain in full generality the remarkable behavior of random forests."))
