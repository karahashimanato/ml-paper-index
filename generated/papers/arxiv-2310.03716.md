## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Setup: three 'helpfulness' preference datasets - WebGPT (question answering, human labels, 19.6K examples), Stack (StackExchange, labels derived from upvotes, 100K pairs) and RLCD (multi-turn dialogue, synthetic preferences, 40K helpfulness pairs); reward models use the Bradley-Terry likelihood; PPO maximizes R(x, y) - lambda KL(pi_RL || pi_SFT) with lambda = 0.04, implemented with Hugging Face TRL on Llama-7B with LoRA (rank 16), starting from public SFT models.([Section 2.1, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.03716v2#page=3 "We use Llama-7B models (Touvron et al., 2023a) as our base for all experiments"))
- **c2** Evaluation: besides intrinsic reward, downstream quality is measured with AlpacaFarm simulated preferences, which query 12 OpenAI API-based 'annotators' and report pairwise win rates on 500 held-out datapoints per task; the authors qualify this metric by noting it may itself have length biases, which is why they focus more on other metrics.([Section 2.1, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.03716v2#page=3 "While useful for additional context, we qualify this metric by noting that it itself may have length biases,"))
- **c3** Length-stratified analysis: across settings PPO significantly increases output length, and although all settings show overall reward gains, non-length reward gains (NRG) are substantially lower; for WebGPT and RLCD 70%-90% of the improvement can be explained by length shifts, and on WebGPT NRG contributes only 2% of the overall gain with standard PPO. Stack shows a higher NRG share, which the authors say is likely because Stack SFT outputs are already near the length limit (and it may rely more on non-length features).([Section 3, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.03716v2#page=5 "Particularly, NRG is almost negligible for WebGPT and contributes only 2% to overall reward gain in the standard PPO setting."))
- **c4** Length-only reward: replacing the learned reward with a purely length-based heuristic score in the PPO pipeline gives simulated-preference performance close to standard PPO (stated in the text as 56% vs 58% win-rate of standard PPO on WebGPT and 64% vs 63% on RLCD). The authors read this as showing that current reward models fail to convincingly outperform simple heuristics and as questioning reported 'progress' with popular metrics.([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.03716v2#page=2 "We find that PPO performance with this length-only reward is close to standard PPO (56% vs 58% win-rate of standard PPO on WebGPT and 64% vs 63% win-rate of standard PPO on RLCD)."))
- **c5** Length-only PPO also beats a longest-of-8 SFT sampling baseline and length-only PPO without KL penalty; the authors hypothesize that the KL term is an important constraint that lets length-only PPO learn good features (pathological outputs would likely have higher KL), and argue that win-rate evaluation alone is not sufficient to tell whether RLHF behaves as expected.([Section 3 (Can a length-only reward improve performance?), p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.03716v2#page=5 "Our hypothesis is that the KL term is an important constraint on the optimization for allowing length-only PPO to learn good features."))
- **c6** PPO interventions (KL coefficient raised from 0.04 to 0.12, omitting long outputs, an explicit length penalty, reward scaling): length often decreases substantially compared with standard PPO, but length still always increases relative to SFT and reward model score is always worse than standard PPO; omitting long outputs and penalizing length often cause convergence failure.([Section 4.1, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.03716v2#page=7 "However, length still always increases relative to SFT, and reward model score is always worse than standard PPO."))
- **c7** Reward-model interventions: all three preference datasets are slightly imbalanced towards longer outputs; interventions such as length balancing reduce the length-reward correlation, but all except random-pairing data augmentation hurt evaluation accuracy. On RLCD strong correlations remain despite balancing, while on Stack balancing lowers correlation with above-random accuracy and leads to shorter-than-SFT outputs with higher downstream preference.([Section 4.2, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.03716v2#page=8 "Interestingly, on RLCD strong correlations remain despite balancing, suggesting the bias may be more challenging to eliminate."))
- **c8** Training dynamics: most training examples get near-zero reward-model confidence across epochs, and the few strong predictions (including confidently wrong ones) follow the length heuristic; the authors conclude reward models likely struggle to learn deeper features from preferences and, even with balancing, are vulnerable to being dominated by easy features like length.([Section 5, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.03716v2#page=9 "Thus reward models likely struggle to learn deeper features from preferences, and even with balancing are vulnerable to being dominated by “easiy” features like length."))
