## 主張

- **c1** Motivation: meta-learners decompose treatment effect estimation into sub-problems solvable with standard supervised learning, but choosing between them in a data-driven way is difficult because it requires counterfactual information; the authors therefore analyze theoretically when some learners can be expected to perform better a priori.([Abstract, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.10943v2#page=1 "Choosing between diﬀerent meta-learners in a data-driven manner is diﬃcult, as it requires access to counterfactual information."))
- **c2** Estimand and identification: the target is the CATE tau(x) = E[Y(1) - Y(0) | X = x] for a binary treatment, estimated under Assumption 1 (consistency, unconfoundedness with no unobserved confounders, and overlap 0 < pi(x) < 1); no parametric form is assumed for tau or the nuisance functions, giving a nonparametric problem. The authors note that identification hinges on untestable assumptions.([Section 2, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.10943v2#page=3 "It is well known that the identiﬁcation of causal eﬀects from observational data hinges on the imposition of untestable assumptions."))
- **c3** Taxonomy: two-step learners regress a pseudo-outcome that is unbiased for CATE when the nuisances are known: the RA-learner (of which Künzel et al.'s X-learner is described as a variant that needs a weighting function g(x)), the PW-learner (Horvitz-Thompson transformation) and the DR-learner (Kennedy, 2020; AIPW pseudo-outcome). The R-learner of Nie and Wager (2020) fits none of these categories, requires specific model-fitting procedures (e.g. manipulating a loss function), and is not considered further.([Section 3, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.10943v2#page=4 "The R-learner proposed in Nie and Wager (2020) does not fall in any of these categories."))
- **c4** Asymptotic comparison (Theorem 1, assuming bounded propensity estimates, smoothness of mu_w, pi and tau, and the two stages fit on separate samples): the RA-learner remainder depends on the outcome-regression errors, the PW-learner remainder on the propensity error, and the DR-learner remainder on their product; by asymptotic properties the DR-learner is preferred among two-step learners, and the PW-learner over the RA-learner if the propensity score is easier to estimate than the outcome regressions. The authors add that it is commonly assumed that tau(x) is simpler than mu_0(x), so RA-learner reaching the oracle rate is unlikely and RA- and plug-in learners are expected to perform similarly asymptotically.([Section 4, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.10943v2#page=5 "By asymptotic properties, we thus prefer the DR-learner over the other two-step learners"))
- **c5** Finite samples cannot rely on convergence rates only: inverse-propensity-weighted estimators have high variance, particularly with extreme propensity scores, and the PW-learner pseudo-outcome has very high variance even with constant, known propensities; for small delta the constants in the PW- and DR-learner error bounds can be large, possibly leading to relatively better performance of the RA-learner in smaller samples.([Section 4, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.10943v2#page=5 "the pseudo-outcome associated with the PW-learner has a very high variance even when propensity scores are constant and known"))
- **c6** Selection bias (pi(x) != 0.5 for some x) does not matter asymptotically but can be severe in finite samples if the propensity bound omega is small, because fitting each outcome surface on factual data gives too much weight to samples with large pi(x) (a similar conclusion is attributed to Alaa and van der Schaar, 2018b); the degree of overlap is therefore of paramount importance, and second-stage regressions on all data can correct for the sub-optimal first-stage weighting.([Section 4, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.10943v2#page=5 "It also shows that the degree of overlap, which determines ω, is of paramount importance."))
- **c7** Neural architectures: besides TNet (separate networks), SNet-1 is TARNet (Shalit et al., 2017), SNet-2 uses only the architecture of DragonNet (Shi et al., 2019; not its targeted ATE estimation), SNet-3 is DR-CFR (Hassanpour and Greiner, 2020) without propensity re-weighting in the loss, and the proposed SNet learns five representations, allowing features that affect only one potential outcome; the authors expect SNet to perform best on average without knowledge of the problem structure but say it may be more difficult to fit.([Section 5, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.10943v2#page=7 "We expect that such a general architecture should perform best on average in absence of knowledge on the underlying problem structure, but may be more diﬃcult to ﬁt than simpler models."))
- **c8** Gap between theory and experiments: the theoretical analyses rely on sample splitting for two-step learners, but the authors observed that using all data for both steps can work better in practice (particularly in small samples) and use no sample splitting in the experiments; they suggest cross-fitting when guarantees are desired and data are scarce, and note that hyper-parameters can be tuned using factual hold-out data only.([Section 5, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.10943v2#page=7 "While we implement all strategies in our code base, we do not use any form of sample splitting in our experiments."))
- **c9** Conclusion: while the DR-learner is asymptotically optimal in theory, the RA-learner and plug-in learners that share information between nuisance tasks can outperform it in finite samples; relative performance depends on the DGP and sample size, so the choice of learner should incorporate an expert's assessment of the most likely DGP. Only NNs were used as base learners; comparing ML methods (e.g. NNs versus random forests) is left as a next step.([Section 7, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.10943v2#page=9 "We demonstrated that while the DR-learner is asymptotically optimal in theory, both the RA-learner and plug-in learners sharing information between nuisance estimation tasks can outperform it in ﬁnite samples."))
- **c10** Pseudo-outcomes of the two-step learners (Eq. 2-3): the RA-learner regresses W(Y - mu0_hat(X)) + (1 - W)(mu1_hat(X) - Y) on X; the PW-learner uses the Horvitz-Thompson transformation (W / pi_hat(X) - (1 - W)/(1 - pi_hat(X))) Y. The X-learner is described as a variant of the RA-learner that fits two separate regressions combined with a weight g(x); the authors prefer RA-learning because it needs no choice of g.([Section 3, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.10943v2#page=4 "We prefer the RA-learning strategy here, as it does not require choice of ‘hyper-parameter’ g(x)."))
