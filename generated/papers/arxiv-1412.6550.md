## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** KD objective as used here (reviewing Hinton & Dean, 2014): L_KD(W_S) = H(y_true, P_S) + lambda H(P_T^tau, P_S^tau), where P^tau = softmax(a/tau) with a relaxation tau > 1 applied to both teacher and student pre-softmax outputs, H is the cross-entropy and lambda balances the two terms.([Section 2.1, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.6550v4#page=3 "where H refers to the cross-entropy and λ is a tunable parameter to balance both cross-entropies."))
- **c2** Hint definition: a hint is the output of a teacher hidden layer used to guide a chosen student (guided) layer, which should predict it. Hints act as a regularizer, so the pair must be chosen to avoid over-regularization: the deeper the guided layer, the less flexibility and the more likely over-regularization. The authors state they choose the middle layers of teacher and student.([Section 2.2, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.6550v4#page=3 "The deeper we set the guided layer, the less ﬂexibility we give to the network and, therefore, FitNets are more likely to suffer from over-regularization."))
- **c3** Hint loss: L_HT(W_Guided, W_r) = 1/2 ||u_h(x; W_Hint) - r(v_g(x; W_Guided); W_r)||^2, where a regressor r on top of the guided layer maps the (usually narrower) student layer to the size of the teacher hint, and u_h and r must have the same non-linearity. Because a fully connected regressor between convolutional layers increases parameters and memory dramatically, a convolutional regressor is used instead.([Section 2.2, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.6550v4#page=3 "To mitigate this limitation, we use a convolutional regressor instead."))
- **c4** Training is stage-wise: first the student up to the guided layer (and the regressor) is trained with the hint loss from a random initialization, then the whole student is trained with the KD loss while lambda is annealed linearly, so that examples on which the teacher is confident first have a stronger effect and their importance then decreases; the authors present this as a generic form of curriculum learning where prior information is deduced from the teacher.([Section 2.4, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.6550v4#page=5 "In order to promote the learning of more complex examples (examples with lower teacher conﬁdence), we gradually anneal λ during the training with a linear decay."))
- **c5** Hyper-parameters (CIFAR-10/100): RMSProp with initial learning rate 0.005, mini-batch 128, parameters initialized in U(-0.005, 0.005); lambda initialized to 4 and decayed linearly over 500 epochs to 1; tau = 3. On CIFAR-10 each stage stopped after 100 epochs without validation improvement (maximum 500 epochs), and the FitNet was then retrained on the full 50K training set; on CIFAR-100 the number of epochs was taken from the CIFAR-10 stopping criterion.([Appendix A.1.2, p.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.6550v4#page=11 "Parameter λ in Eq. (2) was initialized to 4 and decayed linearly during 500 epochs reaching λ = 1."))
- **c6** CIFAR-10: a FitNet with 17 maxout convolutional layers and roughly 1/3 of the parameters of the maxout teacher (student layer 11 guided by teacher layer 2) outperforms the teacher, which the authors say suggests depth is crucial; the text states 91.61% accuracy versus 85.8% for the top-performing compression method of Ba & Caruana (2014), with roughly 28 times fewer parameters.([Section 3.1, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.6550v4#page=5 "Our student model outperforms the teacher model, while requiring notably fewer parameters, suggesting that depth is crucial to achieve better representations."))
- **c7** Hinting with classification targets instead of the teacher: stage-wise training with label-supervised first halves (with or without KD in stage 2) and joint optimization with supervised hints failed to learn; a 19-layer network trained as Deeply-Supervised Networks reached 88.2% versus 91.61% with hint-based training, which the authors say suggests a very discriminative intermediate hint might be too aggressive and a smoother teacher hint offers better generalization.([Section 3.1, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.6550v4#page=6 "Such result suggests that using a very discriminative hint w.r.t. classiﬁcation at intermediate layers might be too aggressive; using a smoother hint (such as the guidance from a teacher network) offers better generalization."))
- **c8** Fixed-budget comparison on CIFAR-10 (about 30M and 107M multiplications; networks of 3 to 11 layers; test accuracy with early stopping on the validation set, no retraining): with 30M multiplications standard backprop could not train networks with more than 5 layers, KD trained up to 7 layers and reached its limits there, and hint-based training trained 13-layer networks. The authors attribute the difference to the starting point only (random vs. hint-pretrained) and state the results suggest HT is a stronger regularizer than KD.([Section 4.1, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.6550v4#page=8 "HT tends to ease these optimization issues and is able to train 13-layer networks of 30M multiplications."))
