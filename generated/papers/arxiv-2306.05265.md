## 主張

- **c1** Main result: the MDL criterion corresponds to the marginal likelihood of a Bayesian model with a specific class of prior distributions, so that the marginal likelihood inherits the MDL criterion's asymptotic properties and can be used to detect CPs, assign posterior probabilities to competing CP methods, and select or combine them.([Introduction, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.05265v1#page=3 "Our paper addresses this gap by showing that the MDL criterion corresponds to the marginal likelihood of a Bayesian model with a specific class of prior distributions."))
- **c2** Background as framed by the authors: frequentist and Bayesian CP approaches have mostly evolved independently; frequentists mostly use SIC variants, sums of squared residuals or MDL, while Bayesians typically maximize the marginal likelihood; consistency of the estimated number and locations of breaks is rarely investigated in the Bayesian framework (citing Du et al., 2016 as an exception for a piecewise-constant model).([Introduction, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.05265v1#page=3 "most criteria used by frequentists consistently estimate the number and locations of the breaks, this is rarely investigated in the Bayesian framework."))
- **c3** Model assumptions: a piecewise-linear regression with m breaks, i.i.d. standard normal errors scaled by a segment variance, independent regimes in the marginal likelihood product, nonsingular X_i'X_i, and the number of breaks bounded by M. In a footnote the authors call the normality assumption restrictive while noting that several recent Bayesian and frequentist CP frameworks rely on it.([Section 2, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.05265v1#page=6 "several recent theoretical and empirical CP frameworks rely on the normality assumption"))
- **c4** Proposition 2: with NIG hyperparameters set per segment to the segment's OLS estimate as prior mean, M_i = X_i'X_i (a g-prior), and shape/scale scaled by sqrt(n_i), the log marginal likelihood equals MDL(m, tau) plus a remainder of order O(min_i(n_i)^-7); the hyperparameters therefore depend on the data of each segment.([Section 2.3, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.05265v1#page=9 "Proposition 2 formally establishes the link between a class of Bayesian CP models and the MDL criterion."))
- **c5** Consistency transfer: Davis et al. (2013) show MDL consistently estimates the number and locations of CPs, AR order and segment parameters for a broad class of piecewise-linear stationary AR processes; the authors state these properties also apply to the MDL marginal likelihood under Davis et al.'s regularity conditions and the Proposition 2 presets, extending Du et al. (2016) from piecewise-constant to piecewise-linear regression.([Section 2.3, p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.05265v1#page=10 "These asymptotic properties also apply to the MDL marginal likelihood under the regularity conditions stated in Davis et al. (2013) and under the presets given in Proposition 2."))
- **c6** Decision rule: replacing the CUSUM statistic in binary and wild binary segmentation by the log ratio of MDL marginal likelihoods (break vs no break on an interval) gives the threshold a probabilistic meaning; a threshold of 0 means the break model has higher posterior probability, and a threshold of 3 is used (posterior probability in favour of a break greater than 95%).([Section 3, p.12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.05265v1#page=12 "When δT > 3, the probability in favor of a break is greater than 95%."))
- **c7** Simulation protocol caveat: six AR DGPs from Yau and Zhao (2016), T = 1,024, 1,000 replications, scored by the proportion of correct numbers of breaks and an 'exact frequency' (correct number and every location within 50); BS and WBS use package defaults. All local methods except BS and WBS are given the true autoregressive lag orders (the MDL-based criteria need segment OLS estimates), whereas BS and WBS do not require a particular DGP.([Section 4, p.17](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.05265v1#page=17 "Note, however, that all local methods except BS and WBS detect the CPs assuming the true lag orders of the autoregressive processes."))
- **c8** Simulation result as stated by the authors: WBSMDL outperforms all methods in Table 2 (including the global GMDL) for both metrics in all DGPs, and the SEL method performs equally well as GMDL in this Monte Carlo exercise; BS and WBS show the worst overall performance.([Section 4, p.17](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.05265v1#page=17 "Notice that WBSMDL outperforms all methods investigated in Table 2 (including the global method GMDL) for both performance metrics in all investigated DGPs."))
- **c9** Computational cost: in the long-series experiment (DGP B, T up to 16,384, 100 replications), the slowest local algorithm takes about twelve minutes for the largest sample size, while the global GMDL takes more than eleven minutes at T = 4,096 and was too demanding for larger samples over 100 replications.([Section 4, p.19](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.05265v1#page=19 "It takes about twelve minutes on a standard computer for the slowest local algorithm to run for the largest sample size."))
