## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** SSD versus Mamba's selective SSM: the A structure is simplified from diagonal to scalar times identity and a larger head dimension (typically P = 64 or 128, versus P = 1 in Mamba) is used; the authors describe this as slightly decreasing expressive power for significant training efficiency gains, enabling matrix multiplication units.([Section 2.4, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.21060v1#page=5 "Compared to the original selective SSM, these changes can be viewed as slightly decreasing the expressive power in return for significant training efficiency improvements."))
- **c2** Theory: SSMs are equivalent to semiseparable matrices (Section 3); linear attention is generalized to structured masked attention (SMA); 1-semiseparable SMA is a diagonal SSM with scalar-identity A (Corollary 5.1), and any SMA that is an autoregressive process of bounded order must have a semiseparable mask (Theorem 5.2). Remark 7 adds that general semiseparable SMA is strictly more expressive than 1-SS SMA and cannot be described by a standard SSM.([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.21060v1#page=2 "We also prove that any kernel attention method possessing a fast recurrent form must be an SSM."))
- **c3** SSD algorithm cost: with state dimension, head dimension and chunk length all equal to N, the block-decomposition algorithm needs O(TN^2) FLOPs and O(TN) memory and consists mainly of (N, N) matrix multiplications; the comparison table lists training FLOPs T^2 N for attention versus T N^2 for SSM and SSD, inference FLOPs TN versus N^2, and state size T versus N.([Section 6.3, p.22](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.21060v1#page=22 "The work consists primarily of matrix multiplications on matrices of shape (N, N)."))
- **c4** Speed as measured (A100 80GB PCIe): the SSD kernel is stated to be 2-8x faster than Mamba's fused associative scan and faster than FlashAttention-2 from sequence length 2K; however, the authors note the full Mamba-2 model might not be as efficient to train as a Transformer at short sequence lengths (e.g. 2K), since it has L SSD layers where a Transformer of equal parameters has L/2 attention layers.([Section 9.3, p.31](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.21060v1#page=31 "might not be as efficient to train as Transformer at short sequence length"))
- **c5** Block design and stability: Mamba-2 produces A, X, B, C with one parallel projection at the start of the block (amenable to Megatron-style tensor parallelism) and adds a normalization layer before the output projection because instabilities tended to arise in larger models in preliminary experiments; the ablation reports the extra normalization slightly improves performance and helps training stability at larger scales.([Section 7.1, p.23](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.21060v1#page=23 "In preliminary experiments, we found that instabilities were prone to arising in larger models."))
- **c6** Language modeling (same setup as Mamba: GPT-3 sizes 125M-2.7B, the Pile, recipe of Brown et al.; Chinchilla scaling with Mamba and Transformer++ baselines; zero-shot comparison with Pythia trained on the same tokenizer, data and 300B tokens): the authors state Mamba-2 2.7B outperforms Mamba-2.8B, Pythia-2.8B and Pythia-6.9B.([Introduction, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.21060v1#page=3 "For example, Mamba-2 with 2.7B parameters trained on 300B tokens on the Pile outperforms Mamba-2.8B, Pythia-2.8B and even Pythia-6.9B trained on the same dataset."))
- **c7** Recall weakness and MQAR: citing De et al. and Jelassi et al., phonebook look-up tasks are described as challenging for recurrent models such as SSMs due to finite state capacity; on a harder MQAR setting Mamba-1 struggles while Mamba-2 performs well even at equal state size (N = 16), for reasons the authors say they are not sure of, and increasing N to 64 and 256 consistently helps.([Section 9.1, p.30](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.21060v1#page=30 "(We are not sure which aspect of the architecture is the predominant factor, which remains a question to explore in future work.)"))
- **c8** Hybrids: around 10% attention layers performed best in a 350M/48-layer, 7B-token experiment, and at 2.7B/300B tokens adding 6 attention layers to 58 SSD layers improved over pure Mamba-2 and Transformer++ (which are around the same quality); the authors hypothesize that attention acts as a retrieval mechanism so the model need not compress all context into the SSM state.([Section 9.2.3, p.30](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.21060v1#page=30 "We hypothesize that the SSM layers function well as a general sequence-to-sequence mapping, and attention layers act as a retrieval mechanism to quickly refer to previous tokens in the sequence instead of forcing the model to compress all the context to its memory (SSM states)."))
