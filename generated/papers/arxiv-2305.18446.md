## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Motivation (citing Grinsztajn et al., 2022): on a recently published tabular benchmark, deep neural networks still fall behind tree-based models.([Abstract, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.18446v2#page=1 "However, based on a recently published tabular benchmark, we can see deep neural networks still fall behind tree-based models on tabular datasets (Grinsztajn et al., 2022)."))
- **c2** Design hypothesis: the authors argue that column importances in tabular data are not invariant across samples and can be grouped into multiple modalities; Trompt separates learning into intrinsic column information (analogous to a pre-trained model) and sample-specific feature importances (analogous to prompts), and learns separated column importances instead of focusing on interactions among columns as transformer-based models do.([Section 2.3, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.18446v2#page=3 "We argue that the column importances of tabular data are not invariant for all samples and can be grouped into multiple modalities."))
- **c3** Architecture: column embeddings and prompt embeddings are input independent and trainable; prompt embeddings are fused with the previous cell's output (a zero tensor for the first cell) and multiplied with the column embeddings, followed by a softmax over the column axis, to obtain feature importances per prompt; categorical features are embedded with an embedding layer and numerical features with a dense layer, as in prior work.([Section 3.1.1, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.18446v2#page=4 "Notice that the column embeddings are not connected to the input and the prompt embeddings are fused with the previous cell’s output."))
- **c4** Protocol on Grinsztajn45 (45 datasets, medium- and large-sized, numerical-only and heterogeneous): the authors follow its train/test split, preprocessing and metrics, omit MLP and HistGradientBoostingTree because of incomplete results in the benchmark, add LightGBM and CatBoost, and give Trompt a search space of only 40 parameter combinations, smaller than the hundreds explored for the other models, due to limited computing resources.([Appendix A.4, p.13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.18446v2#page=13 "Due to limited computing resources, we have chosen a small search space (Table 30) consisting of 40 parameter combinations."))
- **c5** Search-curve handling: because the benchmark truncates every model's random search at the minimum iteration count among compared models, the authors duplicated (oversampled) Trompt's grid-search results beyond the lowest iteration count so that the other models, whose search results come from Grinsztajn45, keep their original number of search iterations.([Appendix A.4, p.13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.18446v2#page=13 "To avoid unfairly truncating random search results of other models, and compromising the low search iterations of Trompt, we duplicated the grid search results of Trompt to exceed the lowest search iteration count among the models provided by Grinsztajn45."))
- **c6** Within-paper relation, classification: on medium-sized tasks Trompt's curve is above the other deep models (SAINT, FT-Transformer, ResNet) and narrows the gap to tree-based models, seeming to join the leading cluster of four tree-based models on heterogeneous features; on large-sized tasks tree-based models remain in the lead but the gap to deep networks is obscure, which the authors say echoes that deep networks require more samples.([Section 4.2.1, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.18446v2#page=6 "On the large-sized classification tasks, tree-based models remain the leading positions but the gap to deep neural networks is obscure."))
- **c7** Within-paper relation, regression: on medium-sized tasks Trompt is comparable to random forest with numerical features only, and on heterogeneous features it narrows the gap but is below all tree-based models; on large-sized numerical-only tasks it is slightly worse than SAINT and FT-Transformer at the end of the search.([Section 4.2.2, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.18446v2#page=7 "On the tasks with heterogeneous features, Trompt narrows the gap but is below all the tree-based models."))
- **c8** Interpretability: following TabNet's setup, feature importances (prompt-weighted Mimportance averaged over cells) are visualized on Syn2/Syn4 (20 randomly picked test samples) and compared with tree models' top-3 features on the mushroom dataset; the authors conclude Trompt's important features are consistent with those of tree-based models.([Section 4.4, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.18446v2#page=8 "Overall speaking, the important features selected by Trompt are consistent with those by tree-based models, and can therefore be used in various analyses that are familiar in the field of machine learning."))
- **c9** On datasets chosen by FT-Transformer, Trompt was not hyperparameter-searched, FT-Transformer numbers were taken from its original paper, results are averaged over three seeds, and Trompt is generally comparable or slightly inferior to FT-Transformer with default hyperparameters; on SAINT's datasets numbers were likewise copied from SAINT's paper and Trompt's are from a single seed.([Appendix B.2, p.35](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.18446v2#page=35 "It’s important to note that due to limited computing resources, Trompt did not undergo hyperparameter search."))
