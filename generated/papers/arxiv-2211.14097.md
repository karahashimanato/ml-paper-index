## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Credible sets for a single change: the posterior over the change location (vector alpha) gives both the MAP point estimate and a credible set formed by the smallest set of time instances whose posterior probabilities sum above p; the set is not necessarily an interval.([Section 2, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.14097v3#page=6 "We highlight that the credible set built through the above is not necessarily an interval."))
- **c2** Theory (Theorem 1, single change point only): under alpha-mixing with exponentially decaying coefficients, a 2+delta moment condition (no Gaussianity), the change away from the boundaries (min{t0, T - t0} > cT), a non-negligible variance ratio and proper priors with pi_t of order 1/T, the MAP estimator localizes at rate sqrt(T) log^{1+eps} T, and at sqrt(T log^{1+eps} T) for independent Gaussian data (the Binary Segmentation rate); the authors state it remains unknown whether it is minimax optimal.([Section 2.1, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.14097v3#page=8 "However, in the scenario of independent Gaussian data, it remains unknown whether our estimator is minimax optimal as the Wild Binary Segmentation algorithm from Wang et al. (2021b)."))
- **c3** Model for multiple changes: L (an upper bound, L >= K needed) single-change components are multiplied; unlike product partition models the model enforces no minimum spacing and nothing prevents two components selecting the same instance, choices motivated by the need for a fast algorithm.([Section 3, p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.14097v3#page=10 "Also note that, as opposed to PPM, model (9) does not enforce any minimum spacing between change points"))
- **c4** MCMC difficulty (citing Cappello et al. 2023, a paper co-authored by the first author): a Gibbs sampler for PRISCA is available but is expected to mix very slowly because the location vectors are highly dependent, and without a very good initialization MCMC chains in Bayesian change point detection often fail to converge.([Section 3.2, p.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.14097v3#page=11 "The deployment of MCMC in Bayesian change point detection commonly suffers from this problem, and lacking a very good initialization (e.g., the output of another change point detection procedure), chains often fail to converge; see Cappello et al. (2023)."))
- **c5** Decision rule and cost: a component counts as a detected change only if its credible set has at most T/2 instances (a threshold the authors call somewhat arbitrary but report results to be insensitive to); overlapping sets are removed in post-processing. Each iteration costs O(TL) worst case and few iterations are typically needed.([Section 3.2, p.12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.14097v3#page=12 "Thus, for typical datasets Algorithm 1 has an effective overall computational complexity of O(TL)."))
- **c6** Approximation: Algorithm 1 is shown to be a mean-field VB approximation of the PRISCA posterior (block-wise coordinate ascent on the ELBO), which converges to a stationary point of the ELBO (Proposition 2, assuming 0 < a0, sigma^2 < infinity); the ELBO change is used as the stopping rule. VB is known to underestimate uncertainty.([Section 3, p.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.14097v3#page=14 "Proposition 1 and Corollary 2 establish that Algorithm 1 is a VB approximation to the posterior distribution of PRISCA, where the approximation lies in the fact that we have factorized the L effects using a mean-field approximation."))
- **c7** Simulation protocol (inspired by Killick et al. 2012): zero-mean Gaussian sequences, T in {200, 500, 1000}, K = floor(T^{1/2}/4) changes at random locations with minimum spacing min{T^{1/2}, 30}, segment variances lognormal, 300 data sets per T; compared with PELT, BINSEG and SEGNEI (R package changepoint) and the Bayesian MCP (given the true K); a change is detected if within min{T^{1/2}, 30}/2. PRISCA uses a0 = .001, L = floor(T/30). Credible sets do not attain the target p but are relatively close, which the authors attribute to VB underestimating uncertainty.([Section 5, p.20](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.14097v3#page=20 "We can see that PRISCA’s sets do not attain the targeted p level, but they are relatively close."))
- **c8** Hedge on the comparison: although PRISCA-based methods had the lowest bias in K and Hausdorff distance in this particular study, the authors explicitly do not claim superiority over state-of-the-art estimators, only comparable point-estimation accuracy plus uncertainty quantification, and orders-of-magnitude faster run time than the competing Bayesian methods tested.([Section 5, p.20](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.14097v3#page=20 "We do not claim that PRISCA is better than state-of-the-art estimators."))
- **c9** Limits of theory: consistency is proved only for the single change point problem; the authors explain that for Bayesian multiple change point estimators one defines a distribution over partitions whose marginal likelihoods are not closed-form, making theory difficult, which may explain the small theoretical literature.([Section 7, p.30](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.14097v3#page=30 "This challenge can explain the relatively small literature on theoretical results for Bayesian change point methodologies."))
