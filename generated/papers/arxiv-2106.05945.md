## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** The authors show that while KD can improve student generalization, there often remains a surprisingly large discrepancy between teacher and student predictive distributions, even when the student has the capacity to match the teacher perfectly; they identify difficulties in optimization as a key reason, and report that matching the teacher more closely paradoxically does not always improve generalization.([Abstract, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.05945v2#page=1 "there often remains a surprisingly large discrepancy between the predictive distributions of the teacher and the student, even in cases when the student has the capacity to perfectly match the teacher."))
- **c2** Objective (Hinton et al.): L_s = alpha * L_NLL + (1 - alpha) * L_KD with L_KD = -tau^2 * sum_j sigma_j(z_t / tau) log sigma_j(z_s / tau); as tau -> infinity the gradient approaches z_t - z_s (equal weight to every logit). Because the study focuses on fidelity, alpha = 0 is used for all main-text experiments (only a limited alpha ablation in the appendix).([Section 3.1, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.05945v2#page=3 "Since we focus on distillation ﬁdelity, we choose α = 0 for all experiments in the main text to avoid any confounding from true labels"))
- **c3** Metrics: generalization is measured by top-1 accuracy, NLL and ECE; fidelity by average top-1 agreement and average predictive KL from teacher to student. Because agreement can be confounded by accuracy, they also distill a second student through an identical procedure from an independent teacher, so that a generic gain in generalization can be distinguished from a gain in fidelity.([Section 3.2, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.05945v2#page=3 "To account for such confounding when evaluating the distillation of a student s from a teacher t, we also evaluate another student s′ distilled through an identical procedure from an independent teacher."))
- **c4** In self-distillation (ResNet-56 on CIFAR-100) the student can exceed the teacher, which the authors note is only possible because distillation fails; for a 3-ResNet-56 ensemble teacher, fidelity becomes positively correlated with generalization but a significant fidelity gap remains.([Section 4.2, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.05945v2#page=4 "This result is only possible by virtue of failing at the distillation procedure: if the student matched the teacher perfectly then the student could not outperform the teacher."))
- **c5** Distilling a 5-ResNet-56 ensemble into a ResNet-56 on CIFAR-100, the augmentations best for generalization (MixUp, GAN) are not the best for fidelity, and even the best-fidelity policy reaches only 86% test agreement (baseline with tau = 4: 84.5%); since the gain from augmentation is small compared with logit tempering, the authors judge it very unlikely that an insufficient quantity of teacher labels is the primary obstacle to high fidelity.([Section 5.1, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.05945v2#page=7 "even the best augmentation policy, Mixup (τ = 4), only achieves a modest 86% test agreement."))
- **c6** Distilling on held-out data the teacher did not see (D1) gives higher fidelity but lower test accuracy than distilling on the teacher's own training data (D0); distilling on both combines their best attributes. The best agreement is still only around 85%, and the authors conclude the evidence does not support blaming poor fidelity on the wrong choice of distillation data.([Section 5.2, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.05945v2#page=7 "In fact, the best teacher-student agreement is only around 85%, no improvement when compared to the results from extensive data augmentation in the last section."))
- **c7** On the distillation data itself, the student often fails to match the teacher: enlarging the data with GAN samples or heavier augmentation lowers train agreement (to 60% for combined augmentations in self-distillation), and the authors write that the results suggest the optimizer cannot reach high fidelity even on the distillation dataset in these settings.([Section 6.1, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.05945v2#page=8 "However, the results presented in this section suggest that in practice the optimization method is unable to achieve high ﬁdelity even on the distillation dataset when extensive data augmentation or synthetic data is used."))
- **c8** In ResNet-20 (LayerNorm) self-distillation on CIFAR-100, training longer or switching to Adam does not substantially improve fidelity; initializing the student as a convex combination of teacher and random weights shows that students initialized far from the teacher converge to a distinct sub-optimal basin, while sharing only the teacher's initial weights gives no observable functional change. The authors conclude the sub-optimal convergence appears to be a consequence of optimization dynamics, not simply initialization bias.([Section 6.2, p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.05945v2#page=10 "Furthermore, the suboptimal convergence of knowledge distillation appears to be a consequence of the optimization dynamics speciﬁcally, and not simply initialization bias."))
