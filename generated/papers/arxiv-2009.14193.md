## 主張

- **c1** The coverage guarantee P(Y in C(X)) >= 1 - alpha is marginal over X and Y: it holds on average, not for a particular image.([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.14193v5#page=2 "Note that the guarantee in Eq. (1) is marginal over X and Y —it holds on average, not for a particular image X."))
- **c2** Motivation: the naive approach (include classes by Platt-scaled softmax probability until the sum exceeds 1 - alpha) fails because CNN probabilities are known to be incorrect (citing Nixon et al., 2019), so the sets do not achieve coverage, and badly miscalibrated tail probabilities lead to large sets.([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.14193v5#page=2 "There are two problems with naive: ﬁrst, the probabilities output by CNNs are known to be incorrect (Nixon et al., 2019), so the sets from naive do not achieve"))
- **c3** Theorem 1 (conformal calibration): assumes an i.i.d. calibration set not used for model training, nested set families that eventually contain all labels, and picks the smallest tau with empirical coverage >= ceil((n+1)(1-alpha))/n; it also holds under exchangeability, and a matching upper bound 1 - alpha + 1/(n+1) holds for most families (roughly, when sets grow smoothly in tau). The authors state the result is not new.([Section 2.1, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.14193v5#page=5 "The result is not new—a special case of this result leveraging sample-splitting ﬁrst appears in the regression setting in Papadopoulos et al. (2002), and the core idea of conformal prediction was introduced even earlier; see Vovk et al. (2005)."))
- **c4** Three desiderata: coverage, small set size, and adaptiveness (smaller sets for easy, larger for hard examples). Coverage and size compete, and size and adaptiveness are also often in tension; e.g. always predicting a set of size five could achieve coverage without being adaptive.([Section 2 (Methods), p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.14193v5#page=4 "Coverage and size are obviously competing objectives, but size and adaptiveness are also often in tension."))
- **c5** Why regularize: in their experiments APS sets are larger than necessary because APS is sensitive to noisy probability estimates far down the class list, where the ordering of unlikely classes is mostly random (a 'permutation problem'); RAPS regularization makes the algorithm avoid these unreliable tail probabilities.([Section 2.3, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.14193v5#page=6 "This noise leads to a permutation problem of unlikely classes, where ordering of the classes with small probability estimates is determined mostly by random chance."))
- **c6** Protocol: nine pretrained torchvision ImageNet classifiers; before naive, APS and RAPS, classifiers are temperature/Platt scaled on the calibration set; randomized versions of the algorithms are used; Imagenet-Val experiments use 100 trials with random calibration and evaluation subsets of 20K each, and RAPS parameters are chosen from data (Appendix E: k_reg and lambda picked on separate tuning data, m = 10000 for ImageNet and 1000 for ImageNet-V2, which the authors say does not invalidate the guarantee because calibration still uses fresh data).([Section 3 (Experiments), p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.14193v5#page=7 "Before applying naive, APS, or RAPS, we calibrated the classiﬁers using the standard temperature scaling/Platt scaling procedure as in Guo et al. (2017) on the calibration set."))
- **c7** ImageNet-V2 (5K calibration and evaluation sets): the authors state the method still provides coverage for models trained on a different distribution as long as the conformal calibration set comes from the new distribution; coverage variance is higher because of less data.([Section 3.2, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.14193v5#page=8 "The result shows that our method can still provide coverage even for models trained on different distributions, as long as the conformal calibration set comes from the new distribution."))
- **c8** Size vs adaptiveness trade-off: with large lambda, RAPS undercovers hard examples and must compensate with larger sets for easy examples to keep 1 - alpha marginal coverage; the size increase is described as slight because easy images are more common. If undesired, lambda can be chosen to optimize adaptiveness instead.([Section 3 (Experiment 4: Adaptiveness of RAPS on Imagenet), p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.14193v5#page=8 "Since RAPS with large λ undercovers hard examples, it must compensate by taking larger sets for easy examples to ensure the 1 −α marginal coverage guarantee."))
- **c9** Conditional coverage: distribution-free conditional coverage is impossible (citing Vovk 2012; Lei & Wasserman 2014). Stratifying coverage by set size shows APS overcovers large sets and undercovers easy ones, so it does not achieve exact conditional coverage. The authors argue that for high-signal problems exact conditional coverage requires a perfect classifier, so it is not the right goal at realistic sample sizes, and propose requiring coverage within every set-size stratum (measured by SSCV) instead.([Section 4, p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.14193v5#page=10 "Therefore, conditional coverage isn’t the right goal for prediction sets with realistic sample sizes."))
