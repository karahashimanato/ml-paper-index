## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** The paper analyses a random forests model suggested by Breiman in a short draft, which the author describes as very close to the original algorithm, and shows that it is consistent and adapts to sparsity (its rate depends only on the number of strong features, not on the number of noise variables).([Abstract, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.0208v3#page=1 "In this paper, we oﬀer an in-depth analysis of a random forests model suggested by Breiman in [12], which is very close to the original algorithm."))
- **c2** Simplifying assumption: the randomizing variable Theta is independent of X and of the training sample, which excludes any bootstrapping/resampling step and any data-dependent split search on the training observations (a second, independent sample is allowed); each tree splits ceil(log2 k_n) times, choosing coordinate j with probability p_nj and cutting at the midpoint of the chosen side.([Introduction (The model), p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.0208v3#page=4 "This excludes in particular any bootstrapping or resampling step in the training set."))
- **c3** Theorem 2.1 covers the 'purely random' case (coordinate chosen uniformly, p_nj = 1/d), which is consistent if k_n goes to infinity and k_n/n to 0; the author calls this a radically simplified version of practical random forests that does not explain the algorithm's good performance.([Asymptotic analysis (Theorem 2.1), p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.0208v3#page=6 "This is however a radically simpliﬁed version of the random forests used in practice, which does not explain the good performance of the algorithm."))
- **c4** Proposition 2.1 (X uniform, bounded conditional variance): the forest variance is O(k_n / (n (log k_n)^{S/2d})), so even with k_n = n it still tends to 0, whereas a single tree's variance is of order k_n/n; the log term comes from the Theta-averaging (correlation between trees). The author believes this offers a perspective on why unpruned trees still work, while noting that the bias bound (Proposition 2.2) contains a term e^{-n/2k_n} that prevents the extreme choice k_n = n.([Asymptotic analysis (Proposition 2.1), p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.0208v3#page=9 "We believe that it provides an interesting perspective on why random forests are still able to do a good job, despite the fact that individual trees are not pruned."))
- **c5** Proposition 2.2 / Corollary 2.1 (X uniform, r* Lipschitz on the S strong coordinates, p_nj = (1/S)(1 + xi_nj) for strong j with xi_nj log n -> 0): the bias rate depends on S rather than d, and the overall rate n^{-0.75/(S log 2 + 0.75)} is faster than the d-dimensional rate n^{-2/(d+2)} as soon as S <= floor(0.54 d).([Asymptotic analysis (Proposition 2.2), p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.0208v3#page=9 "In other words, when the number of active variables is less than (roughly) half of the ambient dimension, the bias of the random forests regression estimate decreases to 0 much faster than the usual rate."))
- **c6** Remark 2: the reduced-dimensional rate is strictly slower than the S-dimensional optimal rate n^{-2/(S+2)}, and the author does not know whether that optimal rate can be achieved by the algorithm.([Asymptotic analysis (Remark 2), p.12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.0208v3#page=12 "We do not know whether the latter rate can be achieved by the algorithm."))
- **c7** Gap to Breiman's algorithm: a CART-like procedure that chooses the best split among M_n random coordinates on a second sample is described as close to random forests, but the original algorithm searches cuts on the training data itself; the author does not know whether the results hold when Theta depends on the training data (as in CART) and calls the heuristic argument a rough approach needing more theoretical work.([Discussion, p.16](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.0208v3#page=16 "We do not know whether our results are still true if Θ depends on Dn (as in the CART algorithm), but the analysis does not appear to be simple."))
- **c8** Variable importance: the out-of-bag permutation importance used by random forests has, as far as the author is aware, no systematic mathematical study; such a study is said to be beyond the paper's scope.([Discussion, p.16](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.0208v3#page=16 "As far as we are aware, there is to date no systematic mathematical study of this criterion."))
- **c9** Simulation (Sinus, Friedman #1, Tree models; test set of 50 000; 100 data sets): Breiman's forests (R randomForest, mtry automatically tuned by the package, 1000 trees, minimum node size 5) are compared with an alternative that fixes the number of terminal nodes to ceil(n/5) but otherwise splits on the actual sample like CART; the author reports very similar overall performance and takes this as confirming that the analysed model is a good approximation. Note: the alternative still uses data-dependent CART splits, unlike the analysed model.([A small simulation study, p.20](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.0208v3#page=20 "This conﬁrms our idea that the model discussed in the present paper is a good approximation of the authentic Breiman’s forests."))
