## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Collapse avoidance: other methods avoid collapse with a contrastive loss, clustering constraints, a predictor or batch normalizations; DINO can be stabilized with multiple normalizations but can also work with only centering and sharpening of the momentum teacher outputs. Centering depends only on first-order batch statistics (an EMA-updated bias added to the teacher output) and sharpening uses a low teacher softmax temperature; the authors describe this choice as trading stability for less dependence on the batch.([Section 3.1 (Avoiding collapse), p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.14294v2#page=4 "it can also work with only a centering and sharpening of the momentum teacher outputs to avoid model collapse."))
- **c2** Why both operations: there are two forms of collapse (uniform output, or output dominated by one dimension). Centering avoids the dominant-dimension collapse but encourages a uniform output, and sharpening has the opposite effect; in the collapse study, if either operation is missing the KL divergence between teacher and student converges to zero (a collapse), and applying both balances the effects.([Section 5.3, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.14294v2#page=9 "The centering avoids the collapse induced by a dominant dimension, but encourages an uniform output."))
- **c3** Teacher choice: using the student from the previous iteration or a copy of the student as teacher does not converge in their setting (it would require more normalizations), while a teacher from the previous epoch does not collapse; the momentum (EMA) teacher gives the best k-NN performance, and the authors observe that it constantly outperforms the student during training, interpreting it as Polyak-Ruppert averaging (a model ensemble guiding the student).([Section 5.2, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.14294v2#page=9 "In our setting, using a teacher based on a recent version of the student does not converge."))
- **c4** Component ablation (ViT-S/16, 300 epochs): without momentum the framework does not work and more advanced operations such as Sinkhorn-Knopp are required to avoid collapse; with momentum, SK has little impact; multi-crop and the cross-entropy loss are important; adding a predictor has little impact, whereas it is critical in BYOL to prevent collapse (citing [16, 30]).([Section 5.1, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.14294v2#page=8 "First, we observe that in the absence of momentum, our framework does not work (row 2) and more advanced operations, SK for example, are required to avoid collapse (row 9)."))
- **c5** Evaluation protocols: linear evaluation on frozen features and fine-tuning are both used, but the authors note both are sensitive to hyperparameters with large run-to-run variance when the learning rate varies; they therefore also use a weighted k-NN classifier on frozen features, sweeping k and finding 20 NN consistently best for most runs, which needs no other hyperparameter tuning or data augmentation.([Section 3.2, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.14294v2#page=5 "However, both evaluations are sensitive to hyperparameters, and we observe a large variance in accuracy between runs when varying the learning rate for example."))
- **c6** Linear probe recipe: projection head removed, linear classifier trained with SGD (batch 1024) for 100 epochs on ImageNet without weight decay, with random resized crops and horizontal flips, reporting central-crop top-1; the learning rate is swept for each model; for ViT-S the [CLS] tokens of the last 4 layers are concatenated, for ViT-B the final [CLS] token is concatenated with average-pooled patch tokens. Accuracies are reported on the ImageNet validation set (Table 2); no separate held-out split for the learning-rate sweep was found in the text (searched for held-out, validation, val set, minival).([Appendix F.2, p.19](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.14294v2#page=19 "For each model, we sweep the learning rate value."))
- **c7** k-NN emergence: with ViT-S, DINO's k-NN accuracy is almost on par with its linear accuracy; the authors state this property emerges only for DINO with ViT, not with other self-supervised methods nor with ResNet-50. The BYOL, MoCov2 and SwAV ViT-S baselines in Table 2 were run by the authors (marked with an asterisk).([Section 4.1, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.14294v2#page=6 "This property emerges only when using DINO with ViT architectures, and does not appear with other existing self-supervised methods nor with a ResNet-50."))
- **c8** Compute and setup: pre-training on ImageNet without labels with AdamW and batch size 1024 (16 GPUs for ViT-S/16), BYOL augmentations (color jittering, Gaussian blur, solarization) plus multi-crop; a ViT-S/16 DINO reaches its reported linear accuracy with two 8-GPU servers for 3 days (Table 8 lists running time and peak memory per multi-crop variant).([Section 5.4, p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.14294v2#page=10 "Overall, training DINO with Vision Transformers achieves 76.1 top-1 accuracy using two 8-GPU servers for 3 days."))
