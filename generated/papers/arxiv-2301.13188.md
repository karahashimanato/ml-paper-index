## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Definition: because verbatim definitions do not suit high-resolution images, memorization is defined approximately: an example is extractable if an efficient algorithm that does not receive it outputs an image within distance delta (normalized Euclidean l2 by default), and (k, l, delta)-eidetic memorized if, additionally, at most k training examples are within delta of it (k counts near-duplicates).([Defining Image Memorization, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.13188v1#page=4 "Instead, we deﬁne a notion of approximate memorization based on image similarity metrics."))
- **c2** The definition is intentionally conservative: e.g. a recognizable but not near-identical generated photograph of a person (Barack Obama) does not count as memorized, although such generations could still cause privacy harms. A footnote stresses this is a very restricted definition of memorization.([Defining Image Memorization (Restrictions of our definition), p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.13188v1#page=4 "Our deﬁnition of extraction is intentionally conservative as compared to what privacy concerns one might ultimately have."))
- **c3** Stable Diffusion attack setup: the search is biased toward training images that are near-duplicated (CLIP-embedding cosine similarity) because these are much more likely to be memorized; for the 350,000 most-duplicated examples, 500 images per caption are generated (175 million in total) and flagged when at least 10 of 500 generations are near-identical. 94 images were (l2, 0.15)-extracted and 13 more were judged near-copies by manual inspection of the top 1000.([Extracting Data from Stable Diffusion, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.13188v1#page=4 "To reduce the computational load of our attack, as is done in [65], we bias our search towards duplicated training examples because these are orders of magnitude more likely to be memorized than non-duplicated examples [47, 41]."))
- **c4** Role of duplication: little eidetic memorization is found for k < 100, which the authors say is expected because the prompts were chosen from highly duplicated images (the figure caption calls the result an upper bound for this reason); they conclude duplication is a major factor behind extraction.([Extracting Data from Stable Diffusion (Measuring eidetic memorization), p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.13188v1#page=6 "These results show that duplication is a major factor behind training data extraction."))
- **c5** Imagen (2B parameters, non-public data): 23 of the top-1000 most duplicated prompts yielded memorized examples, and 3 of the 500 most out-of-distribution (unique) images were extracted, whereas no memorization was found for Stable Diffusion even among the 10,000 most-outlier samples; the authors believe higher capacity, more training iterations and a smaller dataset explain the difference.([Extracting Outlier Examples, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.13188v1#page=7 "Thus, Imagen appears less private than Stable Diffusion both on duplicated and non-duplicated images."))
- **c6** CIFAR-10: 16 diffusion models, each trained on a random half of CIFAR-10, each generate 2^16 images; an uncalibrated l2 threshold or perceptual distances (SSIM, CLIP) failed, so an image counts as extracted if its l2 distance to the nearest training image is abnormally low relative to its 50 nearest training neighbours. The authors note this is a check of capability rather than an attack per se.([Untargeted Extraction, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.13188v1#page=8 "Using the above methodology we identify 1,280 unique extracted images from the CIFAR-10 dataset (2.5% of the entire dataset)."))
- **c7** Diffusion vs GANs on off-the-shelf CIFAR-10 models (1 million unconditional generations each, non-duplicated images counted): diffusion models memorize more than GANs even at similar FID, and both families memorize more as FID improves.([Comparing Diffusion Models to GANs (Data extraction results), p.13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.13188v1#page=13 "Overall, we ﬁnd that diffusion models memorize more data than GANs, even when the GANs reach similar performance, e.g., the best DDPM model memorizes 2× more than StyleGAN-ADA but reaches the same FID."))
- **c8** Deduplication as a defense: retraining on CIFAR-10 after removing 5,275 near-duplicates (imagededup similarity > 0.85) reduced regenerated examples from 1280 to 986; the authors expect deduplication to be more effective for larger datasets such as Stable Diffusion's. DP-SGD caused training to diverge even at epsilon around 50.([Defenses and Recommendations (Deduplicating Training Data), p.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.13188v1#page=14 "While not a substantial drop, these results show that deduplication can mitigate memorization."))
- **c9** Open question: the success of the attacks does not settle whether large models generate novel output or copy and interpolate training examples; the authors also note that more human-aligned definitions of data copying will need a more comprehensive analysis than their l2-based measurement.([Discussion and Conclusion, p.15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.13188v1#page=15 "If our extraction attacks had failed, it may have refuted the hypothesis that models copy and interpolate training data; but because our attacks succeed, this question remains open."))
