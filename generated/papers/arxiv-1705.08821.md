## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Hidden confounding: if a confounder is hidden or unmeasured, the effect of the intervention on the outcome cannot be estimated in the general case without further assumptions (citing Pearl).([Introduction, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.08821v2#page=1 "On the the other hand, if a confounder is hidden or unmeasured, it is impossible in the general case (i.e. without further assumptions) to estimate the effect of the intervention on the outcome [40]."))
- **c2** Proxy variables (noisy views X of a hidden confounder Z) are commonly used, but treating them as ordinary confounders is often incorrect and would induce bias (citing prior work; a simple binary example is given in Appendix A).([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.08821v2#page=2 "it’s well known [20, 15, 18, 31, 41] that it is often incorrect to treat the proxies X as if they are ordinary confounders, as this would induce bias."))
- **c3** Prior proxy-based methods are guaranteed to recover the true effect only under strong assumptions: a categorical hidden confounder with a known number of categories, or a linear-Gaussian model; the authors argue the nature of Z (continuous, categorical, mixed or high-dimensional) is not known in practice.([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.08821v2#page=2 "In particular, it is assumed that the hidden confounder is either categorical with known number of categories, or that the model is linear-Gaussian."))
- **c4** Key assumption: the causal model of Figure 1 holds and the joint p(Z, X, t, y) can be approximately recovered from (X, t, y) alone; the authors note this is impossible if the hidden confounder has no relation to the observed variables, and list cases where it is possible (e.g. three independent views of Z). They also note (citing [51]) that certain VAEs can recover a large class of latent-variable models, with the caveat that optimization is not guaranteed to reach the true minimum.([Identification of causal effect, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.08821v2#page=3 "We further assume that the joint distribution p (Z, X, t, y) of the latent confounders Z and the observed confounders X can be approximately recovered solely from the observations (X, t, y)."))
- **c5** Estimands and identification: ITE(x) = E[y|X = x, do(t = 1)] - E[y|X = x, do(t = 0)] (also called CATE) and ATE = E[ITE(x)]; Theorem 1 states that recovering p(Z, X, t, y) recovers the ITE under the Figure 1 model, by back-door adjustment over Z.([Identification of causal effect, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.08821v2#page=3 "Theorem 1. If we recover p (Z, X, t, y) then we recover the ITE under the causal model in Figure 1."))
- **c6** Caveat of the VAE approach: little theory is available on when learning with VAEs identifies the true model, though VAEs make substantially weaker assumptions about the data-generating process and the structure of the hidden confounders than prior proxy methods.([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.08821v2#page=2 "This has the disadvantage that little theory is currently available to justify when learning with VAEs can identify the true model."))
- **c7** Because the posterior over z depends on x, t and y, predicting for new subjects requires t and y before inferring z; CEVAE therefore adds auxiliary distributions q(t|x) and q(y|x, t) whose log-likelihoods are added to the variational lower bound, and outcomes under do(t) are computed by averaging over 100 samples from the approximate posterior q(Z|X).([Causal effect variational autoencoder, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.08821v2#page=5 "Notice that for out of sample predictions, i.e. new subjects, we require to know the treatment assignment t along with its outcome y before inferring the distribution over z."))
- **c8** Twins benchmark construction: 11984 same-sex twin pairs both under 2 kg, 46 covariates; one twin is hidden to simulate an observational study, treatment assignment is based on GESTAT10 (gestation weeks, ordinal 0-9) plus the 45 other features, and proxies are the one-hot GESTAT10 coding replicated 3 times with each bit flipped independently with probability from 0.05 to 0.5 (0.5 meaning no direct information). The authors note other variables (e.g. incompetent cervix) may still be proxies for the confounder.([Binary treatment outcome on Twins, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.08821v2#page=7 "In order to simulate the case of hidden confounding with proxies, we based the treatment assignment on a single variable which is highly correlated with the outcome: GESTAT10, the number of gestation weeks prior to birth."))
- **c9** Scope limitation: the method does not currently deal with selection bias, which the authors leave to future work.([Introduction, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.08821v2#page=3 "Finally, we note that our method does not currently deal with the related problem of selection bias, and we leave this to future work."))
