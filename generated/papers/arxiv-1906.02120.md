## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Setting: the paper estimates the average effect of a binary treatment from observational data and works throughout in the 'no hidden confounding' setting, where the effect is identified if the covariates contain all confounding variables.([Introduction, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.02120v2#page=1 "We will work in this ‘no hidden confounding’ setting throughout the paper."))
- **c2** Motivation from the sufficiency of the propensity score (Theorem 2.1, citing Rosenbaum & Rubin 1983): if adjusting for X identifies the ATE, adjusting for g(X) also suffices, so covariate information relevant to the outcome but not the treatment is noise for adjustment and is expected to hurt finite-sample performance; a footnote cautions that with high-variance outcomes and small samples modeling all covariates may help as variance reduction.([Dragonnet, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.02120v2#page=3 "In words: it sufﬁces to adjust for only the information in X that is relevant for predicting the treatment."))
- **c3** Architecture: a deep net produces a representation Z(X); two 2-hidden-layer heads predict the outcome under control and treatment, and the propensity head is a simple linear map followed by a sigmoid, chosen so that the representation is tightly coupled to the estimated propensity score; removing the propensity head gives (essentially) TARNET (Shalit et al.).([Dragonnet, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.02120v2#page=3 "The simple map forces the representation layer to tightly couple to the estimated propensity scores."))
- **c4** Theory recalled (citing Chernozhukov et al. and van der Laan & Rose): if the tuple (Q-hat, g-hat, psi-hat) satisfies the efficient-influence-curve estimating equation, psi-hat is asymptotically robust in the double machine-learning sense and efficient; these guarantees require consistent Q-hat and g-hat. The A-IPTW estimator satisfies the equation but, with g-hat in denominators, can be unstable in finite samples.([Targeted Regularization, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.02120v2#page=4 "These asymptotic guarantees hold if (i) ˆQ and ˆg are consistent estimators for the conditional outcome and propensity scores, and (ii) the tuple satisﬁes the non-parametric estimating equation,"))
- **c5** Targeted regularization: minimizing the objective forces the estimating equation to hold at the optimum of epsilon, so psi_treg has the non-parametric asymptotic properties as long as Q_treg and g-hat are consistent; the authors argue consistency is plausible because the model can set epsilon to 0. Estimators satisfying the equation are also doubly robust (consistent if either the outcome or propensity model is consistent).([Targeted Regularization, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.02120v2#page=5 "Consistency is plausible—even with the addition of the targeted regularization term—because the model can choose to set ϵ to 0, which (essentially) recovers the original training objective."))
- **c6** Overlap: some ACIC 2018 datasets have overlap violations (P(T = 1|x) very close to 0 or 1 for many x); as a simple heuristic the authors exclude datasets where Dragonnet's held-out treatment accuracy exceeds 90%, leaving 101 datasets.([Experiments, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.02120v2#page=7 "Although overlap violations are an important area of study, this is not our focus and the methods of this paper are not expected to be appropriate in this setting."))
- **c7** Evaluation design: since ground truth is rarely available, evaluation relies on semi-synthetic data, which must be faithful to the real world; the paper uses IHDP (1000 NPCI realizations, 747 observations, following Shalit et al.) and the IBM ACIC 2018 framework built from linked birth and infant death data (63 data-generating settings; 3 randomly picked datasets of size 5k or 10k per setting).([Experiments, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.02120v2#page=7 "For the conclusions to be useful, the semi-synthetic data must have good ﬁdelity to the real world."))
- **c8** On IHDP, the de-facto standard benchmark for neural treatment-effect methods, the authors caution that its small sample size and limited simulation settings make conclusions difficult; they also note that the established 63/27/10 split procedure is not clearly motivated for parameter estimation and additionally report estimates using all data for both training and estimation.([Experiments, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.02120v2#page=8 "However, the small sample size and limited simulation settings of IHDP make it difﬁcult to draw conclusions about the methods."))
- **c9** The non-parametric guarantees are asymptotic; when datasets are split by the error of the baseline Q-only estimator, the authors report that TMLE and targeted regularization behave similarly when the initial estimate is good, while TMLE significantly degrades estimation when it is poor and targeted regularization does not; they say it appears that adapting the whole learning process avoids some bad finite-sample effects but they lack a theoretical explanation.([Experiments, p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.02120v2#page=10 "We do not have a satisfactory theoretical explanation for this."))
