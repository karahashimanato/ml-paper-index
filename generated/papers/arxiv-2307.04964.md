## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Reward model: a pretrained LM with a scalar head trained on pairwise comparisons with the log-sigmoid of the reward difference, plus (following Askell et al. 2021) an autoregressive LM loss on the preferred response weighted by beta_rm (set to 1). The RL reward is r(x, y) - eta * KL(pi_RL(y|x), pi_SFT(y|x)), which the authors say acts as an entropy bonus and keeps outputs close to what the RM saw in training.([Section 3, Reward Modeling, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.04964v2#page=4 "Additionally, we follow [27] to use imitation learning, which introduces the autoregressive LM loss on the preferred response of each pair, allowing the model to imitate the preferred response in each sentence pair."))
- **c2** RM data and failure cases: the English RM (LLaMA-7B) is trained on 160k HH-RLHF pairs (118k helpful, 42k harmless); the Chinese RM (OpenChineseLLaMA) on pairs labeled by hired professional annotators (39k in total). Inspecting the largest disagreements, the authors found that for Chinese data the RM preferred notably longer responses that fabricated facts, and for English data it gave low scores to honest responses acknowledging lack of information.([Section 4, HH Evaluation Results, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.04964v2#page=9 "For the Chinses test data, we observed that for each pair the response that RM gave a higher reward was notably longer compared to the other which is preferred by human, although more or less involving fabricating facts and making false claims."))
- **c3** RM accuracy is not enough: accuracy improvement slows markedly after about 200 steps (about 0.2 epochs) with accuracy comparable to a full epoch, but using the 200-step RM to initialize PPO gave unsatisfactory performance.([Section 4, Training Performance, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.04964v2#page=9 "Thus, accuracy alone is insufficient as a criterion for the RM."))
- **c4** Pattern collapse in vanilla PPO: the policy learns to cheat the RM through specific patterns; training loss converges stably and reward rises, but higher reward does not reflect better behaviour by human and GPT-4 evaluation. The authors propose monitoring perplexity, KL divergence to the reference model and response length (collapsed models give longer responses with lower perplexity), and report that the approximately linear root-KL vs PM-score relation proposed by Bai et al. (2022) appeared weak for smaller models.([Section 5.2, p.12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.04964v2#page=12 "This means that the reward scores and training losses do not indicate whether the PPO is optimizing correctly."))
- **c5** Token-level KL penalty: whereas Anthropic (Bai et al. 2022) used a small weight (0.001) and found no significant effect, the authors find the KL penalty critical to PPO stability and to scaling up training steps (results shown with the coefficient set to 0.05), and report that RLHF can significantly improve response quality with almost zero KL divergence from the original policy. Entropy bonus was very sensitive to parameter choice and implementation, and they recommend the KL penalty instead.([Section 5.3.2, p.15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.04964v2#page=15 "Instead, we find this constraint critical to the stability of PPO and allow further scaling up on the training step."))
- **c6** Initialization: critic initialization (from the RM or the SFT model) did not significantly affect convergence, and pre-training the critic before PPO improved stability, which they recommend in place of learning-rate warmup; a policy initialized without SFT was clearly incapable in PPO training (severe loss of language modeling ability), indicating that a supervised policy is indispensable.([Section 5.3.3, p.15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.04964v2#page=15 "In contrast, a policy model initialized without SFT training is clearly incapable in PPO training, which indicates that the construction of a supervised policy model is indispensable in RLHF."))
- **c7** PPO-max recipe: rewards are normalized and clipped using historical mean and variance, a KL penalty constrains the policy, the critic is initialized from the RM and pre-trained before PPO, global gradient clipping and a small experience buffer are used, and a pretraining LM loss (as in InstructGPT) plus value-loss clipping are added to reduce alignment tax.([PPO-max Setup, p.16](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.04964v2#page=16 "We normalize and clip the current group of rewards based on historical mean and variance records, and subsequently add a KL-penalty term to constrain the policy optimization."))
- **c8** Evaluation: RLHF (PPO-max) models are compared with their SFT models by human annotators and by GPT-4 as a judge (the authors note GPT-4 may not be a perfect evaluator), and against ChatGPT (gpt-3.5-turbo-0613) on harmless prompts with GPT-4 judging; the models still lag ChatGPT, but the English RLHF model reduced the defeat rate against ChatGPT from 45% (SFT) to 24%, and the Chinese one from 37% to 29%.([Evaluations and Discussions, p.19](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.04964v2#page=19 "Specifically, the RLHF model trained on English text managed to decrease the defeat rate from 45% to 24%."))
- **c9** Limitations: the study mainly uses a 7B model and does not investigate model or data scale; the RM data may be insufficient in quality and quantity; evaluation relies on manual and GPT-4 judgments rather than many benchmarks; the PPO work targets stability rather than final performance, and the reward score cannot reliably predict RLHF performance during training.([Limitations, p.20](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.04964v2#page=20 "Additionally, the reward score cannot reliably serve as an indicator for predicting RLHF performance during the training phase."))
