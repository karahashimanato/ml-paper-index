## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Validating against a single ground-truth parameter value is flawed: in their normal-normal example, a correctly coded analysis can appear to fail on a plausible but extreme dataset while an incorrectly coded model (wrong variances) appears to recover the truth; at the very least multiple ground truths are needed, and the prior defines which parameter values are relevant.([Section 2, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.06788v2#page=2 "Unfortunately this approach is ﬂawed, as demonstarted in a simple example."))
- **c2** The earlier procedure of Cook, Gelman and Rubin (2006) can run into problems with MCMC because empirical CDF values only asymptotically approach the true values; discretization (L + 1 possible positions) and autocorrelation also distort the test statistics. In the authors' Stan 2.17.1 linear regression demonstration, the histogram of estimated CDF values showed strong deviations from uniformity although Stan is known to be extremely accurate for that analysis.([Section 3, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.06788v2#page=4 "These issues make it difﬁcult to determine whether a deviation from normality is due to pre-asymptotic behavior or biases in the posterior computations."))
- **c3** SBC requires just one assumption, a generative model for the data; under it, the rank of the prior draw among L exact posterior draws for any one-dimensional function is uniform on the integers 0..L (Theorem 1), and the check is similar to checking the coverage of a credible interval under the assumed model.([Section 4, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.06788v2#page=4 "SBC requires just one assumption: that we have a generative model for our data."))
- **c4** Scope: the calibration concerns only the computation; it gives no guarantee that the posterior covers the ground truth for any single observation or that the model is rich enough to capture the truth. Validating the model assumptions requires predictive checks such as posterior predictive checks; the authors view both SBC and PPCs as part of a robust Bayesian workflow.([Section 4, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.06788v2#page=4 "Importantly, this calibration is limited exclusively to the computational aspect of our analysis."))
- **c5** Interpreting rank histograms: a symmetric ∩ shape indicates computed posteriors overdispersed relative to the true posterior on average, a ∪ shape indicates underdispersion, asymmetry indicates bias (in the opposite direction in the ranks), and spikes at the boundaries indicate non-negligible autocorrelation; these deviations can co-occur but are readily separated if large enough.([Section 4.2, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.06788v2#page=7 "Conversely, an algorithm that computes posteriors that are, on average, under-dispersed relative to the true posterior produces a histogram of rank statistics with a characteristic ∪ shape (Figure 6)."))
- **c6** Practical settings: histograms are shown with a gray band covering 99% of the variation expected under uniformity (0.005 to 0.995 quantiles of Binomial(N, 1/(L+1))); when the number of replications N is limited, neighboring ranks can be binned, and the authors' experiments suggest keeping N/B ≈ 20 (replications per bin, with B bins) as a good trade-off. They note it will be difficult to identify sufficiently small deviations regardless of binning.([Section 4.1, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.06788v2#page=6 "Our experiments have shown that keeping N/B ≈20 lead to a good trade-off between the expressiveness of the binned histogram and the necessary variance reduction."))
- **c7** Cost: SBC is expensive because N simulated observations must be fit before considering the measured data; the fits are embarrassingly parallel (all of the paper's examples ran on clusters and took at most a few hours), fewer posterior draws speed it up at the cost of sensitivity, and even a few simulations are useful to catch gross problems.([Section 4.3, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.06788v2#page=8 "The downside of using SBC in practice is that it is expensive; instead of ﬁtting a single observation we have to ﬁt N simulated observations before even considering the measured data."))
- **c8** For MCMC, posterior draws are thinned based on effective sample size (Algorithm 2), which in the authors' experience is sufficient to remove autocorrelation artifacts; deviations that cannot be mitigated by thinning provide strong evidence that the MCMC estimators do not follow a central limit theorem and the chains are not adequately exploring the target.([Section 5.1, p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.06788v2#page=10 "Deviations that cannot be mitigated by thinning provide strong evidence that the Markov chain Monte Carlo estimators do not follow a central limit theorem and the Markov chains are not adequately exploring the target parameter space."))
- **c9** Stated limitations: reliance on visual interpretation is a limitation with many parameters; global summaries such as a chi-squared goodness-of-fit test did not perform particularly well in their examples because deviations occur in a few systematic ways; and SBC histograms can only assess the calibration of one-dimensional posterior summaries.([Section 7, p.15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.06788v2#page=15 "Finally, the SBC histograms are only able to assess the calibration of one-dimensional pos-terior summaries."))
