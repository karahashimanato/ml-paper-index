## 主張

- **c1** Scope of BO: it is best suited to continuous domains of fewer than 20 dimensions and tolerates stochastic noise; the introduction adds that d <= 20 in most successful applications and that the feasible set is typically a hyper-rectangle or simplex.([Abstract, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.02811v1#page=1 "It is best-suited for optimization over continuous domains of less than 20 dimensions, and tolerates stochastic noise in function evaluations."))
- **c2** 'Expensive to evaluate' means the number of evaluations is limited, typically to a few hundred, because each evaluation takes substantial time (typically hours) or has a monetary or opportunity cost; the objective is also treated as a derivative-free black box.([Section 1 (Introduction), p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.02811v1#page=1 "f is “expensive to evaluate” in the sense that the number of evaluations that may be performed is limited, typically to a few hundred."))
- **c3** Noise assumption: in almost all BO work, noise is assumed independent across evaluations and Gaussian with constant variance (the tutorial itself assumes noise-free observations until Section 5).([Section 1 (Introduction), p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.02811v1#page=1 "In almost all work on Bayesian optimization, noise is assumed independent across evaluations and Gaussian with constant variance."))
- **c4** GP hyperparameters (kernel and mean parameters) are typically chosen by maximum likelihood, by MAP with a prior on the hyperparameters (useful if MLE sometimes gives unreasonable values), or by a fully Bayesian approach that marginalizes them, approximated by MCMC samples such as slice sampling; MAP can be seen as an approximation to fully Bayesian inference.([Section 3.2, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.02811v1#page=6 "To choose the hyperparameters, three approaches are typically considered."))
- **c5** Expected improvement is the most commonly used acquisition, performs well and is easy to use; knowledge gradient, entropy search and predictive entropy search are most useful in exotic problems where EI's assumption that the benefit of sampling comes through improvement at the sampled point no longer holds.([Section 4, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.02811v1#page=7 "These alternate acquisition functions are most useful in exotic problems where an assumption made by expected improvement, that the primary beneﬁt of sampling occurs through an improvement at the point sampled, is no longer true."))
- **c6** Knowledge gradient values improvement of the maximum of the posterior mean over the whole domain; it gives a small benefit in the standard noise-free problem and, citing works that include the author's own, tends to significantly outperform EI when such effects are first-order (noise, multi-fidelity, derivatives, environmental conditions).([Section 4.2, p.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.02811v1#page=11 "When such phenomenon are ﬁrst-order, KG tends to signiﬁcantly outperform EI (Wu et al., 2017; Poloczek et al., 2017; Wu and Frazier, 2016; Toscano-Palmerin and Frazier, 2018)."))
- **c7** Noisy evaluations: noise variance is usually unknown and treated as a common hyperparameter. Direct use of EI is conceptually challenging because improvement is not easily defined, so heuristic substitutes are used; citing works co-authored by the author, KG can outperform EI substantially under substantial noise.([Section 5, p.13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.02811v1#page=13 "Because of these approximations, KG can outperform EI substantially in problems with substantial noise (Wu and Frazier, 2016; Frazier et al., 2009)."))
- **c8** Multi-step lookahead: EI, KG, ES and PES are one-step optimal; exact multi-step optimal acquisition is extremely challenging (curse of dimensionality in dynamic programming), and approximate methods do not yet seem deployable broadly because their error and extra cost often overwhelm the benefit. In specific settings where multi-step optimal algorithms can be computed, existing one-step acquisitions perform almost as well.([Section 4.4, p.12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.02811v1#page=12 "These methods do not yet seem to be in a state where they can be deployed broadly for practical problems, because the error and extra cost introduced in solving stochastic dynamic programming problems approximately often overwhelms the beneﬁt that considering multiple steps provides."))
- **c9** Open problems: no finite-time bounds explain the near-optimal empirical performance of the acquisitions used in practice and little is known about convergence rates; GPs are used in most BO work but other models may suit some problems better; and BO that works well in high dimensions is of great interest, possibly via structure-exploiting models or new acquisition functions.([Section 7, p.16](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.02811v1#page=16 "It is also possible that new acquisition functions may provide substantial value in high dimensional problems."))
