## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** The ELBO alone cannot assess fit: an unknown multiplicative constant in p(theta, y) changes with reparametrization, making ELBO comparisons across approximations meaningless, and its scale is uninterpretable, so it is not clear at what value the approximation can be trusted.([Introduction, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.02538v2#page=1 "This makes it next to useless as a method to assess how well the variational inference has ﬁt."))
- **c2** PSIS diagnostic: k-hat estimates the smallest k such that the 1/k-th moment of p/q under q is finite; it is invariant to constant multiplication of p or q (so the unnormalized joint can be used) and relates to Rényi divergence: k > 0.5 means infinite chi-square divergence and k > 1 means KL(p, q) is infinite, even though KL(q, p) is minimized in the family.([Section 2.2, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.02538v2#page=3 "ˆk is invariant under any constant multiplication of p or q, which explains why we can suppress the marginal likelihood (normalizing constant) p(y) and replace the intractable p(θ/y) with p(θ, y) in (2)."))
- **c3** Thresholds (set according to the empirical study in Vehtari et al., 2017): k-hat < 0.5 means q is close enough to the true density; 0.5 < k-hat < 0.7 means q is not perfect but still useful (PSIS recommended to shrink errors in both cases); k-hat > 0.7 means PSIS convergence is impractically slow with an even larger error for plain VI, so the VI should be tuned (reparametrization, more iterations, larger minibatch, smaller learning rate, etc.) or exact MCMC used. In finite samples k-hat may exceed 1, indicating even worse performance. Algorithm 1 uses a single cut at 0.7.([Section 2.2, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.02538v2#page=3 "If ˆk > 0.7, the PSIS convergence rate becomes impractically slow, leading to a large mean square error, and a even larger error for plain VI estimate."))
- **c4** k-hat is invariant to any reparametrization of the same distribution, but a reparametrization that changes the approximation family changes the result, so comparing k-hat across parametrizations can guide the choice.([Section 2.3, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.02538v2#page=3 "A nice property of PSIS diagnostics is that the ˆk quantity is invariant under any re-parametrization."))
- **c5** Marginal PSIS diagnostics do not work: the true marginal density is typically unknown, and a small marginal k-hat does not guarantee good marginal estimates because VI marginals can under-estimate tail thickness; in the eight-school example marginal k-hats were misleadingly small while the joint k-hat was large.([Section 2.4, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.02538v2#page=4 "Secondly, a smaller ˆki does not necessary guarantee a well-performed marginal estimation."))
- **c6** VSBC (building on Cook et al., 2006) assesses average calibration over data sets simulated from the model, requiring a VI fit for each of M simulated data sets, and tests symmetry of marginal calibration probabilities (visually or with a KS test, interpreted through a multiple-testing lens). Drawbacks: average behavior need not hold for the observed data, and misspecification is not covered; the authors suggest a conservative reading in which failing implies poor performance but passing does not guarantee it.([Section 3.2, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.02538v2#page=5 "We suggest interpreting the diagnostic conservatively: if a variational inference scheme fails the diagnostic, then it will not perform well on the model in question."))
- **c7** Limitation: the PSIS diagnostic is local; with a multimodal posterior, draws from q may miss modes and k-hat ignores unseen modes (in a two-mode example Gaussian VI gives k = 0). Any divergence based on draws from q, such as KL(q, p), is local; bimodality can be detected by multiple over-dispersed initializations, whereas VSBC draws parameters from the prior and may detect a missing mode unless the prior is too restrictive.([Section 5.1, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.02538v2#page=8 "In this sense, the PSIS diagnostic is a local diagnostic that will not detect unseen modes."))
- **c8** Evaluation protocol: both diagnostics are applied to mean-field Gaussian ADVI on Bayesian linear regression (n = 10000, K = 100, VSBC with M = 1000 replications), logistic regression with design-matrix correlation from 0 to 0.99, the eight-school hierarchical model (centered vs non-centered), and a horseshoe logistic regression on leukemia microarray data (D = 7129, n = 72), with NUTS as the reference.([Section 4, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.02538v2#page=5 "Without loss of generality, we implement mean-ﬁeld Gaussian automatic differentiation variational inference (ADVI) in this section."))
- **c9** ADVI is sensitive to the stopping rule: in the linear-regression example, changing the relative ELBO-change threshold from a conservative 1e-5 to the default 1e-2 raised k-hat from 0.61 to 4.4, so k-hat can be used as a convergence check; simulations were repeated 100 times.([Section 4.1, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1802.02538v2#page=5 "However, the performance of ADVI is sensitive to the stopping time, as in any other optimization problems."))
