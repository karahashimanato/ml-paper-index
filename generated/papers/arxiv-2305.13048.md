## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Time decay: inspired by AFT, RWKV replaces pairwise position biases with w_{t,i} = -(t - i)w, a channel-wise vector w (d channels) multiplied by relative position, so the interaction can be turned into an RNN; w is required to be non-negative so that weights decay backwards in time.([Section 2.2, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.13048v2#page=3 "and the per-channel weights decay backwards in time."))
- **c2** WKV operator: wkv_t = (sum_{i<t} e^{-(t-1-i)w + k_i} v_i + e^{u + k_t} v_t) / (sum_{i<t} e^{-(t-1-i)w + k_i} + e^{u + k_t}); unlike AFT, W is a channel-wise vector modified by relative position rather than a pairwise matrix, and a vector U separately attends to the current token.([Section 3.1.2, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.13048v2#page=4 "To circumvent any potential degradation of W, we introduce a vector U that separately attends to the current token."))
- **c3** Training cost: in time-parallel mode the per-layer cost for a batch is O(BTd^2), dominated by the projection matrix multiplications, while updating wkv_t is a serial scan of complexity O(BTd) that is parallelized along the batch and channel dimensions (a custom CUDA kernel is used); the authors list a parallel scan reducing this to O(B log(T) d) as future work.([Section 3.2, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.13048v2#page=4 "In contrast, updating attention scores wkvt involves a serial scan (see Appendix D for more detail) and has complexity O(BTd)."))
- **c4** Model sizes and FLOP accounting: six models from 169M to 14B parameters, each trained for one epoch (330B tokens) on the Pile with context length 1024; FLOPs per token are computed analytically as 2(2VD + 13D^2 L) for the forward pass (twice the parameters in linear layers) and 6(2VD + 13D^2 L) including the backward pass, which the authors note matches the standard Transformer formula of Kaplan et al. (2020).([Section 4, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.13048v2#page=5 "FLOPs is for a forward pass for one token."))
- **c5** Scaling: the authors train 45 RWKV models for various (dataset, parameters) pairs and report that RWKV follows the same general form of scaling law as transformers, with a linear fit to the Pareto-optimal points of r^2 = 0.994 and r^2 = 0.875 when extrapolated an additional order of magnitude; they contrast this with prior claims (citing Kaplan et al., 2020) that LSTMs do not strictly follow log-log linear scaling.([Section 4.2, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.13048v2#page=6 "We train 45 RWKV models for a variety of pairs (dataset, parameters) and find that RWKV does follow the same general form of the scaling law that is well established for transformers."))
- **c6** Comparison budget with Transformers: RWKV is compared with Pythia, OPT and BLOOM trained for a similar but not identical number of tokens (RWKV: one epoch on the Pile, 330B tokens), so the comparison is made on a FLOP-matched basis; Chinchilla-optimal or overtrained models are deliberately excluded. Twelve zero-shot NLP benchmarks are reported (average in a figure); the abstract states RWKV performs on par with similarly sized Transformers.([Section 5.1, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.13048v2#page=6 "Consequently, we compare our models on a FLOP-matched basis."))
- **c7** Prompt sensitivity: comparisons with ChatGPT / GPT-4 (Appendix L) revealed that RWKV is very sensitive to prompt engineering; re-ordering prompts from those used for GPT to ones more suitable for RWKV increased F1 even from 44.2% to 74.8%.([Section 5.1, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.13048v2#page=6 "They revealed that RWKV is very sensitive to prompt engineering."))
- **c8** Inference benchmark conditions: text generation speed and memory are measured on CPU (x86) and GPU (NVIDIA A100 80 GB) using float32 precision and HuggingFace Transformers, counting all parameters including embeddings; quantization is left to future work. Results (cumulative generation time, linear for RWKV) are shown in figures only.([Section 6, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.13048v2#page=7 "For all of our inference experiments we use float32 precision and the HuggingFace Transformers (Wolf et al., 2020)."))
- **c9** Limitation: the linear attention may limit performance on tasks requiring recall of minute details over very long contexts, because information is funneled through a single vector representation over many time steps; the authors also state that prompt engineering is more important than for standard Transformers.([Section 9, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.13048v2#page=8 "First, the linear attention of RWKV leads to significant efficiency gains but still, it may also limit the model’s performance on tasks that require recalling minutiae information over very long contexts."))
