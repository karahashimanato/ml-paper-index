## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Data vs model uncertainty: predictive uncertainty is separated into data (aleatoric) and model (epistemic) uncertainty; model uncertainty covers four of the authors' five factors (real-world variability, model structure, training procedure, unknown data) and data uncertainty covers measurement error and noise (including label noise). Model uncertainty can (theoretically) be reduced, data uncertainty cannot.([Section II (Predictive Uncertainty Model), p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03342v3#page=5 "While model uncertainty can be (theoretically) reduced by improving the architecture, the learning process, or the training data set, the data uncertainties cannot be explained away [60]."))
- **c2** Taxonomy: uncertainty estimation methods are split into four types by the number and nature of the networks: single deterministic methods (one forward pass), Bayesian methods (stochastic networks), ensemble methods (several deterministic networks), and test-time augmentation (one network, augmented inputs).([Section III, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03342v3#page=7 "In general, the methods for estimating the uncertainty can be split in four different types based on the number (single or multiple) and the nature (deterministic or stochastic) of the used DNNs."))
- **c3** Single deterministic methods are computationally efficient (one network, one or at most two forward passes, often applicable to pretrained networks), but because they rely on a single opinion they can be very sensitive to architecture, training procedure and training data.([Section III (Summing Up Single Deterministic Methods), p.12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03342v3#page=12 "A drawback of single deterministic neural network approaches is the fact that they rely on a single opinion and can therefore become very sensitive to the underlying network architecture, training procedure, and the training data."))
- **c4** Ensemble cost and caveats: ensembles are easy to apply and parallelize, but memory and computation grow linearly with the number of members for training and testing, limiting deployment under compute/memory/time constraints; the main challenge is member diversity, and overconfidence of single members can transfer to the whole ensemble.([Section III (Sum Up Ensemble Methods), p.20](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03342v3#page=20 "The independence of the single ensemble members leads to a linear increase in the required memory and computation power with each additional member."))
- **c5** Ensembles vs Bayesian approaches: citing Gustafsson et al. [56], the survey reports that in their tests ensembles outperformed the considered Bayesian approaches, which it takes to indicate that multi-mode evaluation is a powerful property; it adds that Bayesian approaches have also delivered strong results with a strong theoretical foundation, and that prior-network-style deterministic methods often need separate in- and out-of-distribution sets for training.([Section III (Neural Network Uncertainty Quantification Approaches for Real Life Applications), p.21](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03342v3#page=21 "Interestingly, they found for their tests ensembles to outperform the considered Bayesian approaches."))
- **c6** Calibration methods are grouped as regularization during training, post-processing after training, and uncertainty estimation methods; post-processing methods require a held-out calibration set, only work if its distribution equals the inference distribution, and their result can depend on the size of that set.([Section V (Calibration Methods), p.24](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03342v3#page=24 "They only work under the assumption that the distribution of the left-out validation set is equivalent to the distribution, on which inference is done."))
- **c7** Recalibration does not reduce model uncertainty: the survey states that explicit calibration methods propagate model uncertainty onto the representation of data uncertainty (e.g. mapping an overfitted classifier's outputs to 0.5).([Section V (Calibration), p.24](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03342v3#page=24 "It is important to note that these methods do not reduce the model uncertainty, but propagate the model uncertainty onto the representation of the data uncertainty."))
- **c8** Calibration measures: ECE uses M equal-width bins and only the top-label confidence; SCE averages over all classes; adaptive (equal-mass) versions aECE and aSCE are, citing empirical results in [280] and [288], more robust to the number of bins. Classwise or not, calibration errors are weighted by class sizes, so larger classes can shadow poor calibration on small classes.([Section V (Evaluating Calibration Quality), p.28](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03342v3#page=28 "As has been empirically shown in [280] and [288], the adaptive binning calibration measures aECE and aSCE are more robust to the number of bins than the corresponding equal-width binning calibration measures ECE and SCE."))
- **c9** Open challenges named for real-world adoption: missing validation on real-world problems (baselines mostly developed on standard data sets such as CIFAR/ImageNet), lack of a standardized evaluation protocol, inability to evaluate the uncertainty of a single decision (measures such as ECE aggregate over the test set), lack of ground-truth uncertainties (and calibration not transferring across shifted data), and an explainability issue.([Section VIII (Conclusion), p.32](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.03342v3#page=32 "However, a clear standardized protocol of tests that should be performed on uncertainty quantiﬁcation methods is still not available."))
