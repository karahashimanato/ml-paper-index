## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Method: diffusion ODEs are semi-linear; the proposed exact formulation computes the linear part of the solution analytically instead of leaving all terms to black-box ODE solvers, and by change of variable reduces to an exponentially weighted integral of the neural network.([Abstract, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00927v3#page=1 "The formulation analytically computes the linear part of the solution, rather than leaving all terms to black-box ODE solvers as adopted in previous works."))
- **c2** Requirements: DPM-Solver applies to both discrete-time and continuous-time DPMs without any further training; for discrete-time models the noise-prediction network is fed non-integer (continuous) time inputs, which the authors find still works and hypothesize is due to smooth time embeddings.([Abstract, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00927v3#page=1 "DPM-Solver is suitable for both discrete-time and continuous-time DPMs without any further training."))
- **c3** Positioning: the authors divide fast samplers into training-based ones (knowledge distillation, noise-level or trajectory learning), which require a possibly expensive training stage and whose applicability and flexibility might be limited, and training-free ones, which (citing [21]) still require about 50 function evaluations for high-quality samples.([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00927v3#page=2 "Such methods require a possibly expensive training stage before they can be used for efﬁcient sampling."))
- **c4** Cost per step and theory: DPM-Solver-k needs k function evaluations per step (k = 1, 2, 3); higher orders are usually more efficient because they need far fewer steps. Theorem 3.2 states DPM-Solver-k is a k-th order solver under regularity conditions on the noise-prediction model (Appendix B.1); orders k >= 4 are not considered.([High-order solvers for diffusion ODEs, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00927v3#page=6 "As shown in the algorithm, DPM-Solver-k requires k function evaluations per step for k = 1, 2, 3."))
- **c5** DDIM's update is shown to be identical to DPM-Solver-1, which the authors say explains DDIM's advantage over the first-order Euler discretization (it uses the semi-linearity of diffusion ODEs).([Comparison with existing fast sampling methods, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00927v3#page=7 "Although motivated by entirely different perspectives, we show that the updates of DPM-Solver-1 and Denoising Diffusion Implicit Models (DDIM) [19] are identical."))
- **c6** Citing progressive distillation [13], the authors note it can give a sampler within 4 steps but needs further training and loses part of the original model's information (the distilled model cannot predict the noise at every time step), whereas training-free samplers keep the original model and can be combined with an external classifier for guidance.([Training-based fast sampling methods for DPMs, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00927v3#page=8 "Although the progressive distillation method [13] can obtain a fast sampler within 4 steps, it needs further training costs and loses part of the information in the original DPM"))
- **c7** Evaluation protocol: NFE is the number of calls to the noise-prediction model; FID is computed from 50K samples; existing pre-trained checkpoints are used (e.g. Ho et al. for CIFAR-10, Dhariwal & Nichol for ImageNet 128x128 with classifier guidance scale 1.25 and LSUN bedroom); a solver combination with uniform step size is used for NFE <= 20 and the adaptive DPM-Solver otherwise. Some baseline results are taken from the original papers (GGDM, and the improved-Euler SDE solver).([Experiments, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00927v3#page=8 "For each experiment, We draw 50K samples and use the widely adopted FID score [37] to evaluate the sample quality, where lower FID usually implies better sample quality."))
- **c8** With the continuous-time 'VP deep' CIFAR-10 model, the authors report FID 4.70 at 10 NFE, 3.75 at 12 NFE, 3.24 at 15 NFE and 2.87 at 20 NFE; they state other solvers have large discretization error even at 50 NFE.([Experiments: comparison with continuous-time sampling methods, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00927v3#page=9 "In particular, we achieve 4.70 FID with 10 NFE, 3.75 FID with 12 NFE, 3.24 FID with 15 NFE, and 2.87 FID with 20 NFE, which is the fastest sampler on CIFAR-10."))
- **c9** Limitations stated: DPM-Solver is designed for fast sampling and may not be suitable for accelerating likelihood evaluation; compared with GANs, diffusion models with DPM-Solver are still not fast enough for real-time applications.([Conclusions (Limitations and broader impact), p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.00927v3#page=10 "Besides, compared to the commonly-used GANs, diffusion models with DPM-Solver are still not fast enough for real-time applications."))
