- **c1** Motivation, citing Dhariwal & Nichol [15]: the most powerful pixel-space diffusion models take hundreds of GPU days to train (150-1000 V100 days in [15]) and inference is also expensive because of repeated network evaluations.([Introduction, p.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.10752v2#page=1 "As an example, training the most powerful DMs often takes hundreds of GPU days (e.g. 150 - 1000 V100 days in [15]) and repeated evaluations on a noisy version of the input space render also inference expensive,"))
- **c2** First-stage autoencoder: to avoid arbitrarily high-variance latents, two regularizations are studied, a slight KL penalty toward a standard normal (VAE-like) and a vector-quantization layer within the decoder; because the diffusion model works on the 2D latent, relatively mild compression suffices.([Section 3.1, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.10752v2#page=4 "The ﬁrst variant, KL-reg., imposes a slight KL-penalty towards a standard normal on the learned latent, similar to a VAE [46, 69], whereas VQ-reg. uses a vector quantization layer [96] within the decoder."))
- **c3** Regularization vs sample quality: LDMs trained in VQ-regularized latent spaces sometimes achieve better sample quality even though VQ-regularized first stages reconstruct slightly worse than their continuous (KL) counterparts.([Section 4, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.10752v2#page=5 "Interestingly, we ﬁnd that LDMs trained in VQ-regularized latent spaces sometimes achieve better sample quality, even though the reconstruction capabilities of VQ-regularized ﬁrst stage models slightly fall behind those of their continuous counterparts, cf. Tab. 8."))
- **c4** Compression trade-off (downsampling factor f in {1,...,32}, single A100, same steps and parameter count, class-conditional ImageNet for 2M steps): small f (1, 2) trains slowly and large f stagnates in fidelity; the authors attribute this to leaving most perceptual compression to the diffusion model versus information loss from too strong first-stage compression, and conclude LDM-4 and -8 offer the best conditions.([Section 4.1, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.10752v2#page=5 "we attribute this to i) leaving most of perceptual compression to the diffusion model and ii) too strong ﬁrst stage compression resulting in information loss and thus limiting the achievable quality."))
- **c5** Protocol of the efficiency analysis: the sample-quality curves in Figures 6, 7 and 17 are computed on 5k samples for efficiency, so they may differ from the 50k-sample results in Tables 1 and 10; learning rates were maximized per model subject to stable training and therefore vary slightly between runs.([Appendix E.3.5, p.27](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.10752v2#page=27 "For efﬁciency reasons we compute the sample quality metrics plotted in Fig. 6, 17 and 7 based on 5k samples."))
- **c6** FID protocol for unconditional and class-conditional results: statistics from 50k model samples and the entire training set, computed with torch-fidelity and cross-checked with Dhariwal and Nichol's script; scores mainly coincided with slight differences on ImageNet and LSUN-Bedrooms, and the authors emphasize the importance of a unified procedure for sample quality assessment. Text-to-image FID/IS use 30000 MS-COCO validation samples, and some Table 2 baseline numbers are taken from other papers.([Appendix E.3.1, p.26](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.10752v2#page=26 "We follow common practice and estimate the statistics for calculating the FID-, Precision- and Recall-scores [29,50] shown in Tab. 1 and 10 based on 50k samples from our models and the entire training set of each of the shown datasets."))
- **c7** Guidance in latent space: classifier-free guidance (cited as [32]) greatly boosts text-to-image sample quality on MS-COCO; reported guided settings use scales 1.5 (text-to-image, ImageNet LDM-4-G) and 1.25 (ImageNet, Table 10). No description of how the guidance scale was chosen was found in the text (searched for sweep, select, choose, tune).([Section 4.3.1, p.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.10752v2#page=7 "We note that applying classiﬁer-free diffusion guidance [32] greatly boosts sample quality, such that the guided LDM-KL-8-G is on par with the recent state-of-the-art AR [26] and diffusion models [59] for text-to-image synthesis, while substantially reducing parameter count."))
- **c8** Compute comparison: competing methods' compute (V100-days) is taken from [15] unless stated otherwise, and the authors' single-A100 training days are converted to V100-days by assuming a 2.2x A100-over-V100 speedup.([Appendix F (Computational Requirements), p.28](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.10752v2#page=28 "we convert the A100 days to V100 days by assuming a ×2.2 speedup of A100 vs V100"))
- **c9** Stated limitations: sequential sampling is still slower than GANs, and the autoencoder's reconstruction capability can become a bottleneck for tasks needing fine-grained pixel accuracy even though the f = 4 quality loss is very small; the authors assume their super-resolution models are already somewhat limited in this respect.([Section 5, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.10752v2#page=9 "Moreover, the use of LDMs can be questionable when high precision is required: although the loss of image quality is very small in our f = 4 autoencoding models (see Fig. 1), their reconstruction capability can become a bottleneck for tasks that require ﬁne-grained accuracy in pixel space."))
