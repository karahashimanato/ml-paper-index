## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Discrete and convolutional views: the SSM is discretized with the bilinear method using a step size Delta (A-bar = (I - Delta/2 A)^-1 (I + Delta/2 A), B-bar = (I - Delta/2 A)^-1 Delta B), giving an RNN-like recurrence; because the recurrence is sequential, training instead uses the equivalent convolution y = K * u with K = (CB, CAB, ..., CA^(L-1)B), computable with FFTs once K is known, and computing K is the focus of the technical contributions.([Section 2.4, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.00396v3#page=4 "The recurrent SSM (3) is not practical for training on modern hardware due to its sequentiality."))
- **c2** Bottleneck of prior work: computing the kernel naively as in the LSSL needs O(N^2 L) operations and O(NL) space; diagonalizing A would reduce it to a Vandermonde product, but the HiPPO matrix is diagonalized by a matrix with entries up to about 2^(4N/3), so naive diagonalization is numerically infeasible, and the authors prove that a faster algorithm proposed in the LSSL paper is also numerically unstable.([Section 3.1, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.00396v3#page=5 "Unfortunately, the naive application of diagonalization does not work due to numerical issues."))
- **c3** NPLR parameterization: the HiPPO matrix is not normal but is the sum of a normal and a low-rank matrix (rank 1 for equation (2); r = 1 or 2 for HiPPO-LegS, LegT, LagT), and S4 computes the kernel's spectrum through the truncated generating function at roots of unity, corrects the low-rank term with the Woodbury identity and reduces the diagonal case to a Cauchy kernel; Theorem 3 states O~(N + L) operations and O(N + L) space for the kernel, and Theorem 2 O(N) per recurrent step.([Section 3.2, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.00396v3#page=5 "We make the observation that although the HiPPO matrix is not normal, it can be decomposed as the sum of a normal and low-rank matrix."))
- **c4** Implementation caveat: the actual implementation uses the naive O(NL) Cauchy algorithm via the pykeops library (which the authors say may not be optimized for these kernels), because the asymptotically faster stable Cauchy algorithms are not implemented for GPUs; the authors believe S4 may have significant room for efficiency improvements. Separately, the paper notes that follow-up work found this version of S4 can sometimes be numerically unstable when A has eigenvalues in the right half-plane, corrected by changing Lambda - PQ* to Lambda - PP*.([Appendix A, p.16](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.00396v3#page=16 "Our current implementation of S4 actually uses the naive O(NL) algorithm which is easily parallelized on GPUs"))
- **c5** Efficiency as measured: versus the LSSL, S4 is stated to be up to 30x faster with 400x less memory; versus efficient Transformers (Linear Transformer, Performer) the comparison is parameter-matched and follows the LRA protocol of Tay et al., with S4's speed and memory described as competitive with the most efficient variants.([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.00396v3#page=2 "Compared to the LSSL, S4 is up to 30× faster with 400× less memory usage, while exceeding the LSSL’s performance empirically."))
- **c6** Long Range Arena as stated in the text: S4 outperforms all baselines (the 11 Transformer variants from Tay et al. plus follow-up work) on all tasks, averaging 80.48% versus less than 60% for every baseline, and solves Path-X (length 16384), on which all previous models were at random guessing.([Section 4.2, p.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.00396v3#page=9 "S4 substantially advances the SoTA, outperforming all baselines on all tasks and averaging 80.48% compared to less than 60% for every baseline."))
- **c7** Reproduction caveat: since the first version, several experiments were updated; the LRA results in Table 4 were replaced by results from follow-up papers (S4-LegS, same model) with minor hyperparameter changes, the original numbers are in Table 10, Speech Commands users are asked to cite a different table from a follow-up paper (full 35-class SC), and the WikiText-103 model was retrained with batch size 1 and context 8192.([Appendix D.5, p.30](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.00396v3#page=30 "Since the ﬁrst version of this paper, several experiments have been updated."))
- **c8** Ablation (sequential CIFAR-10, at most 100K parameters, plateau scheduler, no regularization): random NPLR matrices still do not perform well, which the authors take as showing that S4's effectiveness comes primarily from the HiPPO initialization rather than the NPLR parameterization; with trainable A all initializations reach perfect training accuracy but validation accuracies differ by over 15%.([Section 4.4, p.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.00396v3#page=11 "Fig. 4a shows that random NPLR matrices still do not perform well, which validates that S4’s eﬀectiveness primarily comes from the HiPPO initialization, not the NPLR parameterization."))
- **c9** Stated limitation: S4 may not be the most suitable model for all data types; language modeling (WikiText-103, where S4 replaced the attention layers of a Transformer baseline and is stated to be within 0.8 perplexity) still shows a gap to Transformers, and combining S4 with other sequence models is suggested as future work.([Appendix A (Limitations and Future Directions), p.16](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.00396v3#page=16 "For example, Table 8 still found a gap compared to Transformers for language modeling."))
