## 主張

- **c1** Setting and assumption: positive pairs are typically two independent random augmentations of the same sample; the analysis assumes the positive-pair distribution is symmetric and that its marginal matches the data distribution, and studies one specific form of contrastive loss (normalized features, temperature tau, M negatives drawn from the data distribution).([Section 3, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.10242v10#page=3 "Based on empirical practices, we assume the following property."))
- **c2** Instead of reading the contrastive loss as a lower bound on mutual information, the authors analyse the loss itself, because (citing Tschannen et al., 2019) the bound interpretation is known to have issues in practice, e.g. maximizing a tighter bound often gives worse downstream performance.([Section 3, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.10242v10#page=3 "However, this interpretation is known to have issues in practice, e.g., maximizing a tighter bound often leads to worse downstream task performance (Tschannen et al., 2019)."))
- **c3** Theorem 1 (limit of infinitely many negatives, fixed tau): the first term of the limiting loss is minimized iff the encoder is perfectly aligned, perfectly uniform encoders, if they exist, are the exact minimizers of the second term, and the deviation from the limit decays as O(M^-1/2).([Section 4.2 (Theorem 1), p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.10242v10#page=6 "If perfectly uniform encoders exist, they form the exact minimizers of the second term."))
- **c4** Perfect uniformity is not always realizable (e.g. when the data manifold is lower-dimensional than the feature space), and when the pair distributions come from augmenting a finite dataset no encoder can be both perfectly aligned and perfectly uniform; perfectly uniform encoders do exist when n >= m - 1 and the data distribution has bounded density.([Section 4.2 (Realizability of perfect uniformity), p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.10242v10#page=6 "Moreover, in the case that pdata and ppos are formed from sampling augmented samples from a ﬁnite dataset, there cannot be an encoder that is both perfectly aligned and perfectly uniform, because perfect alignment implies that all augmentations from a single element have the same feature vector."))
- **c5** On the latent-class analysis (citing Saunshi et al., 2019): the authors say it gives nice theoretical insights but has a rather large gap with practice, because its result that representation quality suffers with a large number of negatives is inconsistent with empirical observations.([Section 2 (Related Work), p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.10242v10#page=2 "Analysis based on the assumption of latent classes provides nice theoretical insights (Saunshi et al., 2019), but unfortunately has a rather large gap with empirical practices"))
- **c6** Evaluation protocol: 304 STL-10, 64 NYU-Depth-V2, 45 ImageNet-100 (MoCo-based) and 108 BookCorpus (Quick-Thought-based) encoders are trained with weighted combinations of the three losses; for the tables, the 'best' encoder is selected by cross-validation on the training set (5-fold for STL-10) and then reported on the validation set.([Table 1, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.10242v10#page=8 "The best result is picked by encoder outputs linear classiﬁer accuracy from a 5-fold training set cross validation, among all 150 encoders trained from scratch with 128-dimensional output and 768 batch size."))
- **c7** Encoders trained only with L_align and L_uniform consistently outperformed their contrastive-loss counterparts in the STL-10 and NYU-Depth-V2 tables; the authors suggest that directly optimizing the two properties can be superior in practice when only finite negatives are available.([Section 5, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.10242v10#page=8 "This empirical performance gap suggests that directly optimizing these properties can be superior in practice, when we can only have ﬁnite negatives."))
- **c8** Both properties are needed: weighting alignment much higher than uniformity produces a degenerate (collapsed) solution mapping all inputs to the same feature; as long as the weight ratio is not too large (e.g. < 4), representation quality stays relatively good and insensitive to the exact weights.([Section 5, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.10242v10#page=8 "When Lalign is weighted much higher than Luniform, degenerate solution occurs and all inputs are mapped to the same feature vector (exp Luniform = 1)."))
