## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Critique of existing benchmarks: most frequently used graph datasets are extremely small compared with real applications (e.g. Cora, CiteSeer, PubMed have 2,700 to 20,000 nodes; TU graph classification datasets 200 to 5,000 graphs), and GNN performance on them is often unstable and nearly statistically identical (citing Dwivedi et al., 2020 and Hu et al., 2020a).([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.00687v7#page=2 "Most of the frequently-used graph datasets are extremely small compared to graphs found in real applications (with more than 1 million nodes or 100 thousand graphs)"))
- **c2** There is no unified experimental protocol (studies adopt their own splits, metrics and cross-validation), and the convention of random train/test splits is, according to the authors, not realistic for real-world applications and generally leads to overly optimistic performance results (citing Lohr, 2009).([Introduction, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.00687v7#page=3 "Furthermore, there is no uniﬁed and commonly-followed experimental protocol."))
- **c3** Citing Zou et al. (2020), the authors report data quality issues in standard node classification datasets: in Cora 42% of nodes leak information between features and labels and 1% are duplicated; CiteSeer has leakage rates of 62% and duplication rates of 5%. Existing larger datasets (PPI, Reddit, Amazon2M) use 83%, 65% and 90% of nodes for training, giving an artificially small distribution shift.([Section 2, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.00687v7#page=5 "For example, in CORA, 42% of the nodes leak information between their features and labels, and 1% of the nodes are duplicated."))
- **c4** Scale definition: even the 'small' OGB graphs have more than 100 thousand nodes or more than 1 million edges (but fit in a single GPU), 'medium' means more than 1 million nodes or 10 million edges, and 'large' is on the order of 100 million nodes or 1 billion edges; ogbn-papers100M is a citation graph of 111 million papers.([Introduction, p.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.00687v7#page=3 "Even the “small” OGB graphs have more than 100 thousand nodes or more than 1 million edges, but are small enough to ﬁt into the memory of a single GPU, making them suitable for testing computationally intensive algorithms."))
- **c5** Realistic splits: e.g. ogbn-products is split by sales ranking (top 8% train, next 2% validation, rest test) instead of random 90/10, ogbn-arxiv trains on papers until 2017, validates on 2018 and tests on papers since 2019, and molecular datasets use the most challenging version of the scaffold split, in which test molecules are maximally diverse.([Section 4.3, p.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.00687v7#page=11 "Speciﬁcally, we propose to train on papers published until 2017, validate on those published in 2018, and test on those published since 2019."))
- **c6** Baseline protocol: each experiment is repeated 10 times with different random seeds, reporting mean and unbiased standard deviation of training and test results corresponding to the best validation results; node-level models use a fixed hidden dimensionality of 256, two or three layers and a tuned dropout in {0.0, 0.5}.([Section 3, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.00687v7#page=6 "We repeat each experiment 10 times using different random seeds, and report the mean and unbiased standard deviation of all training and test results corresponding to the best validation results."))
- **c7** Random vs realistic splits: with the same split ratio, random splits are consistently easier. Examples stated in the body text: GraphSAGE reaches 88.20±0.08% test accuracy on a random 8/2/90 split of ogbn-products with about 1 percentage point generalization gap; on random splits of ogbg-molhiv and ogbg-molpcba the best GIN achieves 82.73±2.02% ROC-AUC (5.66 points higher than scaffold) and 34.40±0.90% AP (7.37 points higher).([Section 6.1, p.22](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.00687v7#page=22 "We ﬁnd the random split to be much easier than scaffold split."))
- **c8** On ogbn-proteins, simple MLPs (whose input features are averaged incoming edge features) perform better than node2vec and GCN, and only GraphSAGE outperforms the MLP, which the authors read as indicating that central node information (not explicitly modeled in GCN's message passing) already contains much crucial information.([Section 4.2, p.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.00687v7#page=10 "Surprisingly, simple MLPs10 perform better than more sophisticated approaches like NODE2VEC and GCN."))
- **c9** Standardized evaluation: the ogb.*.Evaluator class takes dataset-specific inputs (e.g. y_true and y_pred matrices for ogbg-molpcba) and returns the metric appropriate for the dataset (e.g. Average Precision for ogbg-molpcba); AP averaged over tasks is used because only 1.4% of molpcba data is positive.([Section 7.2, p.25](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.00687v7#page=25 "OGB also enables standardized and reliable evaluation with the ogb.*.Evaluator class."))
