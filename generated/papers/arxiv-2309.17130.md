## 主張

出典のリンクは原論文PDFの該当ページを開く。リンクにカーソルを合わせると原文の引用が表示される。

- **c1** Method: GRANDE extends the authors' earlier GradTree (Marton et al., 2023) from single trees to a jointly optimized weighted tree ensemble with hard, axis-aligned splits, using a dense representation and the straight-through operator; the authors contrast this with NODE's soft, oblique splits.([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.17130v3#page=2 "We extend GradTree (Marton et al., 2023) from individual trees to an end-to-end gradient-based tree ensemble, maintaining efficient computation (Section 3.1)."))
- **c2** Motivation (citing Rahaman et al., 2019 and Grinsztajn et al., 2022): DL methods are typically biased towards smooth solutions while target functions in tabular data are usually not smooth; hard, axis-aligned trees learn piece-wise constant functions and do not show this bias.([Introduction, p.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.17130v3#page=2 "As the target function in tabular datasets is usually not smooth, DL methods struggle to find these irregular functions."))
- **c3** Softsign split function: sigmoid gradients decline smoothly and entmoid gradients drop to zero for large differences from the threshold, so a softsign scaled to (0, 1) is proposed to keep responsive gradients for samples far from the threshold; an ablation (Table 4) reports it outperforms sigmoid and entmoid.([Section 3.2, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.17130v3#page=4 "Therefore, we propose using a softsign function, scaled to (0, 1), as a differentiable split function:"))
- **c4** Instance-wise weighting: instead of one weight per estimator (which the authors say forces a homogeneous ensemble), each leaf has a learnable weight, and a softmax over the weights of the leaves a sample reaches gives a per-sample weighted average; the ablation reports this significantly enhances performance compared with a single weight per estimator.([Section 3.3, p.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.17130v3#page=4 "To address this, we propose an advanced weighting scheme that allows calculating instance-wise weights that can be learned within the gradient-based optimization."))
- **c5** Benchmark: 19 binary classification datasets selected by the predefined procedure of Bischl et al. (2021) from OpenML benchmark suites, which the authors argue is not biased towards their method (CC18 and the Grinsztajn et al. benchmark were rejected; multi-class tasks were excluded for scope); one-hot/leave-one-out encoding and quantile transformation; mean and std of test macro F1 over 5-fold cross-validation.([Section 4.1, p.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.17130v3#page=5 "The selection process was adopted from Bischl et al. (2021) and therefore is not biased towards our method."))
- **c6** Tuning protocol: baselines in the main comparison are XGBoost, CatBoost and NODE (SAINT, RandomForest and ExtraTrees in the appendix); all are tuned with Optuna for 250 trials with search spaces and default parameters following Borisov et al. (2022), best parameters chosen by 5x2 cross-validation with each fold's test data held out, and class weights for imbalance. Default-hyperparameter results are also reported.([Section 4.1, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.17130v3#page=6 "We optimized the hyperparameters using Optuna (Akiba et al., 2019) with 250 trials and selected the search space as well as the default parameters for related work in accordance with Borisov et al. (2022)."))
- **c7** Asymmetry in tuned hyperparameters acknowledged: GRANDE has 11 tuned hyperparameters versus 4 for XGBoost and 3 for CatBoost; in an additional HPO with only 4 GRANDE parameters the authors report GRANDE still achieves SOTA results but benefits from the larger grid. The number of estimators for XGBoost and CatBoost was not tuned (early stopping), checked in an extra HPO; a 30-trial HPO is also reported.([Appendix E, p.22](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.17130v3#page=22 "In contrast, for XGBoost and CatBoost we only optimized 4 and 3 hyperparameters, respectively."))
- **c8** Relation to GBDTs within the paper (tuned): GRANDE has the highest mean reciprocal rank (0.702) and normalized mean (0.776), followed by CatBoost (0.570, 0.671), XGBoost (0.417, 0.483) and NODE (0.395, 0.327); the authors add that this is in line with there being no universal method for tabular data, that differences are substantial on some datasets, and that results are especially good for small datasets.([Section 4.2, p.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.17130v3#page=6 "GRANDE outperformed existing methods and achieved the highest mean reciprocal rank (MRR) of 0.702 and the highest normalized mean of 0.776."))
- **c9** Interpretability claim and its evidence: instance-wise weights let one inspect the highest-weighted estimators for a given instance; the evidence is a case study of one PhishingWebsites instance where a single depth-two tree carries 94% of the weight and its rule matches the Anchors explanation, with GRANDE's anchor simpler than those of the other methods at equal precision.([Section 4.3, p.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.17130v3#page=8 "In contrast, existing ensemble methods require a global interpretation of the model and do not provide simple, local explanations out-of-the-box."))
